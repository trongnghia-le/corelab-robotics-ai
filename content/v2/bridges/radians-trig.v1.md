# Radians, trigonometry and coordinates

This optional bridge takes about 15 minutes. You can skip it and open any activity. Reading it creates no evidence.

A full turn is 2π radians = 360 degrees. Multiply degrees by π/180 to obtain radians, and radians by 180/π to obtain degrees. Always write the unit: 90 degrees and 90 radians are different angles.

For a counter-clockwise angle θ measured from the positive x-axis, a point at distance l has coordinates (l cos θ, l sin θ). Cosine is the horizontal component; sine is the vertical component. Lengths are in metres in the Frames/FK activities. The signs change with the quadrant.

| θ (rad) | 0 | π/6 | π/4 | π/2 | π |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cos θ | 1 | √3/2 | √2/2 | 0 | −1 |
| sin θ | 0 | 1/2 | √2/2 | 1 | 0 |

For example, θ = π/2 rad and l = 2 m gives (0, 2) m. At θ = −π/2 rad it gives (0, −2) m. Predict a point before checking with a calculator. Check that x²+y² = l².

A two-link arm uses relative joint angles: the second link points along q1+q2. Derive its endpoint by adding the two link vectors, rather than treating q2 as an absolute angle.
