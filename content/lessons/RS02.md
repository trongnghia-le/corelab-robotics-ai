## Why this matters

A team adds a new loss function and doubles the training data in the same week; the model improves by nine points. Which change deserves the credit? Without a designed comparison the honest answer is "we do not know", and the next three months of work may go into the wrong idea. Experimental design is the craft of arranging runs so the answer to "what caused the change" is contained in the data.

Robotics and machine learning experiments add three difficulties: runs are costly (so few replicates), results are noisy (seed, initial state, wear), and many things change at once (code, data, hardware). This lesson gives the vocabulary and the 2x2 factorial ablation, the simplest design that separates two improvements and reveals whether they interact.

## Intuition

Every experiment has an **independent variable** (what you set: augmentation on/off), a **dependent variable** (what you measure: accuracy), and **controlled variables** (what you hold fixed: architecture, training steps, evaluation set). A **confounder** is any other variable that changes along with the independent variable and could explain the result, such as "the run with more data also used the newer GPU driver."

![A 2x2 grid of conditions (A off/on by B off/on) with cell means 70, 73, 74, 79, arrows for the main effects and the interaction, and a bar showing the confounded design where A and B always change together.](/lesson-figures/RS02.svg)

*Cell means of the worked example. The factorial layout isolates A, B and their interaction; the confounded design (A and B together) has only two conditions and cannot.*

Two protections do most of the work:

1. **Vary one thing at a time, or better, vary all factors independently** (factorial design). Every factor then appears at each level of the others, so effects are separable.
2. **Randomise** everything you cannot control (run order, seeds, which machine) so that leftover differences are unrelated to the factor.

## Theory and derivation

### The 2x2 factorial

Two binary factors $A,B$ (0 = off, 1 = on) give four conditions. With cell means $\bar y_{ab}$ the **main effects** are the average change when a factor is switched on, averaged over the other factor:
$$E_A=\tfrac12\big[(\bar y_{10}-\bar y_{00})+(\bar y_{11}-\bar y_{01})\big],\qquad E_B=\tfrac12\big[(\bar y_{01}-\bar y_{00})+(\bar y_{11}-\bar y_{10})\big].$$
The **interaction** measures how much the effect of $A$ depends on $B$:
$$E_{AB}=\tfrac12\big[(\bar y_{11}-\bar y_{01})-(\bar y_{10}-\bar y_{00})\big]=\tfrac12\big[\bar y_{11}+\bar y_{00}-\bar y_{10}-\bar y_{01}\big].$$
If $E_{AB}=0$ the effects are additive and the "both" cell is predicted as $\bar y_{00}+E_A+E_B$ relative to baseline. A positive interaction means synergy (the improvements help more together); a negative one means redundancy.

### Confounding

If the design has only two conditions, (0,0) and (1,1), the columns of $A$ and $B$ are identical: their correlation is 1 and no analysis can separate them. A design is **non-confounded** for two factors when their columns are uncorrelated; in the balanced full factorial the correlation is exactly 0.

### Noise, replicates and standard error

Suppose runs in a cell have variance $\sigma^2$ and each cell has $r$ replicates. Then $\mathrm{Var}(\bar y_{ab})=\sigma^2/r$, and each effect above is a combination of four cell means with coefficients $\pm\tfrac12$, so
$$\mathrm{Var}(E)=4\cdot\frac14\cdot\frac{\sigma^2}{r}=\frac{\sigma^2}{r},\qquad \mathrm{SE}(E)=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt r}.$$
The same SE applies to $E_A$, $E_B$ and $E_{AB}$. Estimate $\sigma$ by the **pooled within-cell standard deviation**, $s^2=\sum(y-\bar y_{\text{cell}})^2/(N-4)$. A 95% interval is $E\pm t_{N-4,\,0.975}\,s/\sqrt r$.

To detect an effect of size $\delta$ with about 80% power at the 5% level (two-sided) you need
$$r\approx\frac{(1.96+0.84)^2\sigma^2}{\delta^2}=\frac{7.84\,\sigma^2}{\delta^2}.$$
Halving $\delta$ quadruples $r$.

### Ablations

An **ablation** removes a component from the full system to see what it contributed. Removing $A$ from the full system measures $\bar y_{11}-\bar y_{01}$, while adding $A$ to the baseline measures $\bar y_{10}-\bar y_{00}$. They agree only if $E_{AB}=0$. Report which version you ran, and in the presence of interaction run the full factorial.

### Randomisation, blocking and seeds

Random seeds are your replicates, so each cell needs several different seeds, and the same set of seeds across cells lets you **block** on seed (compare paired runs) when seeds have large effects. Randomise run order to spread drifting conditions (thermal throttling, a changing dataset on disk) across cells instead of letting them line up with a factor.

## Worked example

A vision model's accuracy (%) under augmentation (A) and more data (B), five seeded runs per cell:

| cell | runs | mean |
|---|---|---|
| baseline (A=0,B=0) | 68, 69, 70, 71, 72 | 70.0 |
| A only | 71, 72, 73, 74, 75 | 73.0 |
| B only | 72, 73, 74, 75, 76 | 74.0 |
| both | 77, 78, 79, 80, 81 | 79.0 |

**Effects.**
- $E_A=\tfrac12[(73-70)+(79-74)]=\tfrac12(3+5)=4.0$.
- $E_B=\tfrac12[(74-70)+(79-73)]=\tfrac12(4+6)=5.0$.
- $E_{AB}=\tfrac12[79+70-73-74]=\tfrac12(2)=1.0$.

**Naive reading.** "Both vs baseline = +9" attributes all nine points to the bundle. The factorial shows A contributes 4 on average, B contributes 5, and they add up to 9 with an interaction of 1 (the combination adds 2 points beyond additivity: $70+3+4=77$ predicted, 79 observed; the interaction effect is half of that cell discrepancy).

**Noise.** Within each cell the SD is $\sqrt{2.5}=1.581$ (values spread $\pm1,\pm2$ around the mean), so the pooled $s=1.581$ with $N-4=16$ degrees of freedom. $\mathrm{SE}=1.581/\sqrt5=0.707$. With $t_{16,0.975}=2.120$ the half-width is $1.50$:

| effect | estimate | 95% interval |
|---|---|---|
| A | 4.0 | [2.5, 5.5] |
| B | 5.0 | [3.5, 6.5] |
| AB | 1.0 | [-0.5, 2.5] |

A and B clearly help; the interaction is not distinguishable from zero.

**Replicates.** To detect $\delta=2$ with $\sigma=1.581$: $r=7.84(2.5)/4=4.9\to5$ replicates per cell, which is what we used.

**Confounded alternative.** If the team had run only baseline and "both" with five seeds each, it would see +9 with a similar interval but have no way to attribute it. Four cells cost 20 runs instead of 10, which is the price of attribution.

## Reference implementation

```python
import math
import random

def cell_means(runs):
    acc = {}
    for a, b, y in runs:
        acc.setdefault((a, b), []).append(y)
    return {k: sum(v) / len(v) for k, v in acc.items()}

def effects(m):
    A = ((m[(1, 0)] + m[(1, 1)]) - (m[(0, 0)] + m[(0, 1)])) / 2
    B = ((m[(0, 1)] + m[(1, 1)]) - (m[(0, 0)] + m[(1, 0)])) / 2
    AB = (m[(1, 1)] + m[(0, 0)] - m[(1, 0)] - m[(0, 1)]) / 2
    return A, B, AB

def pooled_sd(runs):
    m = cell_means(runs)
    ss = sum((y - m[(a, b)]) ** 2 for a, b, y in runs)
    return math.sqrt(ss / (len(runs) - len(m)))

def factor_correlation(design):
    n = len(design); xa = [d[0] for d in design]; xb = [d[1] for d in design]
    ma, mb = sum(xa) / n, sum(xb) / n
    sa = math.sqrt(sum((x - ma) ** 2 for x in xa)); sb = math.sqrt(sum((x - mb) ** 2 for x in xb))
    return 0.0 if sa == 0 or sb == 0 else sum((x - ma) * (y - mb) for x, y in zip(xa, xb)) / (sa * sb)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    runs = ([(0, 0, y) for y in [68, 69, 70, 71, 72]] + [(1, 0, y) for y in [71, 72, 73, 74, 75]] +
            [(0, 1, y) for y in [72, 73, 74, 75, 76]] + [(1, 1, y) for y in [77, 78, 79, 80, 81]])
    m = cell_means(runs); print(m); print(effects(m))
    s = pooled_sd(runs); print(round(s, 3), round(s / math.sqrt(5), 3))
    d = [(a, b) for a in (0, 1) for b in (0, 1)] * 5; random.Random(1).shuffle(d)
    print(round(factor_correlation(d), 6), round(factor_correlation([(0, 0), (1, 1)] * 5), 6))
    # --- self-test ---
    assert effects(m) == (4.0, 5.0, 1.0)
    assert abs(s - math.sqrt(2.5)) < 1e-12
    assert abs(factor_correlation(d)) < 1e-12 and abs(factor_correlation([(0, 0), (1, 1)] * 5) - 1) < 1e-9
    print("self-test passed")
```

### Starter code

```python
import math
import random
from typing import Dict, List, Tuple

Run = Tuple[int, int, float]       # (a, b, y), a and b in {0, 1}

def cell_means(runs: List[Run]) -> Dict[Tuple[int, int], float]:
    """Mean of y for each (a, b) cell present in runs."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def effects(m: Dict[Tuple[int, int], float]) -> Tuple[float, float, float]:
    """(E_A, E_B, E_AB): E_A = average over B of (A=1 minus A=0), E_B likewise,
    E_AB = (m11 + m00 - m10 - m01) / 2."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def pooled_sd(runs: List[Run]) -> float:
    """Within-cell standard deviation pooled over cells: sqrt(SS_within / (N - cells))."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def effect_se(sigma: float, r: int) -> float:
    """Standard error of a 2x2 factorial effect with r replicates per cell."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def replicates_needed(sigma: float, delta: float) -> int:
    """ceil(7.84 * sigma**2 / delta**2): replicates per cell for ~80% power at 5%."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def factor_correlation(design: List[Tuple[int, int]]) -> float:
    """Pearson correlation between the A and B columns (0.0 if either is constant)."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def randomized_design(r: int, seed: int) -> List[Tuple[int, int]]:
    """Each of the four (a, b) cells r times, in a random order
    (build the list a-major then b, then random.Random(seed).shuffle)."""
    raise NotImplementedError
```

### Extension challenge

Simulate 5000 experiments from the model $y=70+3A+4B+2AB+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon\sim N(0,1.58^2)$, $r=3$ versus $r=5$ replicates per cell, and estimate how often the 95% interval for $E_{AB}$ excludes zero (power). Compare with the formula $r=7.84\sigma^2/\delta^2$.

### Library track

Redo the analysis with NumPy and SciPy. With $\pm1$ coding of the factors, the effects of a 2x2 factorial are twice the least-squares coefficients of $y=\beta_0+\beta_A s_A+\beta_B s_B+\beta_{AB}s_As_B$, and SciPy's `stats.t.ppf` gives the critical value. The script also runs the power simulation of the extension challenge in vectorised form (note that the model $y=70+3A+4B+2AB$ has $E_{AB}=1$, since the effect is half of the cell contrast, so power is low at $\sigma=1.58$).

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

runs = ([(0, 0, y) for y in [68, 69, 70, 71, 72]] + [(1, 0, y) for y in [71, 72, 73, 74, 75]] +
        [(0, 1, y) for y in [72, 73, 74, 75, 76]] + [(1, 1, y) for y in [77, 78, 79, 80, 81]])
a, b, y = (np.array(c, float) for c in zip(*runs))

# 1) Effects as regression coefficients: with +-1 coding, effect = 2 * coefficient.
sa, sb = 2 * a - 1, 2 * b - 1
X = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(y), sa, sb, sa * sb])
coef, res, rank, _ = np.linalg.lstsq(X, y, rcond=None)
EA, EB, EAB = 2 * coef[1], 2 * coef[2], 2 * coef[3]
print("effects", EA, EB, EAB)
assert np.allclose([EA, EB, EAB], [4.0, 5.0, 1.0])

# 2) Standard error and 95% intervals with the t distribution (N - 4 = 16 degrees of freedom).
dof = len(y) - 4
s = np.sqrt(((y - X @ coef) ** 2).sum() / dof)
se = s / np.sqrt(5)
tcrit = stats.t.ppf(0.975, dof)
print("s", s.round(3), "SE", se.round(3), "t", tcrit.round(3))
for name, e in (("A", EA), ("B", EB), ("AB", EAB)):
    print(name, round(e - tcrit * se, 2), round(e + tcrit * se, 2))
assert abs(s - 1.581) < 1e-3 and abs(se - 0.707) < 1e-3 and abs(tcrit - 2.120) < 1e-3
assert EAB - tcrit * se < 0 < EAB + tcrit * se                       # interaction not distinguishable from 0
# the same analysis as a coefficient t-test: coefficient SE is SE(E)/2
cov = s ** 2 * np.linalg.inv(X.T @ X)
assert np.allclose(2 * np.sqrt(np.diag(cov))[1:], se)

# 3) Power of the interval for E_AB by simulation (extension challenge).
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
def power(r, sims=5000, sigma=1.58):
    A = np.repeat([0, 1, 0, 1], r); B = np.repeat([0, 0, 1, 1], r)
    sa, sb = 2 * A - 1, 2 * B - 1
    X = np.column_stack([np.ones(4 * r), sa, sb, sa * sb]); XtXi = np.linalg.inv(X.T @ X)
    mu = 70 + 3 * A + 4 * B + 2 * A * B
    Y = mu[None, :] + rng.normal(0, sigma, (sims, 4 * r))
    coef = Y @ X @ XtXi.T
    resid = Y - coef @ X.T
    s2 = (resid ** 2).sum(1) / (4 * r - 4)
    se_c = np.sqrt(s2 * XtXi[3, 3])
    return np.mean(np.abs(coef[:, 3]) / se_c > stats.t.ppf(0.975, 4 * r - 4))
p3, p5 = power(3), power(5)
print("power for E_AB (true 2): r=3", p3, " r=5", p5)
assert p3 < p5 < 1.0
print("library track passed")
```

Expected: effects `4.0 5.0 1.0`, `s 1.581 SE 0.707 t 2.12`, intervals `[2.5, 5.5]`, `[3.5, 6.5]`, `[-0.5, 2.5]`, a power line in which $r=5$ beats $r=3$ (roughly 0.27 versus 0.17 here), then `library track passed`.

## Verification

- **Hand-check the effects** from the four means before running code.
- **Differences, not levels:** an offset added to all runs (a different eval set size, a constant) must not change effects. The shift test above checks this.
- **Design table:** print the run list and confirm every cell appears equally often and the factor columns are uncorrelated.
- **Replicates:** record the seeds. Cells with a single seed give no noise estimate.
- **Falsifier:** write down in advance the interval for $E_A$ that would make you drop the idea (for example upper bound below 1 point).

## Failure modes

| Mistake | Wrong value or symptom |
|---|---|
| Changing A and B together | "+9 points" assigned to one idea; correlation of columns 1.0 |
| One seed per cell | no variance estimate; a 2-point difference could be seed luck |
| Different eval sets per cell | spurious effect equal to the evaluation difference |
| Running cell by cell in time order | drift (thermal, data, library update) aligned with a factor |
| Ignoring interaction | "remove A" and "add A" give 5 and 3 points; reporting only one is misleading |
| Reusing the test set for tuning | optimistic effects (see E15) |
| Reporting means without intervals | cannot tell 1.0 from 0.0 (here the interaction interval is $[-0.5,2.5]$) |

## Alternatives and trade-offs

- **One-factor-at-a-time**: cheap, but cannot detect interactions; fine only if you know effects are additive.
- **Full factorial $2^k$**: complete, but $2^k$ cells; for $k\ge5$ use a fractional factorial that deliberately confounds high-order interactions.
- **Paired/blocked designs**: use the same seeds or initial states across conditions to cancel nuisance variance.
- **Randomised controlled trial (A/B test)**: for deployed systems with many independent users; needs a sample-size plan.
- **Observational comparison of logs**: no control of confounders; useful for hypotheses, not for causal claims.
- **Bayesian optimisation or random search for hyperparameters**: efficient for tuning, not for attributing effects.

## Transfer and retention

The same logic governs ablation tables in papers (E15), comparison of prompts or models (E10, E12) and any claim of the form "X caused Y". The standard error $\sigma/\sqrt r$ leads directly to the confidence intervals of RS03, and recording seeds, versions and run order is the substance of RS04.

Retention prompts: write $E_A$, $E_B$, $E_{AB}$ from memory; state why a two-condition design cannot separate two changes; state the scaling of replicates with effect size.

6. Output: `8 4 4 2`. The design has each of the four cells twice, so there are 8 runs, A is on in 4, B is on in 4, and (1,1) occurs in 2. Shuffling reorders runs but does not change counts.
7. Confounded by day, GPU (hardware/driver), and seeds (0-4 versus 5-9), all aligned with the method. Corrected protocol: run both methods on the same machines and seeds (paired), interleave runs in random order, record versions, and use at least 5 seeds per method.
8. $E_A=\tfrac12[(58-50)+(56-55)]=4.5$; $E_B=\tfrac12[(55-50)+(56-58)]=1.5$; $E_{AB}=\tfrac12[56+50-58-55]=-3.5$. Adding A to the baseline: $58-50=+8$. Removing A from the full system: $56-55=+1$. They disagree because of the strong negative interaction (redundancy): A helps a lot alone but little once B is present. Report the full table, not one number.
9. $E_A=\tfrac12(\bar y_{10}+\bar y_{11}-\bar y_{00}-\bar y_{01})$. Four independent cell means each with variance $\sigma^2/r$ and coefficients $\pm\tfrac12$: $\mathrm{Var}=4\cdot\tfrac14\cdot\sigma^2/r=\sigma^2/r$. With a seed effect shared across cells (same seeds in all cells) the shared term has coefficient $\tfrac12(1+1-1-1)=0$ in the contrast, so it cancels and the variance falls to the residual variance $\sigma_e^2/r$ (the seed variance $\tau^2$ no longer appears); blocking helps when $\tau^2$ is large.
10. A good answer states the factors, the run table with seeds and randomised order, cell means, effects with intervals, the pooled SD, and a falsifier such as "if the 95% interval for $E_A$ is entirely below +0.5 points, I reject that augmentation helps".
