## Why this matters

A robot that picks the highest immediate reward at each step will cheerfully grab a nearby item, wedge itself into a dead end, or drain its battery on the first easy task. Real tasks are chains: opening the door pays nothing now and everything later. This lesson gives the basic tools for reasoning over a chain of decisions: the horizon (how far you look), the information you have (state or belief), and whether the plan can react to what actually happens (closed loop).

It builds on RL02 (MDPs, rewards and transitions) and on C02 (basic programming structures used in the code). Later modules (E02 search, E03 uncertainty, E05 task planning) all refine these three ideas.

## Intuition

A fast-food counter robot can grab a snack now (reward 1) and then be stuck in a dead end, or walk to the kitchen (reward 0 now, but a chance of slipping) and then cook for reward 3 per step. If it has only one step left, grabbing is right. With four steps left, walking wins by a mile. The *same* state gives different best actions depending on how many steps remain.

![Left: the three-state decision problem A, B, C with rewards and the 0.8/0.2 slip. Right: value of the greedy policy, the best open-loop plan and the optimal closed-loop policy for horizon 4.](/lesson-figures/E01.svg)

*Figure: grab gives 1 and ends in a dead end; walk reaches the rewarding state B with probability 0.8. For four steps: greedy 1.0, open-loop plan 7.2, closed-loop 8.264.*

A second effect is uncertainty: after walking, the robot may not know whether it slipped. If it only senses its state through a noisy sensor, it should act on a belief, a probability distribution over the states, rather than on a guess.

## Theory and derivation

### Finite-horizon decision problems

An MDP with state $s$, action $a$, transition $P(s'|s,a)$ and reward $r(s,a,s')$. With $h$ steps to go, define the optimal value $V_h(s)$ as the best achievable expected total reward. Backward induction computes it from the end:
$$V_0(s)=0,\qquad V_h(s)=\max_a\sum_{s'}P(s'|s,a)\big[r(s,a,s')+V_{h-1}(s')\big].$$
The maximising action $\pi_h(s)$ may differ for each $h$: the optimal policy is *non-stationary* in the finite-horizon case. With a discount $\gamma<1$ and an infinite horizon, the horizon is replaced by an effective horizon of about $1/(1-\gamma)$, and the optimal policy is stationary.

### Greedy versus planning

The greedy rule maximises only the immediate expected reward $\sum_{s'}P(s'|s,a)\,r(s,a,s')$, i.e. it uses $V_{h-1}\equiv0$. It is optimal only if immediate reward is a perfect proxy for long-term value. Otherwise a lookahead depth $d$ gives a family of approximations: $d=1$ is greedy, $d=h$ is exact. A *receding-horizon* (model predictive) controller plans $d$ steps, applies the first action, observes, and replans.

### Open loop versus closed loop

An **open-loop plan** is a fixed sequence $a_1,\dots,a_H$ chosen in advance. A **closed-loop policy** chooses $a_t$ as a function of what has been observed. If transitions are stochastic, the closed-loop policy can correct deviations, so
$$V^{\text{closed}}\ge\max_{a_{1:H}}V^{\text{open}}(a_{1:H}),$$
because an open-loop plan is a special case of a policy that ignores feedback. The gap measures the value of feedback; it is zero if the dynamics are deterministic.

### Belief

When the state is hidden, the robot keeps a belief $b(s)=P(s_t=s\mid\text{history})$. After action $a$ and observation $z$:
$$b'(s')=\eta\,Z(z|s')\sum_sP(s'|s,a)\,b(s),$$
where $Z(z|s')$ is the sensor model and $\eta$ normalises. The belief is a sufficient statistic: planning on beliefs gives a Markov problem (a belief-MDP) even though the underlying observations are not Markov. E03 builds on this.

## Worked example

### Planning with a slippery action: horizon values, open loop, belief

**Problem.** A small stochastic MDP: in A, `grab` gives reward 1 and moves to the dead end C; `walk` gives 0 and moves to B with probability 0.8, otherwise stays at A. In B, `work` gives 3 and stays; `rest` gives 1. In C nothing happens. An action that is not available in a state gives reward 0 and leaves the state unchanged when it appears in a fixed plan. Find (1) the finite-horizon values $V_1(A),\dots,V_4(A)$ and the best first action; (2) the value of the greedy policy that always grabs, and the ratio to the planner at horizon 4; (3) the value of the open-loop plan (walk, work, work, work) and the gain of the closed-loop policy over it; (4) the belief after the robot starts in A, walks, and the sensor says "a" (probability 0.9 if the robot is in A, 0.1 if in B); (5) the switch point: with two steps to go, for which walk success probability $p$ does walking beat grabbing, and what changes if a failed walk led to the dead end C.

**Solution.**
1. Backward induction, $V_h(s)=\max_a\big[r(s,a)+\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)V_{h-1}(s')\big]$ with $V_0\equiv0$.
   - Horizon 1: $V_1(B)=3$, $V_1(C)=0$, $V_1(A)=\max(1,\ 0.8\cdot0+0.2\cdot0)=1$ via grab.
   - Horizon 2: $V_2(B)=3+V_1(B)=6$. For A: grab $=1+V_1(C)=1$; walk $=0+0.8V_1(B)+0.2V_1(A)=0.8\cdot3+0.2\cdot1=2.6$. Walk wins: $V_2(A)=2.6$.
   - Horizon 3: $V_3(B)=3+V_2(B)=9$. $V_3(A)=\max(1,\ 0.8\cdot6+0.2\cdot2.6)=\max(1,\ 5.32)=5.32$.
   - Horizon 4: $V_4(A)=0.8V_3(B)+0.2V_3(A)=0.8\cdot9+0.2\cdot5.32=7.2+1.064=8.264$.
   The first actions are grab, walk, walk, walk for horizons 1 to 4.
2. Greedy: always grab, the reward is $1$ and the episode ends in C. At horizon 4 the planner is $8.264/1=8.264$ times better.
3. Open loop, plan (walk, work, work, work): with probability 0.8 the walk succeeds and the three works pay $3\cdot3=9$; with probability 0.2 the robot stays at A, where `work` is unavailable and pays 0. Value $0.8\cdot9+0.2\cdot0=7.2$. The closed-loop policy walks again after a slip, gaining $8.264-7.2=1.064$.
4. Belief update (Bayes). Start in A with certainty and walk: predicted belief A $0.2$, B $0.8$. Observation "a": unnormalised posterior A $0.2\cdot0.9=0.18$, B $0.8\cdot0.1=0.08$. The normaliser is $0.18+0.08=0.26$, so $b(A)=0.18/0.26=0.6923$ and $b(B)=0.08/0.26=0.3077$. A reading that contradicts the model's prior 0.8 for B outweighed it because the sensor is fairly reliable.
5. Switch point, two steps to go. Grab is worth $1+V_1(C)=1$. Walk with success probability $p$ leads to B (worth $V_1(B)=3$) or stays at A (worth $V_1(A)=1$): $3p+(1-p)\cdot1=1+2p$. This exceeds 1 for every $p>0$, because a slip leaves the robot at A where grab is still available. If a failed walk led to the dead end C (value 0), walk would be worth $3p+(1-p)\cdot0=3p$, which beats grab only for $3p>1$, i.e. $p>1/3$.

**Check.** The recursion was recomputed numerically: $V_h(A)=1,\ 2.6,\ 5.32,\ 8.264$ and the open-loop value $7.2$; the posterior $(0.6923,0.3077)$ sums to 1. Sanity bounds: $V_4(A)$ lies between the open-loop value $7.2$ and the best deterministic payoff $9$ (success at once), and the posterior with a reliable sensor moves toward A as expected.

**Takeaway.** With stochastic dynamics the plan must be a policy, not a fixed action sequence: the closed-loop value (8.264) beats open loop (7.2) and greedy (1), and the best action can change with the horizon or with the belief.

## Reference implementation

Greedy action, backward induction, open-loop evaluation and a belief update for the three-state problem.

```python
# mdp[state][action] = list of (probability, next_state, reward)
MDP = {
    "A": {"grab": [(1.0, "C", 1.0)],                       # tempting immediate reward, then a dead end
          "walk": [(0.8, "B", 0.0), (0.2, "A", 0.0)]},     # invest: may slip and stay at A
    "B": {"work": [(1.0, "B", 3.0)], "rest": [(1.0, "B", 1.0)]},
    "C": {"idle": [(1.0, "C", 0.0)]},
}
# Z[state][observation]: a noisy sensor that reports which of A/B the robot is in
Z = {"A": {"a": 0.9, "b": 0.1}, "B": {"a": 0.1, "b": 0.9}, "C": {"a": 0.5, "b": 0.5}}

def q_value(mdp, s, a, v_next):
    return sum(p * (r + v_next[s2]) for p, s2, r in mdp[s][a])

def greedy_action(mdp, s):
    """One-step lookahead on immediate expected reward only."""
    return max(mdp[s], key=lambda a: sum(p * r for p, _, r in mdp[s][a]))

def backward_induction(mdp, H):
    """Finite-horizon optimal values V[h][s] (h steps to go) and the first-step policy at each h."""
    V = [{s: 0.0 for s in mdp}]; pi = [None]
    for h in range(1, H + 1):
        v, p = {}, {}
        for s in mdp:
            best = max(mdp[s], key=lambda a: q_value(mdp, s, a, V[h - 1]))
            p[s] = best; v[s] = q_value(mdp, s, best, V[h - 1])
        V.append(v); pi.append(p)
    return V, pi

def open_loop_value(mdp, s0, actions):
    """Expected return of a FIXED action sequence. An action that is not available in the
    current state does nothing (stay, reward 0)."""
    dist = {s0: 1.0}; total = 0.0
    for a in actions:
        new = {}
        for s, ps in dist.items():
            if a not in mdp[s]:
                new[s] = new.get(s, 0.0) + ps; continue
            for p, s2, r in mdp[s][a]:
                new[s2] = new.get(s2, 0.0) + ps * p; total += ps * p * r
        dist = new
    return total

def greedy_value(mdp, s0, H):
    """Expected return of the policy 'always take the greedy action' over H steps."""
    dist = {s0: 1.0}; total = 0.0
    for _ in range(H):
        new = {}
        for s, ps in dist.items():
            for p, s2, r in mdp[s][greedy_action(mdp, s)]:
                new[s2] = new.get(s2, 0.0) + ps * p; total += ps * p * r
        dist = new
    return total

def belief_update(b, mdp, a, z, Z):
    """Bayes filter: predict through action a, then weight by P(z | state) and renormalise."""
    pred = {s: 0.0 for s in mdp}
    for s, ps in b.items():
        if a not in mdp[s]:
            pred[s] += ps; continue
        for p, s2, _ in mdp[s][a]:
            pred[s2] += ps * p
    post = {s: pred[s] * Z[s][z] for s in mdp}
    n = sum(post.values())
    if n == 0:
        raise ValueError("observation impossible under the model")
    return {s: v / n for s, v in post.items()}

if __name__ == "__main__":
    V, pi = backward_induction(MDP, 4)
    print("V_h(A), h=1..4:", [round(V[h]["A"], 4) for h in range(1, 5)])
    print("first action from A by horizon:", [pi[h]["A"] for h in range(1, 5)])
    print("greedy value (H=4): %.4f" % greedy_value(MDP, "A", 4))
    print("open-loop walk,work,work,work: %.4f" % open_loop_value(MDP, "A", ["walk", "work", "work", "work"]))
    print("closed-loop optimum (H=4): %.4f" % V[4]["A"])
    b = belief_update({"A": 1.0, "B": 0.0, "C": 0.0}, MDP, "walk", "a", Z)
    print("belief after walk and a sensor reading 'a':", {s: round(p, 4) for s, p in b.items()})
    assert abs(V[4]["A"] - 8.264) < 1e-9 and greedy_value(MDP, "A", 4) == 1.0
    assert abs(open_loop_value(MDP, "A", ["walk", "work", "work", "work"]) - 7.2) < 1e-9
    print("self-test passed")
```

Expected: $V_h(A)=1,\ 2.6,\ 5.32,\ 8.264$; first actions grab, walk, walk, walk; greedy 1.0; open-loop 7.2; belief $A=0.6923$.

### Library track

Redo the three-state problem with NumPy (`pip install numpy`). Save your stdlib solution as `lab_e01.py` (starter with stubs implemented, without tests). The script turns the MDP into a transition tensor `T[a, s, s']` and a reward matrix, runs backward induction as one matrix product per step, evaluates the open-loop plan as a product of matrices, and does the belief update as $b'\propto Z[:,z]\odot(T_a^\top b)$, each checked against your stdlib code.

```python
import numpy as np
from lab_e01 import MDP, Z, backward_induction, open_loop_value, belief_update

S = ["A", "B", "C"]; ACT = ["grab", "walk", "work", "rest", "idle"]
# T[a, s, s'] transition tensor and Rw[a, s] expected immediate reward; unavailable actions are
# marked with a mask and excluded from the max (they are NOT "stay with reward 0" here).
T = np.zeros((5, 3, 3)); Rw = np.zeros((5, 3)); ok = np.zeros((5, 3), bool)
for i, s in enumerate(S):
    for a, outs in MDP[s].items():
        j = ACT.index(a); ok[j, i] = True
        for p, s2, r in outs:
            T[j, i, S.index(s2)] += p; Rw[j, i] += p * r

def backward_np(H):
    V = np.zeros(3); out = [V]
    for _ in range(H):
        Q = Rw + T @ V                         # shape (action, state): Q[a, s] = r + sum_s' P V
        V = np.where(ok, Q, -np.inf).max(axis=0); out.append(V)
    return out

Vs = backward_np(4)
V_ref, _ = backward_induction(MDP, 4)
for h in range(5):
    assert np.allclose(Vs[h], [V_ref[h][s] for s in S])
print("V_h(A):", [round(float(v[0]), 4) for v in Vs[1:]])

# open-loop value of walk,work,work,work as a product of matrices
dist = np.array([1.0, 0, 0]); total = 0.0
for a in ["walk", "work", "work", "work"]:
    j = ACT.index(a)
    # unavailable action = stay in place with reward 0
    Tj = np.where(ok[j][:, None], T[j], np.eye(3)); rj = np.where(ok[j], Rw[j], 0.0)
    total += dist @ rj; dist = dist @ Tj
assert abs(total - open_loop_value(MDP, "A", ["walk", "work", "work", "work"])) < 1e-12
print("open loop: %.4f" % total)

# belief update as matrix algebra: b' proportional to Z[:, z] * (T[a].T @ b)
Zm = np.array([[Z[s][z] for z in "ab"] for s in S])        # (state, obs)
b = np.array([1.0, 0, 0])
b = Zm[:, 0] * (T[ACT.index("walk")].T @ b); b /= b.sum()
ref = belief_update({"A": 1.0, "B": 0.0, "C": 0.0}, MDP, "walk", "a", Z)
assert np.allclose(b, [ref[s] for s in S]); print("belief:", b.round(4))
```

Expected output: `V_h(A): [1.0, 2.6, 5.32, 8.264]`, `open loop: 7.2000` and `belief: [0.6923 0.3077 0.    ]`. An unavailable action is masked out of the max in the closed-loop recursion, and treated as "stay, reward 0" only in the open-loop evaluation, exactly as in the stdlib version.

## Verification

- Check backward induction on a case you can do by hand ($V_1,V_2$).
- Confirm that closed-loop value is at least every open-loop value (brute force on short horizons).
- Confirm that beliefs sum to 1 and that deterministic dynamics give a gap of zero between open and closed loop.
- Vary the horizon: the first action at A should change from grab to walk exactly at the crossover.

## Failure modes

- **Greedy trap**: grab gives 1 when walking is worth 8.264 at horizon 4.
- **Wrong horizon**: using a stationary policy for a finite-horizon task; the best first action at $h=1$ differs from $h\ge2$.
- **Forgetting probabilities** (exercise 5): sums without $p$ give $4$ instead of $2.6$ for $V_2(A)$.
- **Open-loop plan on a stochastic system**: 7.2 instead of 8.264, and zero reward on a slip.
- **Using the last observation as the state**: with a 0.9-reliable sensor it is wrong 10% of the time; keep a belief.
- **Unnormalised belief** or an impossible observation silently producing NaN.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Method | When | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greedy | Immediate reward predicts long-term value | Cheapest; traps |
| Fixed lookahead / MPC | Moderate horizons, good model | Plans each step; horizon tuning |
| Full dynamic programming | Small state space, known model | $O(H\,|S|^2|A|)$ |

Use MPC with a horizon at least as long as the time it takes for a bad action to reveal its cost.

## Transfer and retention

The same structure runs through RL (value iteration is backward induction without a horizon), through search (E02, where deterministic open-loop plans suffice), and through task planning (E05).
