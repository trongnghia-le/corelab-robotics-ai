## Why this matters

Classification (V07) says what is in the image; a robot also needs to know where. A detector gives each object a box, a class and a confidence, and a downstream planner uses the box to steer, grasp or track. To compare detectors, or to decide whether yours is good enough to drive a robot, you need numbers that mean the same thing every time: when is a box correct, how are duplicates treated, and how does the score threshold trade missed objects against false alarms? The same questions return when a tracker (V09) consumes the detections and when you set the confidence threshold in a deployed system. The lesson extends confusion-matrix evaluation (V07, ML07) to localisation.

## Intuition

A detection is a *claim*: "there is a cone inside this rectangle, and I am 87% sure." The claim is right only if the rectangle overlaps a real cone well enough. Overlap is measured by **intersection over union (IoU)**: the area both boxes cover divided by the area either covers. It is 1 for identical boxes, 0 for disjoint ones, and it does not depend on the image size.

A real detector produces many overlapping candidates for one object. Without cleaning, a single cone appears as five boxes, one right and four "false positives". **Non-maximum suppression (NMS)** keeps the highest-scoring box and discards neighbours that overlap it too much. Then, sweeping the score threshold from high to low, each threshold gives a (recall, precision) point; the area under that curve is **average precision (AP)**.

*IoU is intersection over union (left); AP is the area under the precision-recall envelope when detections are ranked by score (right).*

## Theory and derivation

### Boxes and IoU

An axis-aligned box can be given as corners $(x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)$, as corner plus size $(x,y,w,h)$ or as centre plus size $(c_x,c_y,w,h)$. Conversions are trivial but a frequent source of silent bugs, so state the format. Treat coordinates as continuous (pixel edges); with integer pixel indices that include both ends you must add 1 to widths, and mixing the two conventions changes IoU slightly.

For boxes $A$ and $B$,
$$\mathrm{IoU}(A,B)=\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A\cup B|}=\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A|+|B|-|A\cap B|}.$$
The intersection is a box with width $\max(0,\min(x_2^A,x_2^B)-\max(x_1^A,x_1^B))$ and similarly for height. The clamp at zero is essential. IoU is symmetric, lies in $[0,1]$ and is invariant to scaling both boxes. Two equal squares of side $s$ whose positions differ by $d<s$ along one axis have
$$\mathrm{IoU}=\frac{s(s-d)}{s^2+s^2-s(s-d)}=\frac{s-d}{s+d},$$
so IoU $=0.5$ at $d=s/3$: a box shifted by a third of its side is only just a match. For a small box inside a big one IoU is the area ratio, which is why IoU punishes loose boxes.

### Non-maximum suppression

Greedy NMS with threshold $\tau$: sort by score; repeatedly keep the best remaining box and delete every remaining box with $\mathrm{IoU}>\tau$ with it. A low $\tau$ (0.3) removes more duplicates and also real neighbours (crowds); a high $\tau$ (0.7) keeps more duplicates. NMS is usually applied per class. Soft-NMS and learned NMS decay scores instead of deleting.

### Matching rule

To evaluate, sort detections of a class by decreasing score. Walk down the list; for each detection find the ground-truth box with the highest IoU among those **not yet matched**. If that IoU is at least $\tau_{IoU}$ (0.5 in the original PASCAL VOC rule) the detection is a **true positive** and the ground truth is marked used; otherwise it is a **false positive**. Ground truths never matched are **false negatives**. One-to-one matching means that a second box on the same object counts as a false positive, which is what penalises duplicates.

### Precision, recall and the curve

After the first $i$ detections let $TP_i$, $FP_i$ be the cumulative counts, and $N$ the number of ground-truth objects. Then
$$P_i=\frac{TP_i}{TP_i+FP_i},\qquad R_i=\frac{TP_i}{N}.$$
Recall never decreases down the ranked list; precision goes up and down.

### Average precision

Make precision a non-increasing envelope: $\tilde P_i=\max_{j\ge i}P_j$. Then
$$\mathrm{AP}=\sum_i(R_i-R_{i-1})\,\tilde P_i,\qquad R_0=0 .$$
This is the area under the interpolated curve (PASCAL VOC 2010+ all-point rule). COCO samples precision at 101 recall levels and averages over IoU thresholds $0.50,0.55,\dots,0.95$ and over classes ("mAP@[.5:.95]"). AP does not choose a threshold; a deployed system must, and the operating point (precision and recall at the chosen score cut-off) is what the robot experiences. Because of the envelope, extra low-ranked false positives after the last true positive do not change AP, but they do hurt precision at the operating threshold.

### Where errors come from

Classification error (wrong class), localisation error (right class, IoU too low), duplicate error (NMS failure), background error (box on nothing), and missed objects. Diagnose by categorising false positives, not just by reporting one AP.

## Worked example

**1. IoU by hand.** $A=(0,0,10,10)$, $B=(5,5,15,15)$. Intersection: width $\min(10,15)-\max(0,5)=5$, height 5, area 25. Union $=100+100-25=175$. $\mathrm{IoU}=25/175=0.143$: not a match.

**2. A realistic pair.** $A=(20,20,80,100)$, $B=(50,40,110,120)$. Intersection $x\in[50,80]$, $y\in[40,100]$: $30\times60=1800$. Areas $60\cdot80=4800$ each. Union $=9600-1800=7800$. $\mathrm{IoU}=0.231$.

**3. Shifted squares.** Side 60, shift 20: $\mathrm{IoU}=(60-20)/(60+20)=0.5$, computed directly from $(0,0,60,60)$ and $(20,0,80,60)$: intersection $40\cdot60=2400$, union $3600+3600-2400=4800$, $0.5$.

**4. NMS.** Boxes $b_0=(10,10,60,60)$ score 0.8, $b_1=(15,12,65,64)$ 0.9, $b_2=(100,100,150,150)$ 0.7, $b_3=(12,8,58,55)$ 0.6. $\mathrm{IoU}(b_1,b_0)=0.735$, $\mathrm{IoU}(b_1,b_3)=0.635$. With $\tau=0.5$: keep $b_1$ (best), delete $b_0$ and $b_3$; keep $b_2$. Result: $[1,2]$.

**5. AP.** Ranked flags $[1,1,0,1,0]$, $N=4$: precision $[1,1,0.667,0.75,0.6]$, recall $[0.25,0.5,0.5,0.75,0.75]$. Envelope $[1,1,0.75,0.75,0.6]$. AP $=0.25\cdot1+0.25\cdot1+0.25\cdot0.75=0.6875$.

**6. Duplicates.** Ground truths $g_0=(10,10,60,60)$, $g_1=(100,100,150,150)$. Detections: $(0.9,\,(15,12,65,64))$, $(0.8,\,(10,10,60,60))$, $(0.7,\,(102,98,152,148))$, $(0.3,\,(200,200,230,230))$. The second box also overlaps $g_0$ (IoU 0.735) but $g_0$ is already used, so it is an FP: flags $[1,0,1,0]$, AP $=0.5\cdot1+0.5\cdot0.667=0.833$. Apply NMS first (removes the 0.8 box): flags $[1,1,0]$, AP $=1.0$.

## Reference implementation

IoU, format conversion and greedy NMS, checked against the worked examples.

```python
def area(b):
    return max(0.0, b[2] - b[0]) * max(0.0, b[3] - b[1])

def iou(a, b):
    ix = min(a[2], b[2]) - max(a[0], b[0])
    iy = min(a[3], b[3]) - max(a[1], b[1])
    if ix <= 0 or iy <= 0:
        return 0.0
    inter = ix * iy
    return inter / (area(a) + area(b) - inter)

def xywh_to_xyxy(b):
    x, y, w, h = b
    return (x, y, x + w, y + h)

def nms(boxes, scores, thr=0.5):
    order = sorted(range(len(boxes)), key=lambda i: (-scores[i], i))
    keep = []
    for i in order:
        if all(iou(boxes[i], boxes[k]) <= thr for k in keep):
            keep.append(i)
    return keep

assert abs(iou((0, 0, 10, 10), (5, 5, 15, 15)) - 25 / 175) < 1e-12
assert abs(iou((20, 20, 80, 100), (50, 40, 110, 120)) - 1800 / 7800) < 1e-12
assert iou((0, 0, 2, 2), (5, 5, 7, 7)) == 0.0
assert iou((0, 0, 60, 60), (20, 0, 80, 60)) == 0.5
assert iou((10, 10, 30, 30), (0, 0, 100, 100)) == 0.04
assert xywh_to_xyxy((5, 6, 10, 20)) == (5, 6, 15, 26)
boxes = [(10, 10, 60, 60), (15, 12, 65, 64), (100, 100, 150, 150), (12, 8, 58, 55)]
assert nms(boxes, [0.8, 0.9, 0.7, 0.6], 0.5) == [1, 2]
assert nms(boxes, [0.8, 0.9, 0.7, 0.6], 0.8) == [1, 0, 2, 3]
print("reference checks passed")
```

## Verification

Test IoU on cases with known answers: identical, disjoint, touching edges (IoU 0), nested (area ratio), shifted squares ($(s-d)/(s+d)$). Test invariants: symmetry, range, scale invariance. Check the matcher with a hand-built example including a duplicate and a missed object, and check that the number of TP never exceeds the number of ground truths. For AP, build the curve on a sheet of paper first. Compare against a trusted library (for example `pycocotools`) on a small dataset: results should agree to about three decimals if you use the same rule. Always view detections drawn on images next to the numbers.

## Failure modes

- **Missing clamp.** Disjoint boxes get a positive "intersection" when both extents are negative: IoU $=-9$ in Exercise 6.
- **Coordinate format mix-up.** Treating $(x,y,w,h)$ as $(x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)$ gives nonsense boxes and IoU near 0 for good detections.
- **Not marking ground truth as used.** Every duplicate counts as TP, recall exceeds 1.
- **Sorting ascending.** Low-score boxes claim ground truth first; AP collapses.
- **NMS across classes.** A cone and a person standing close are merged.
- **Threshold confusion.** IoU threshold (match), NMS threshold (suppress) and score threshold (deploy) are three different numbers.
- **Reporting AP alone.** Trailing false positives do not change AP but give the robot false alarms: precision at the deployed threshold may be 0.33.
- **Tiny objects.** One pixel of error changes IoU of a 10-pixel box a lot, so small-object AP is low even when the system is fine for the task.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Choice | Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Axis-aligned boxes | Simple, fast IoU | Loose for rotated objects |
| Rotated boxes / polygons | Tighter fit | Harder IoU, labels |
| Instance masks (V10) | Exact shape | More labelling and compute |
| Keypoints / centre points | No NMS needed (CenterNet-style) | Different pipeline |
| IoU 0.5 | Forgiving, standard | Allows sloppy boxes |
| IoU 0.5:0.95 average | Rewards precise boxes | Hard to interpret |
| Hard NMS | Simple | Fails in crowds |
| Soft-NMS | Keeps crowded objects | Extra parameter |
| One-stage detectors (YOLO, SSD) | Fast | Often weaker on small objects |
| Two-stage detectors (Faster R-CNN) | Accurate | Slower |

Pick the evaluation that matches the use: for a gripper, require tight boxes (high IoU) and check the downstream pose error directly; for obstacle avoidance, recall at a low false-alarm rate matters more than box precision.

## Transfer and retention

Detections feed trackers (V09), which have the same matching problem across time with a motion prediction. The precision-recall trade-off reappears in any alarm system. The matching procedure (greedy, one-to-one, by score) is the same pattern as data association in tracking and in SLAM landmark matching, with a distance instead of IoU. Without looking, derive $(s-d)/(s+d)$ and compute AP for flags $[1,0,1,1,0]$ with $N=4$ (it is $0.25\cdot1+0.25\cdot0.75+0.25\cdot0.75=0.625$). Return in a week and write NMS from scratch.
