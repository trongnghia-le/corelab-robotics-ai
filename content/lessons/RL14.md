## Why this matters

An RL algorithm maximises the reward you wrote, not the behaviour you wanted. If the two differ, a good optimiser finds the difference. This is not an exotic failure: a simulated boat circling a bonus target instead of finishing the race, a manipulation policy that drops the object into the bin from a height so that a "distance to bin" term is minimal, a cleaning robot that hides dirt where the sensor cannot see it. Each policy is optimal for its stated reward and useless for the task.

Reward engineering is the practice of writing the reward, adding learning signals that make a sparse task tractable (shaping), and testing that the result is what you meant. This lesson makes the issues exact on a six-cell corridor where the optimal policy can be computed by hand, and gives a recipe for evaluation that does not trust the training reward.

## Intuition

Think of the reward as a contract. An intended objective such as "bring the part to the bin without breaking anything" has many implicit clauses. The reward you write captures a few, and the optimiser fills the unwritten clauses in whatever way pays best. Three typical gaps are:

- **Loopholes**: a repeatable bonus (for passing a checkpoint, for touching the object, for speed) can be collected again and again.
- **Side effects**: things you care about but did not price (collisions, wear, energy, safety margins) are treated as free.
- **Sparse reward**: if only the final goal pays, a random policy rarely sees any signal, so designers add intermediate rewards, which then become the loophole.

![A corridor of six cells with a checkpoint bonus at cell 3, the intended path to the goal and a loop between cells 2 and 3 with its higher proxy value](/lesson-figures/RL14.svg)

*With a repeatable +2 checkpoint bonus and discount 0.95, bouncing between cells 2 and 3 is worth 18.51 against 10.83 for finishing, so the optimal policy for the proxy never reaches the goal.*

Shaping that adds the **difference of a potential** $\gamma\phi(s')-\phi(s)$ is special: what it gives on the way in, it takes back on the way out, so no cycle pays. A bonus for *entering* a state has no such give-back.

## Theory and derivation

### Optimal policy for a given reward

For a deterministic MDP with reward $r(s,a,s')$ and discount $\gamma<1$, the optimal values satisfy $V(s)=\max_a[r(s,a,s')+\gamma V(s')]$ with $V=0$ at the terminal state. Any change to $r$ is a change to the problem; the optimiser answers the new question.

### Loop versus finish

Suppose entering a checkpoint pays an extra bonus $b$, each step costs $c$ and finishing pays $G$. From the cell before the checkpoint, repeating the two-step cycle $[b-c,\,-c]$ forever is worth

$$V_{\text{loop}}=\frac{(b-c)-\gamma c}{1-\gamma^2},$$

while heading to the goal (checkpoint, one more step, then goal reward $G$) is worth

$$V_{\text{finish}}=(b-c)-\gamma c+\gamma^2G.$$

Setting them equal, with $X=b-c-\gamma c$, gives $X\gamma^2/(1-\gamma^2)=\gamma^2G$, so the loop wins exactly when

$$b>b^*=G(1-\gamma^2)+c(1+\gamma).$$

With $G=10$, $c=0.1$, $\gamma=0.95$: $b^*=0.975+0.195=1.17$. The threshold grows as $\gamma$ falls, because discounting favours the nearby goal reward; a loophole that is invisible with $\gamma=0.9$ appears with $\gamma=0.95$. Rewards must be tested with the discount actually used in training.

### Potential-based shaping

Given any function $\phi(s)$ (with $\phi=0$ at terminal states), define

$$r'(s,a,s')=r(s,a,s')+\gamma\phi(s')-\phi(s).$$

For any trajectory ending in a terminal state, the discounted shaping terms telescope: $\sum_t\gamma^t[\gamma\phi(s_{t+1})-\phi(s_t)]=-\phi(s_0)$. The return changes by a quantity that depends only on the start state, so $Q'(s,a)=Q(s,a)-\phi(s)$ and the ranking of actions in every state is unchanged: **the optimal policy is preserved**. A good $\phi$ (such as minus the distance to the goal) still speeds learning by making informative rewards denser. A bonus for entering a state is not a potential difference: along the loop $2\to3\to2$ the bonus is collected each lap, whereas a potential gives $+(\gamma\phi_3-\phi_2)$ and $+(\gamma\phi_2-\phi_3)$, whose discounted sum over any number of laps telescopes, so it stays bounded and cannot grow with the number of laps.

### Side effects and evaluation

What you do not reward you cannot rely on. For each clause that matters (safety, wear, time, collisions), either price it in the reward or *measure* it separately and gate deployment on it. The key evaluation principle: report **task success and side-effect metrics computed from the environment state**, not the training return. A detector for the loophole above needs two numbers: did the episode reach the goal, and how many times was the checkpoint entered?

## Worked example

Corridor cells $0,\dots,5$, goal at 5 (absorbing, pays $+10$ on entry), each other step $-0.1$, $\gamma=0.95$, start at cell 0. Actions left/right, deterministic, left at cell 0 stays.

1. **True reward.** Optimal values (by value iteration) $V=(7.774,\,8.289,\,8.830,\,9.400,\,10.000)$ for cells $0$ to $4$; policy: always right. Return along the path: $-0.1(1+\gamma+\gamma^2+\gamma^3)+10\gamma^4=-0.371+8.145=7.774$.
2. **Checkpoint bonus +2 on every entry to cell 3.** Optimal values are $V=(16.513,\,17.487,\,18.513,\,17.487,\,18.513)$ and the policy is right at cells 0, 1, 2 and *left* at cells 3, 4. Roll-out from cell 0: $0,1,2,3,2,3,2,3,\dots$ for the whole 40-step horizon, 19 checkpoint entries and no success. Check cell 2: looping is worth $(1.9-0.095)/(1-0.9025)=18.513$ and finishing only $2+8.83=10.83$.
3. **Threshold.** $b^*=1.17$; the sweep gives the same: bonus $1$ finishes, bonus $1.25$ loops.
4. **Discount dependence.** With $b=2$ the policy finishes for $\gamma\le0.9$ and loops for $\gamma=0.95$; the exact switch is $\gamma=0.905$ (solve $10(1-\gamma^2)+0.1(1+\gamma)=2$).
5. **Potential shaping** with $\phi(s)=-(5-s)$. Values are $V=(12.774,\,12.288,\,11.83,\,11.4,\,11.0)$ and the policy is unchanged (always right). Along the path the shaped return minus the true return is $5.0=-\phi(0)$, as the telescoping identity predicts.
6. **The bonus written as a potential**, $\phi(s)=2\cdot[s\ge3]$: the policy finishes, with $V(0)=7.774$ identical to the true reward because $\phi(0)=0$. The same "reach the checkpoint region" incentive is kept without the farming.
7. **Evaluation.** The detector on the bonus policy returns `success=False, steps=40, entries=19, hacked=True`; on the other policies `success=True, steps=5, entries=1`. The training return of the hacked policy (16.5) is *higher* than that of the intended one (7.8), which is why training return cannot be the metric.

## Reference implementation

```python
"""A reward loophole on a 6-cell corridor, potential-based shaping, and an evaluation that detects hacking."""
N, GOAL, CHECK, GAMMA, BONUS = 6, 5, 3, 0.95, 2.0

def step(s, a):                      # a = 0 left, 1 right; deterministic; the goal is absorbing
    if s == GOAL:
        return GOAL
    return max(0, s - 1) if a == 0 else s + 1

def true_reward(s, a, s2):
    return 10.0 if s2 == GOAL else -0.1

def checkpoint_reward(s, a, s2):     # repeatable bonus for entering the checkpoint cell: the loophole
    return true_reward(s, a, s2) + (BONUS if s2 == CHECK else 0.0)

def phi(s): return -float(GOAL - s)  # potential: minus the distance to the goal

def shaped_reward(s, a, s2):         # potential-based shaping F = gamma*phi(s') - phi(s)
    f = 0.0 if s2 == GOAL else GAMMA * phi(s2)
    return true_reward(s, a, s2) + f - phi(s)

def value_iteration(reward, iters=2000):
    V = [0.0] * N
    for _ in range(iters):
        V = [0.0 if s == GOAL else max(reward(s, a, step(s, a)) + (0.0 if step(s, a) == GOAL else GAMMA * V[step(s, a)]) for a in (0, 1)) for s in range(N)]
    pol = [max((0, 1), key=lambda a: reward(s, a, step(s, a)) + (0.0 if step(s, a) == GOAL else GAMMA * V[step(s, a)])) for s in range(N)]
    return V, pol

def rollout(policy, start=0, horizon=40):
    s, trace = start, [start]
    for t in range(horizon):
        s = step(s, policy[s]); trace.append(s)
        if s == GOAL: break
    return trace

def potential_bonus(s, a, s2):       # the same bonus written as a potential difference: reaching >= CHECK is worth BONUS once
    p = lambda x: BONUS if x >= CHECK else 0.0
    return true_reward(s, a, s2) + (0.0 if s2 == GOAL else GAMMA * p(s2)) - p(s)

def evaluate(trace):
    """Evaluation that targets the intended behaviour, not the proxy: success, steps used, checkpoint entries."""
    return {"success": trace[-1] == GOAL, "steps": len(trace) - 1, "checkpoint_entries": sum(1 for x in trace[1:] if x == CHECK)}

if __name__ == "__main__":
    for name, rw in (("true", true_reward), ("checkpoint bonus", checkpoint_reward), ("potential shaping", shaped_reward), ("bonus as potential", potential_bonus)):
        V, pol = value_iteration(rw)
        tr = rollout(pol)
        print(f"{name:18s} V(0)={V[0]:7.3f}  policy={pol[:5]}  trace={tr[:12]}{'...' if len(tr) > 12 else ''}  {evaluate(tr)}")
    # Telescoping check: shaped return along a goal-reaching path equals true return - phi(start) (+ gamma^T phi(end)=0).
    path = [0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5]
    G = lambda rw: sum(GAMMA ** t * rw(path[t], 1, path[t + 1]) for t in range(len(path) - 1))
    print("true return", round(G(true_reward), 4), " shaped return", round(G(shaped_reward), 4), " difference", round(G(shaped_reward) - G(true_reward), 4), "= -phi(0) =", -phi(0))
    # Loop value: alternating 2,3,2,3,... under the bonus reward
    loop = sum(GAMMA ** t * checkpoint_reward(2 if t % 2 == 0 else 3, 1 if t % 2 == 0 else 0, 3 if t % 2 == 0 else 2) for t in range(2000))
    print("value of looping 2<->3 from cell 2 under the bonus:", round(loop, 3))
    V, pol = value_iteration(checkpoint_reward); assert pol[3] == 0 and not evaluate(rollout(pol))["success"]
    for rw in (shaped_reward, potential_bonus, true_reward):
        assert evaluate(rollout(value_iteration(rw)[1]))["success"]
    print("self-test passed")
```

### Library track

Redo the audit with NumPy and Gymnasium: a vectorised value iteration that reproduces the lesson's values and the bonus threshold $b^*=1.17$ (and the potential-shaped values), then a `gymnasium.Wrapper` that adds $\gamma\phi(s')-\phi(s)$ to a custom corridor environment and confirms the telescoping identity on a real rollout. ```python
import numpy as np
import gymnasium as gym
from gymnasium import spaces

N, GOAL, CHECK, GAMMA = 6, 5, 3, 0.95

# 1. Vectorised value iteration on the corridor, for any bonus (repeatable) -- compare with the lesson.
def solve(bonus=0.0, potential=None, gamma=GAMMA):
    nxt = np.array([[max(0, s - 1) if a == 0 else min(GOAL, s + 1) for a in (0, 1)] for s in range(N)])
    nxt[GOAL] = GOAL
    R = np.where(nxt == GOAL, 10.0, -0.1) + bonus * (nxt == CHECK)
    if potential is not None:
        phi = potential(np.arange(N)); phi_next = np.where(nxt == GOAL, 0.0, phi[nxt])
        R = R + gamma * phi_next - phi[:, None]
    V = np.zeros(N)
    for _ in range(2000):
        Q = R + gamma * np.where(nxt == GOAL, 0.0, V[nxt])
        V = Q.max(1); V[GOAL] = 0.0
    return V, Q.argmax(1)

V, pol = solve()
print("true V:", V[:5].round(3), "policy", pol[:5])
assert np.allclose(V[:5], [7.774, 8.289, 8.830, 9.400, 10.0], atol=1e-3)
V2, pol2 = solve(bonus=2.0)
print("bonus 2 V:", V2[:5].round(3), "policy", pol2[:5])
assert np.allclose(V2[:5], [16.513, 17.487, 18.513, 17.487, 18.513], atol=1e-3) and list(pol2[3:5]) == [0, 0]
b_star = 10 * (1 - GAMMA ** 2) + 0.1 * (1 + GAMMA)
for b in (1.0, 1.1, 1.25, 2.0):
    loops = solve(bonus=b)[1][3] == 0
    print(f"bonus {b}: {'loops' if loops else 'finishes'}")
    assert loops == (b > b_star)
Vp, polp = solve(bonus=0.0, potential=lambda s: -(GOAL - s).astype(float))
assert list(polp[:5]) == [1] * 5 and np.isclose(Vp[0] - V[0], 5.0)      # same policy, V shifted by -phi(0)

# 2. A Gymnasium wrapper doing potential-based shaping, checked by the telescoping identity.
class Corridor(gym.Env):
    observation_space = spaces.Discrete(N); action_space = spaces.Discrete(2)
    def reset(self, *, seed=None, options=None):
        super().reset(seed=seed); self.s = 0; return self.s, {}
    def step(self, a):
        self.s = max(0, self.s - 1) if a == 0 else self.s + 1
        done = self.s == GOAL
        return self.s, 10.0 if done else -0.1, done, False, {}

class PotentialShaping(gym.Wrapper):
    def __init__(self, env, phi, gamma):
        super().__init__(env); self.phi, self.gamma = phi, gamma
    def reset(self, **kw):
        self.last, info = self.env.reset(**kw); return self.last, info
    def step(self, a):
        s2, r, term, trunc, info = self.env.step(a)
        f = self.gamma * (0.0 if term else self.phi(s2)) - self.phi(self.last)   # terminal potential is 0
        self.last = s2
        return s2, r + f, term, trunc, info

def episode_return(env, gamma):
    s, _ = env.reset(seed=0); G, t, done = 0.0, 0, False
    while not done:
        s, r, done, _, _ = env.step(1); G += gamma ** t * r; t += 1
    return G

true_G = episode_return(Corridor(), GAMMA)
shaped_G = episode_return(PotentialShaping(Corridor(), lambda s: -(GOAL - s), GAMMA), GAMMA)
print(f"true return {true_G:.4f}  shaped return {shaped_G:.4f}  difference {shaped_G - true_G:.4f}")
assert np.isclose(true_G, 7.7741, atol=1e-3) and np.isclose(shaped_G - true_G, 5.0)
print("library track passed")

## Verification

- Roll out the optimal policy for the reward you are about to train on and read the trace: a loop or an unexpected stop is visible immediately in a small environment.
- Check the policy for several discounts around the one you train with; a threshold close to your $\gamma$ is a warning.
- Compare the training return with an independent success metric. A rising return with falling success is the signature of exploitation.
- For shaping, verify numerically that the shaped return differs from the true return by $-\phi(s_0)$ along a terminating path and that the greedy policy is the same.
- Test every reward term in isolation with a "do nothing" and a "farm it" scripted policy.

## Failure modes

- **Repeatable bonuses.** A bonus of 2 per entry turns an intended 5-step task into an endless loop (value 18.5 versus 10.8).
- **Shaping that is not a potential difference.** Dropping $\gamma$ leaves a residual that pays $1/(1+\gamma)=0.513$ per loop in Exercise 5.
- **Evaluating on the training reward.** The hacked policy scores 16.5, the intended one 7.8.
- **Hidden side effects.** Anything not priced or measured can be sacrificed for reward.
- **Discount-dependent hacks.** The loophole appears only for $\gamma>0.905$ when the bonus is 2.
- **Reward scale drift.** Rescaling one term without the others changes the exchange rate between goals.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Approach | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Sparse success reward | cannot be farmed | hard exploration |
| Potential-based shaping | dense signal, optimal policy unchanged | needs a sensible potential |
| Hand-written dense reward | fast learning | loopholes and side effects |
| Constraints / penalty with threshold | explicit safety | needs measurement of the constraint |
| Learning from demonstrations or preferences | avoids specifying the reward | needs data, reward model may be wrong |
| Curriculum (next lessons) | keeps the true reward, changes the tasks | needs task design |

Use sparse plus potential shaping when you can state a distance-to-goal; add explicit constraints for safety; and always keep an evaluation separate from the training reward.

## Transfer and retention

For a real robot, write the reward and the evaluation together: for each term in the reward, add a line in the evaluation sheet that checks the corresponding clause of the intended behaviour, and add checks for the clauses you left out (contact forces, joint limits, time). Keep a log of exploits found, since each one is a missing clause. Retain: the loop-versus-finish threshold formula, the telescoping identity for potential shaping, and the rule "evaluate on success and side effects computed from the world, not on training return".
