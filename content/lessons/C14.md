## Why this matters

A humanoid reaching for a shelf, a legged robot carrying a payload or a mobile manipulator opening a door must do several things at once: stay balanced, keep feet from slipping, move the hand, avoid joint limits and not exceed motor torque. These demands compete for the same joints. Whole-body control (WBC) turns "do all of it" into an explicit, solvable problem: which tasks are hard constraints, which have priority over which, and what happens when they cannot all be met. It is the layer between motion planning (C13) and the joint controllers (C03).

## Intuition

Think of a juggler who is also carrying a tray. Not dropping the tray (balance) is non-negotiable; catching the ball (reaching) is attempted with whatever freedom is left; standing tall (posture) is a nicety. With a **strict hierarchy** the tray is never compromised for the ball; with a **weighted sum** both are compromised a little and the tray may wobble. A redundant robot has *spare* motion directions (the null space of the primary task) in which lower-priority tasks can be executed for free. When the spare directions run out, the low-priority task simply fails.

## Theory and derivation

### Whole-body dynamics with contacts

For a robot with generalised coordinates $q$ (including the floating base),
$$M(q)\ddot q+h(q,\dot q)=S^\top\tau+J_c(q)^\top\lambda,$$
where $h$ collects Coriolis and gravity terms, $S$ selects the actuated coordinates (the base has no motor), $J_c$ is the stacked contact Jacobian and $\lambda$ the contact forces. If a foot does not move, its acceleration is zero:
$$J_c\ddot q+\dot J_c\dot q=0.$$
Contact forces must be admissible: normal force $\lambda_n\ge0$ (the ground only pushes), tangential force inside the friction cone $|\lambda_t|\le\mu\lambda_n$ (the planar version), and for a flat foot the centre of pressure (CoP) must stay inside the foot. For a quasi-static pose the CoP lies under the centre of mass, so staying balanced means keeping the COM projection inside the support region.

### Tasks

A task is a desired acceleration of some quantity $y(q)$, with $\ddot y=J\ddot q+\dot J\dot q$. A PD law supplies $\ddot y_{des}=\ddot y_{ref}+K_d(\dot y_{ref}-\dot y)+K_p(y_{ref}-y)$. At velocity level this reduces to $J\,\dot q=\dot y_{des}$, which is what the lab uses.

### Strict priority by null-space projection

With the primary task $J_1\dot q=e_1$, all solutions are $\dot q=J_1^+e_1+N_1z$ with $N_1=I-J_1^+J_1$, which projects onto directions that do not change task 1 ($J_1N_1=0$). The secondary task chooses $z$ best satisfying $J_2\dot q=e_2$:
$$\dot q=J_1^+e_1+(J_2N_1)^+\big(e_2-J_2J_1^+e_1\big).$$
Properties: the primary task is met exactly (when feasible), and the secondary task is met only as far as the null space allows. Near singularities $J_2N_1\to0$, so use a damped pseudo-inverse $J^\top/(JJ^\top+\lambda^2)$.

### Weighted combination

Stack the tasks and minimise $w_1\|J_1\dot q-e_1\|^2+w_2\|J_2\dot q-e_2\|^2$. When the tasks are compatible both are met; when they conflict the error is shared according to the weights. Weights are easy to tune but give no guarantee for the important task, and large weights ill-condition the problem.

### The QP form

A modern WBC solves, every control cycle, a quadratic program in $(\ddot q,\tau,\lambda)$: minimise weighted task errors subject to the dynamics, the contact constraint, friction cones and torque limits (linear inequalities). Strict priorities are obtained by solving a sequence of QPs, each restricted to the solutions that keep the higher priorities optimal (lexicographic QP), or approximately by large weight ratios. Torque limits and friction cones belong in the constraints, not in the tasks.

### Limited actuation

With $\tau$ limited, holding a pose may be impossible. For the arm of the lab ($l_1=l_2=1$ m, $m_1=m_2=1$ kg) the shoulder torque to hold the straight horizontal arm is $g(1.5+0.5)=19.62$ N$\,$m; with a limit of 15 N$\,$m the arm must be at least $\arccos(15/19.62)=0.70$ rad (40 degrees) above horizontal. Saturating torques after the fact (scaling) changes the commanded accelerations in an uncontrolled way; limits must enter the optimisation.

## Worked example

Planar two-link arm, $q=(q_1,q_2)$, tasks: (1) centre-of-mass $x$ (balance proxy), (2) hand height. At $q_0=(1.2,-0.8)$ the task Jacobians are $J_1=[-0.7964,\ -0.0974]$ and $J_2=[1.2834,\ 0.9211]$.

1. $J_1J_1^\top=0.6342+0.0095=0.6437$; $J_1^+=J_1^\top/0.6437=(-1.2372,\ -0.1513)^\top$.
2. $N_1=I-J_1^+J_1$; check $J_1N_1=(0,0)$ to rounding (the lab prints $10^{-12}$).
3. Want $e_1=0.01$ (move the COM +1 cm) and $e_2=0.05$ (raise the hand 5 cm). Primary step: $\dot q_1=J_1^+e_1=(-0.01237,\ -0.00151)$; it gives $J_2\dot q_1=-0.01587-0.00139=-0.01727$ on the hand.
4. Residual for the secondary task: $0.05-(-0.01727)=0.06727$. The remaining direction $J_2N_1$ lets the secondary task absorb it exactly (two joints, two independent scalar tasks): total step $\dot q=(-0.02314,\ 0.08652)$, which satisfies $J_1\dot q=0.01$ and $J_2\dot q=0.05$.
5. Conflict case: push the hand to 1.8 m while holding the COM at $x=0.6$. At that COM the maximum feasible height is 1.668 m (the null space is exhausted at the boundary). Strict priority converges to COM $=0.6$ exactly and hand $=1.668$; equal-weight least squares leaves a COM error of 0.004 m and a hand height of 1.656.

## Reference implementation

The null-space projection at the worked-example Jacobians, with the two checks $J_1N_1=0$ and both tasks achieved.

```python
# Null-space priority on a 2-joint arm with two scalar tasks.
J1 = [-0.7964, -0.0974]          # primary task Jacobian (centre of mass x)
J2 = [1.2834, 0.9211]            # secondary task Jacobian (hand height)
n2 = sum(a * a for a in J1)
p1 = [a / n2 for a in J1]        # J1+  (1x2 pseudo-inverse)
N1 = [[(1.0 if i == j else 0.0) - p1[i] * J1[j] for j in range(2)] for i in range(2)]
print("J1 N1 =", [round(sum(J1[i] * N1[i][j] for i in range(2)), 12) for j in range(2)])
assert all(abs(sum(J1[i] * N1[i][j] for i in range(2))) < 1e-12 for j in range(2))   # null-space projector

e1, e2 = 0.01, 0.05
dq1 = [a * e1 for a in p1]                                   # primary step
J2N = [sum(J2[i] * N1[i][j] for i in range(2)) for j in range(2)]
resid = e2 - sum(J2[i] * dq1[i] for i in range(2))
m2 = sum(a * a for a in J2N)
dq = [dq1[i] + J2N[i] / m2 * resid for i in range(2)]
print("dq =", [round(x, 5) for x in dq])
print("primary achieved:", round(sum(J1[i] * dq[i] for i in range(2)), 8), "(wanted 0.01)")
print("secondary achieved:", round(sum(J2[i] * dq[i] for i in range(2)), 8), "(wanted 0.05)")
assert abs(sum(J1[i] * dq[i] for i in range(2)) - e1) < 1e-9
assert abs(sum(J2[i] * dq[i] for i in range(2)) - e2) < 1e-9   # one free null-space direction: both met here
```

### Starter code

```python
import math
G = 9.81   # l = (1, 1), m = (1, 1) everywhere in this lab

def com_x(q):
    """Centre-of-mass x of a planar 2-link arm hanging from the origin (angles from the x axis)."""
    return (1.5 * math.cos(q[0]) + 0.5 * math.cos(q[0] + q[1])) / 2.0

def jac_com_x(q):
    """1x2 Jacobian of com_x as a list [d/dq1, d/dq2]."""
    s1, s12 = math.sin(q[0]), math.sin(q[0] + q[1])
    return [(-1.5 * s1 - 0.5 * s12) / 2.0, -0.5 * s12 / 2.0]

def hand_y(q):
    return math.sin(q[0]) + math.sin(q[0] + q[1])

def jac_hand_y(q):
    return [math.cos(q[0]) + math.cos(q[0] + q[1]), math.cos(q[0] + q[1])]

def pinv_row(J, damping=1e-3):
    """Damped pseudo-inverse of a 1 x n row J (list of n floats): the n-vector J'/(J J' + damping).
    (With damping > 0 it is finite even when J is zero: the result is then the zero vector.)"""
    raise NotImplementedError

def weighted_dq(J1, e1, J2, e2, w1=1.0, w2=1.0):
    """Joint step for TWO scalar tasks combined by weighted least squares (2 joints):
    minimise w1*(J1 dq - e1)^2 + w2*(J2 dq - e2)^2 + 1e-9*|dq|^2. Solve the 2x2 normal equations
    (w1 J1'J1 + w2 J2'J2 + 1e-9 I) dq = w1 J1' e1 + w2 J2' e2. Return [dq1, dq2]."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def prioritized_dq(J1, e1, J2, e2):
    """Strict priority: dq = J1+ e1 + (J2 N1)+ (e2 - J2 J1+ e1), N1 = I - J1+ J1 (2x2).
    J1, J2 are 1x2 rows; use pinv_row(J1, 1e-9) for the primary and pinv_row(J2 N1, 1e-3) for the secondary (damped, because J2 N1 can vanish).
    Return [dq1, dq2]."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def run(q0, com_goal, y_goal, iters, k, mode="priority"):
    """Resolved-rate loop: at each step e1 = k*(com_goal - com_x(q)), e2 = k*(y_goal - hand_y(q)),
    dq = prioritized_dq(...) if mode == 'priority' else weighted_dq(...), q += dq.
    Return the final q as a list [q1, q2]."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def gravity_torque(q):
    """Joint torques [tau1, tau2] that hold the arm still (l = m = 1, g = G):
    tau1 = G*(1.5*cos(q1) + 0.5*cos(q1+q2)); tau2 = G*0.5*cos(q1+q2)."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def friction_ok(ft, fn, mu):
    """True iff the contact force is admissible: fn >= 0 and |ft| <= mu*fn."""
    raise NotImplementedError
```

import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import lsq_linear, minimize

q0 = np.array([1.2, -0.8])
J1 = np.array(jac_com_x(q0)); J2 = np.array(jac_hand_y(q0))

# 1. null-space priority with numpy.linalg.pinv vs your prioritized_dq
def prio_np(J1, e1, J2, e2):
    J1 = J1.reshape(1, -1); J2 = J2.reshape(1, -1)
    P1 = np.linalg.pinv(J1); N1 = np.eye(2) - P1 @ J1
    return (P1 * e1).ravel() + (np.linalg.pinv(J2 @ N1) @ (np.array([[e2]]) - J2 @ P1 * e1)).ravel()
dq_np = prio_np(J1, 0.01, J2, 0.05)
print("numpy dq", dq_np.round(5), " lab dq", np.round(prioritized_dq(list(J1), 0.01, list(J2), 0.05), 5))
assert np.allclose(dq_np, prioritized_dq(list(J1), 0.01, list(J2), 0.05), atol=1e-3)
assert abs(J1 @ dq_np - 0.01) < 1e-9 and abs(J2 @ dq_np - 0.05) < 1e-9

# 2. compatible tasks: the 2x2 stacked system has one exact solution; both methods must find it
Jst = np.vstack([J1, J2])
dq_exact = np.linalg.solve(Jst, [0.01, 0.05])
assert np.allclose(dq_exact, weighted_dq(list(J1), 0.01, list(J2), 0.05), atol=1e-6)
assert np.allclose(dq_exact, dq_np, atol=1e-3)
print("stacked solve:", dq_exact.round(5), " cond(J) = %.1f" % np.linalg.cond(Jst))
# near the conflict limit the stacked matrix becomes singular: watch the condition number blow up
for y in (1.2, 1.6, 1.66, 1.668):
    qy = np.array(run(q0, 0.6, y, 3000, 0.02, "priority"))
    print("hand goal %.3f: cond(J) = %.1f" % (y, np.linalg.cond(np.vstack([jac_com_x(qy), jac_hand_y(qy)]))))

# 3. torque limit as a hard constraint: reachable hand heights for the straight arm with |tau1| <= 15
heights = []
for el in np.linspace(0, np.pi / 2, 2001):
    if abs(gravity_torque([el, 0.0])[0]) <= 15.0: heights.append(2 * np.sin(el))
print("with |tau1|<=15 the straight arm reaches heights from %.3f m and a horizontal reach of %.3f m" %
      (min(heights), 2 * np.cos(np.arccos(15 / 19.62))))

# 4. friction cone as a linear constraint: smallest normal force that supports a tangential demand
from scipy.optimize import linprog
r = linprog([1.0], A_ub=[[-0.4]], b_ub=[-90.0], bounds=[(0, None)])    # 0.4*fn >= 90

Then check the hierarchy on a compatible case (both tasks met) and a conflict case (primary met, secondary at its feasible limit). For dynamics, verify the contact conditions on a one-DOF example before a full robot: normal force non-negative, friction ratio below $\mu$, torques within limits. ## Failure modes

- **Swapped priority** (exercise 6): the primary task is projected away and never executes.
- **Singularities:** $J_2N_1$ or $J_1$ near zero gives huge steps without damping.
- **Large gains with strict priority:** a big step along the null space changes $J_1$ and leaks error into the primary task; use small steps or iterate.
- **Clipping torques after the fact:** the achieved accelerations differ from the commanded ones and balance can be lost.
- **Ignoring friction and unilateral contact:** a feasible-looking command pulls on the ground or slips.
- **Wrong weights:** a "high" weight is not a guarantee; strict priority needs projectors or lexicographic solves.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

Weighted QPs are simple and robust to infeasibility but compromise important tasks. Lexicographic or null-space hierarchies protect the primary task but are more expensive and can be discontinuous when ranks change. Operational-space control (Khatib) expresses tasks with the inertia-weighted pseudo-inverse. MPC over a centroidal model (C11) plans balance and contact forces over a horizon, with WBC tracking the result at the whole-body level. Learned policies can replace parts of the stack but typically keep a WBC or torque-limit layer for safety.

The same idea (primary requirement first, extras in the null space) appears in redundancy resolution for manipulators, in grasping with multiple contacts, and in safety filters for learned policies. ## Exercise answers

1. A strict priority guarantees the primary task is optimised first and the secondary uses only the remaining freedom: the primary error is unaffected by the secondary. A heavy weight merely makes the primary error small relative to the secondary; some leak remains and very heavy weights make the problem ill-conditioned. They coincide when the tasks are compatible (both met), or in the limit of infinite weight ratio; they differ when tasks conflict.
2. CoP = COM when static. $(40\cdot0.02+5\cdot0.6)/45=3.8/45=0.0844$ m: inside the $\pm0.10$ m foot. With the arm at 0.9 m: $(0.8+4.5)/45=0.1178$ m: outside, so the robot tips (it must lean back, step, or use the other hand).
3. $|f_t|\le\mu f_n=0.4\cdot200=80$ N. A request of 90 N would slip. Options: increase the normal force (lower the COM or shift weight), reduce the tangential demand (slower acceleration), or constrain the QP so that requests stay inside the cone.
4. $J_1^+=(1,0)^\top$ (damping ignored), $e_1$-step $(0.1,0)$; $N_1=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$, $J_2N_1=[0,1]$. Residual $0.2-0.5\cdot0.1=0.15$; $\dot q=(0.1,\ 0.15)$. Check: $J_1\dot q=0.1$, $J_2\dot q=0.05+0.15=0.2$. Both achieved.
5. $J_2N_1=[0,0]$, so the secondary task has no free direction: strict priority gives $\dot q=(0.1,0)$; task 1 achieved (0.1), task 2 achieves $+0.1$ against $-0.1$ (error 0.2). Equal-weight least squares minimises $(\dot q_1-0.1)^2+(\dot q_1+0.1)^2$: $\dot q=(0,0)$, with both errors 0.1: it sacrifices the primary.
6. $N_1J_1^+=(I-J_1^+J_1)J_1^+=J_1^+-J_1^+J_1J_1^+=0$, so the primary command is annihilated: task 1 gets nothing and task 2 gets full priority (the roles are reversed). Correct: `dq = pinv(J1)@e1 + N1 @ pinv(J2@N1) @ (e2 - J2@pinv(J1)@e1)`.
7. $M\ddot z+h=\lambda_n$ with $h=Mg=98.1$ N (no $S^\top\tau$ term in $z$). Contact constraint $\ddot z=0$: $\lambda_n=98.1$ N, equal to the weight. For general $\ddot z$ (if the contact is released), $\lambda_n=M\ddot z+98.1\ge0$ requires $\ddot z\ge-9.81$ m/s$^2$: the ground cannot pull, so no body can be pushed downward faster than gravity by the ground alone; the robot must use its limbs' inertia (swing) or other contacts.
8. Torque $\tau_1=19.62\cos q_1\le15$ gives $q_1\ge0.7005$ rad, so the straight arm's maximum horizontal reach is $2\cos0.7005=1.529$ m (a fully horizontal 2 m reach would need 19.62 N$\,$m). In a WBC put $-\tau_{\max}\le\tau\le\tau_{\max}$ as inequality constraints in the QP (with $\tau=M\ddot q+h-J_c^\top\lambda$ as an equality), so the optimiser trades accelerations to respect them; do not clip afterwards.
9. A good answer: hard constraints: dynamics, non-slip foot contacts, friction cones, unilateral forces, torque and joint limits. Priorities: (1) COM/CoP inside the support, (2) hand pose to the box (or the box approach), (3) torso/head posture, (4) joint-limit-avoidance posture. Lexicographic QP if the hierarchy must be guaranteed and compute allows; weights with a large ratio if a single QP is needed. Log task errors per level, CoP margin, friction ratio, torque margins, QP slack and solver status; detect failure by a growing primary error or a CoP at the support boundary.
10. COM goal 0.6: for hand 1.2 and 1.5 both methods reach both goals (errors 0). For 1.8: priority COM error 0.000, hand error $-0.132$ (hand stops at 1.668); weights COM error 0.004, hand error $-0.144$. Priority protects the balance task and also gets nearer to the feasible limit than the compromise; weights could be preferable if neither task is critical and a smooth trade is wanted, or when infeasibility makes strict hierarchy discontinuous.
