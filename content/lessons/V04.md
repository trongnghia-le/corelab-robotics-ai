## Why this matters

A camera datasheet gives a nominal focal length; the real one differs by a percent or two, the principal point is off centre, and cheap or wide-angle lenses bend straight lines. If you measure distance from pixel size, steer from a bearing angle or triangulate in stereo, those errors enter every number. Calibration is the procedure that measures them. The risk is the opposite one: a calibration that looks perfect on the images it was fitted to and is wrong where the data did not reach, usually near the image corners. This lesson builds on the pinhole model (V03) and least-squares optimisation (M10). A two-line least-squares fit of ideal intrinsics is also covered in the optional pinhole-calibration bridge; here we add distortion and the discipline of held-out validation.

## Intuition

Calibration is curve fitting for a camera. You know where some 3-D points are (corners of a printed checkerboard) and you measure where they appear in the image. You adjust parameters until the model's predicted pixels match the measured ones. The mismatch for one point is its **reprojection error**, a distance in pixels.

Two traps follow from this. First, if you add enough parameters the fit always looks good on its own data, so the error on fitted points is optimistic. Second, if all your points are near the image centre, the distortion term is invisible there, because distortion grows with distance from the centre. A model without it fits the centre perfectly and fails at the edge.

![A distorted grid of points with the residual vectors to the ideal pinhole grid growing toward the image corners, and a bar chart of fit RMS versus held-out RMS](/lesson-figures/V04.svg)

*Radial distortion displaces points more at larger radius; a fit on central points hides it, a held-out set at the edges reveals it.*

## Theory and derivation

### Radial distortion

Work in normalised coordinates $x=X/Z$, $y=Y/Z$ with $r^2=x^2+y^2$. The common radial model scales the point:
$$x_d=x\,(1+k_1r^2+k_2r^4),\qquad y_d=y\,(1+k_1r^2+k_2r^4),$$
then $u=f_xx_d+c_x$, $v=f_yy_d+c_y$. Negative $k_1$ gives barrel distortion (straight lines bow outwards, typical of wide-angle lenses), positive gives pincushion. Displacement in pixels is about $f\,r\,|k_1|\,r^2$ near the edge, so it grows with the cube of the radius. Tangential distortion terms exist but are usually small. Undistortion has no closed form; iterate $x\leftarrow x_d/(1+k_1r^2+k_2r^4)$ with $r$ computed from the current estimate, starting at $x_d$.

### Reprojection error

For correspondence $i$ with 3-D point $P_i$ and measured pixel $m_i$, the residual is $e_i=\pi(P_i;\theta)-m_i\in\mathbb R^2$, where $\theta$ holds the parameters. The usual summary is the root mean square
$$\mathrm{RMS}=\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_i\|e_i\|^2}$$
(not the mean of $\|e_i\|$, which hides outliers). It is in pixels. Corner detectors are accurate to roughly $0.1$ to $0.3$ px, so an RMS of about $0.2$ to $0.5$ px on a good setup is realistic; much larger values mean a wrong model, wrong correspondences, motion blur, or a bad target.

### Calibration as least squares

Calibration minimises $\sum_i\|e_i\|^2$ over $\theta$. With a planar checkerboard and unknown board poses (Zhang's method), each view contributes a homography with eight degrees of freedom, and each view gives two constraints on the intrinsics; with zero skew four intrinsics need at least two views, and in practice you use 10 to 20 varied views. The final step is a nonlinear refinement (Levenberg-Marquardt) of intrinsics, distortion and every view's pose together.

When the 3-D points are known in the camera frame, the model is *linear* in the right unknowns:
$$u=c_x+f_x\,x+(f_xk_1)\,x r^2,$$
so $(f_x,c_x,f_xk_1)$ follow from ordinary least squares with design-matrix rows $[x,\,1,\,xr^2]$, and likewise for $v$. The normal equations $A^TAw=A^Tb$ give the solution; $k_1$ is the ratio of the third and first components. This toy is exact when the data are noise free and shows every statistical effect that matters.

### Parameters versus residuals

A $7\times10$ board seen in 12 views has $70$ corners per view and $2\cdot70\cdot12=1680$ scalar residuals. Parameters: 4 intrinsics, 2 distortion coefficients and 6 pose values per view, $4+2+72=78$. The ratio of data to parameters (about 21) is what keeps the fit from memorising noise.

### Fit error versus held-out error

Fit error is measured on the correspondences used by the optimiser and never exceeds the error of a model with fewer parameters fitted on the same data (nested models). **Held-out error** is measured on correspondences the fit never saw, ideally whole views, split by a rule fixed before fitting. If held-out RMS is much larger, the model does not describe those regions. Reusing a held-out point in the fit (leakage) makes its number meaningless.

### Coverage and degeneracy

Parameters are identified only if the data excite them. Distortion needs points at large radius; focal length needs target tilts (a board always parallel to the image plane cannot separate focal length from distance); the principal point needs views with varied depth and tilt. Cover the whole image, including corners, with tilts up to roughly $\pm45^\circ$.

## Worked example

**1. Distortion of one point.** $f=500$, $c=(320,240)$, $k_1=-0.25$, $k_2=0$, point $(x,y)=(0.4,0.3)$. $r^2=0.25$, factor $1-0.25\cdot0.25=0.9375$. $x_d=0.375$, $y_d=0.28125$. Pixels: $u=500\cdot0.375+320=507.5$, $v=500\cdot0.28125+240=380.625$; the ideal pinhole pixels are $(520,390)$. The shift is $(-12.5,-9.375)$, length $15.625$ px $=500\cdot0.5\cdot0.0625$.

**2. RMS.** Residuals in px: $(0.3,-0.4)$, $(0.5,0.0)$, $(-0.2,0.2)$, $(0,-0.6)$. Squared lengths: $0.25,\;0.25,\;0.08,\;0.36$; sum $0.94$; mean $0.235$; RMS $=0.485$ px. The mean of the lengths is $(0.5+0.5+0.283+0.6)/4=0.471$, smaller, because it downweights the largest residual.

**3. Fit versus held-out.** Synthetic camera $f_x=520$, $f_y=515$, $c=(322,238)$, $k_1=-0.25$. Points on a grid $X=0.2i$ ($i=-4..4$), $Y=0.2j$ ($j=-3..3$), $Z=2$ (63 points). Fit on the 13 points with $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\le0.2$ using a model *without* $k_1$: it gives $f_x=516.1$, $f_y=511.1$ and a training RMS of $0.23$ px, apparently excellent. On the 50 remaining points (radius above 0.2) the same parameters give RMS $6.43$ px. Fitting with $k_1$ on the same 13 points recovers all five parameters exactly (noise free) and the held-out RMS is $\approx0$. With Gaussian noise of $0.3$ px per axis and all 63 points, the fit gives $k_1=-0.248$ and RMS $0.39$ px, about $0.3\sqrt2\sqrt{1-3/63}\approx0.41$ as theory predicts for noise level 0.3 px.

## Reference implementation

Radial distortion, its inverse by fixed-point iteration, the projection and RMS.

```python
import math

def distort(x, y, k1, k2=0.0):
    r2 = x * x + y * y
    d = 1 + k1 * r2 + k2 * r2 * r2
    return x * d, y * d

def undistort(xd, yd, k1, k2=0.0, iters=50):
    x, y = xd, yd
    for _ in range(iters):
        r2 = x * x + y * y
        d = 1 + k1 * r2 + k2 * r2 * r2
        x, y = xd / d, yd / d
    return x, y

def project(p, fx, fy, cx, cy, k1, k2=0.0):
    X, Y, Z = p
    xd, yd = distort(X / Z, Y / Z, k1, k2)
    return fx * xd + cx, fy * yd + cy

def rms(residuals):
    return math.sqrt(sum(du * du + dv * dv for du, dv in residuals) / len(residuals))

u, v = project((0.8, 0.6, 2.0), 500, 500, 320, 240, -0.25)
assert abs(u - 507.5) < 1e-9 and abs(v - 380.625) < 1e-9
xd, yd = distort(0.4, 0.3, -0.25)
x, y = undistort(xd, yd, -0.25)
assert abs(x - 0.4) < 1e-9 and abs(y - 0.3) < 1e-9
assert abs(rms([(0.3, -0.4), (0.5, 0.0), (-0.2, 0.2), (0.0, -0.6)]) - 0.484768) < 1e-5
assert abs(rms([(3, 4), (0, 0)]) - 3.5355339) < 1e-6
print("reference checks passed")
```

### Library track

Redo the calibration with OpenCV on synthetic data (`pip install numpy opencv-python-headless`). The script builds a 7x10 board, projects it through a known camera with distortion (`k1=-0.25`, `k2=0.08`) and 0.2 px noise, fits 12 views with `cv2.calibrateCamera`, and scores 4 held-out views that were split off before fitting. It then repeats the fit with the distortion terms fixed to zero, as in the lab.

```python
import numpy as np, cv2

rng = np.random.default_rng(3)
W, H = 1280, 720
K_true = np.array([[800.0, 0, 642.0], [0, 795.0, 358.0], [0, 0, 1]])
dist_true = np.array([-0.25, 0.08, 0, 0, 0])           # k1, k2, p1, p2, k3
board = np.array([[i * 0.04, j * 0.04, 0.0] for j in range(7) for i in range(10)], np.float32)
board -= board.mean(axis=0)                              # 7 x 10 corners, 4 cm squares

def random_view():
    rvec = rng.uniform(-0.7, 0.7, 3)                     # tilts up to about 40 degrees
    tvec = np.array([rng.uniform(-0.25, 0.25), rng.uniform(-0.15, 0.15), rng.uniform(0.7, 1.3)])
    return rvec, tvec

def observe(rvec, tvec, sigma=0.2):
    px, _ = cv2.projectPoints(board, rvec, tvec, K_true, dist_true)
    return (px.reshape(-1, 2) + rng.normal(0, sigma, (len(board), 2))).astype(np.float32)

views = []
while len(views) < 16:
    rv, tv = random_view()
    px = observe(rv, tv)
    if (px[:, 0].min() > 0 and px[:, 0].max() < W and px[:, 1].min() > 0 and px[:, 1].max() < H):
        views.append((rv, tv, px))
fit, held = views[:12], views[12:]                       # split BEFORE fitting

obj = [board] * len(fit)
img = [v[2] for v in fit]
flags = cv2.CALIB_FIX_K3 | cv2.CALIB_ZERO_TANGENT_DIST
rms_fit, K, dist, _, _ = cv2.calibrateCamera(obj, img, (W, H), None, None, flags=flags)
print("fit RMS %.3f px (expected about 0.2*sqrt(2)=0.28)" % rms_fit)
print("fx, fy, cx, cy:", np.round([K[0, 0], K[1, 1], K[0, 2], K[1, 2]], 1))
print("k1, k2:", np.round(dist.ravel()[:2], 3))

def rms_on(px, K, dist):
    ok, rv, tv = cv2.solvePnP(board, px, K, dist)        # pose of this view under the fitted model
    proj, _ = cv2.projectPoints(board, rv, tv, K, dist)
    return np.sqrt(np.mean(np.sum((proj.reshape(-1, 2) - px) ** 2, axis=1)))

held_rms = np.sqrt(np.mean([rms_on(v[2], K, dist) ** 2 for v in held]))
print("held-out RMS %.3f px" % held_rms)
assert abs(K[0, 0] - 800) < 8 and abs(dist.ravel()[0] + 0.25) < 0.03
assert rms_fit < 0.4 and held_rms < 0.5

# the same data without any distortion terms: the error shows up, and more at the held-out corners
flags0 = cv2.CALIB_FIX_K1 | cv2.CALIB_FIX_K2 | cv2.CALIB_FIX_K3 | cv2.CALIB_ZERO_TANGENT_DIST
rms0, K0, dist0, _, _ = cv2.calibrateCamera(obj, img, (W, H), None, None, flags=flags0)
held0 = np.sqrt(np.mean([rms_on(v[2], K0, dist0) ** 2 for v in held]))
print("no-distortion model: fit RMS %.3f, held-out RMS %.3f" % (rms0, held0))
assert rms0 > rms_fit and held0 > held_rms
print("library track checks passed")
```

Expected (values vary slightly with the seed): fit RMS about 0.27 px (close to $0.2\sqrt2$), held-out RMS about 0.28 px, `fx` within about 1% of 800 and `k1` near $-0.25$; without distortion terms both RMS values rise (about 1.1 and 1.6 px), and the held-out value is the larger one. ## Verification

Run the pipeline on synthetic data with known parameters: noise-free data must recover them to numerical precision, and noise of $\sigma$ per axis should give RMS about $\sigma\sqrt2$. Check held-out error on views (not points) that were excluded before fitting. Look at the residuals, not only the RMS: plot the residual vectors over the image, and if they point radially or grow at the edge, the model is missing something. Compare estimated focal length with the datasheet value (a few percent) and with a second, independent session. Check the principal point is near the centre (within a few percent of the width). Undistort a photo of a straight edge and verify the edge is straight.

## Failure modes

- **Optimistic fit error.** Reporting the training RMS (0.23 px) instead of the held-out RMS (6.43 px).
- **Poor coverage.** All points near the centre: $k_1$ undetermined; the model extrapolates badly at corners.
- **Frontal-only views.** Focal length and distance trade off; the optimiser returns a plausible but wrong $f$ (for example 650 instead of 540).
- **Too many parameters.** Adding $k_3$, tangential and skew terms with few views fits noise and gives a different result on a second session.
- **Mean instead of RMS.** Hides outliers: `[(3,4),(0,0)]` gives 2.5 instead of 3.54.
- **Blur and rolling shutter.** Corner localisation error is not Gaussian; the optimiser is confidently wrong.
- **Re-using a calibration after refocus or zoom.** Focus changes the focal length slightly; recalibrate after changing lens settings.
- **Leakage.** Using held-out views in the final refinement.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Approach | Needs | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planar checkerboard (Zhang) | Printed board, 10-20 views | Cheap, standard | Needs varied tilts, flat board |
| 3-D target / known points | Surveyed rig | Linear start, fewer views | Costly to build |
| Self-calibration | Many natural views | No target | Less accurate, needs good motion |
| Fisheye model | Wide-angle lens | Handles above $120^\circ$ | Different parameters, not a drop-in |
| Factory calibration | None | Convenient | Drifts, no validation by you |

Use the simplest distortion model that removes systematic residuals ($k_1$, then $k_2$). Recalibrate when the lens or mount changes and keep the held-out test in your regression suite.

7. $(x,y)=(0.4,0.3)$, $r^2=0.25$, factor $0.9375$: $u=500\cdot0.375+320=507.5$, $v=500\cdot0.28125+240=380.625$. The ideal pixel is $(520,390)$; the distance is $\sqrt{12.5^2+9.375^2}=15.625$ px.
8. $u_i=c_x+f_x\,x_i+(f_xk_1)\,x_ir_i^2$ is a linear combination of known quantities with unknowns $(f_x,c_x,f_xk_1)$. Design rows $[x,1,xr^2]$: $[-0.2,1,-0.008]$, $[0,1,0]$, $[0.4,1,0.064]$. Normal equations $A^TAw=A^Tb$, a $3\times3$ system. Three unknowns need three independent equations, so at least three points whose rows $[x,1,xr^2]$ are linearly independent. If all $x$ are equal, or all non-central points have the same $r$ (then $xr^2$ is proportional to $x$), $A^TA$ is singular.
9. A good plan: 15-25 images, board tilted $\pm30$-$45^\circ$ about both axes, several distances, corners and edges covered, sharp images (no motion blur), fixed focus. Hold out about 20% of the images by a fixed rule (every fifth) *before* fitting. Report intrinsics with uncertainty, training and held-out RMS, residual plots, and the number of images. Thresholds: held-out RMS within about 1.5x the training RMS and below 0.5-1 px, parameters stable within a few tenths of a percent across two sessions; otherwise add views at the poorly covered regions.
10. Typical result: with limit 0.1 the model without $k_1$ has tiny training RMS but held-out RMS of many pixels; as the limit grows the gap shrinks because edge points enter the fit and the no-$k_1$ model's training RMS rises; the model with $k_1$ stays near 0 for every limit from 0.2 up in the noise-free case (and near $\sigma\sqrt2$ with noise). With limit 0.1 only 5 points remain and all the off-centre ones share one radius, so $xr^2\propto x$ and the fit with $k_1$ is singular: coverage, not the model, is the problem. Measured (no-$k_1$ model; train/held-out RMS): limit 0.1: 0.00/6.76; 0.2: 0.23/6.43; 0.3: 0.67/6.12; 0.5 (all points): 2.61/-. Explanation: distortion is invisible near the centre, so small training sets cannot identify it.
