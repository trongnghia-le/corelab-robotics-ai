## Why this matters

The Kalman filter (C07) needs linear models. Robots are not linear: a range-bearing sensor measures $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ and $\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$, a unicycle turns velocity into position through $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$, and a camera projects 3-D points through a division. Nonlinear estimators keep the Kalman machinery but replace the linear map by an approximation of how a Gaussian changes under it. Knowing how good or bad the approximation is decides whether your filter is consistent or silently overconfident.

## Intuition

Push a bell curve through a curved function. If the curve is almost straight over the width of the bell, the output is almost a bell curve whose centre is $g(\mu)$ and whose width is scaled by the slope. If the bell is wide compared with the curvature, the output is skewed and shifted. The **EKF** linearises $g$ at the mean (fast, one Jacobian, wrong when curvature matters). The **UKF** instead *samples* the bell with a few carefully chosen points, pushes each through the true $g$, and recomputes mean and variance from the results (no Jacobian, exact for quadratics, a better match for smooth curves). Neither can represent a belief with two separate bumps.

## Theory and derivation

### Moment propagation

For $x\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ and $y=g(x)$, the first two moments of $y$ are what a Gaussian filter keeps. A Taylor expansion at $\mu$ gives
$$\mathbb E[y]\approx g(\mu)+\tfrac12g''(\mu)\sigma^2,\qquad\operatorname{Var}[y]\approx g'(\mu)^2\sigma^2+\tfrac12g''(\mu)^2\sigma^4.$$
The EKF keeps only the first terms: mean $g(\mu)$ and variance $g'(\mu)^2\sigma^2$. Its mean error is the **curvature bias** $\tfrac12g''\sigma^2$.

### EKF

Prediction: $\hat x^-=f(\hat x)$, $P^-=FPF^\top+Q$ with $F=\partial f/\partial x$ at $\hat x$. Update with $z=h(x)+v$:
$$H=\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}\Big|_{\hat x^-},\quad S=HP^-H^\top+R,\quad K=P^-H^\top S^{-1},\quad\hat x=\hat x^-+K\big(z-h(\hat x^-)\big),\quad P=(I-KH)P^-(I-KH)^\top+KRK^\top.$$
The Jacobian is evaluated at the *current estimate*; if that is poor, so is $H$, and there is no recovery inside the step.

### Unscented transform

For $n$ dimensions choose $2n+1$ **sigma points**
$$x_0=\mu,\qquad x_{\pm i}=\mu\pm\sqrt{n+\kappa}\,\big(\sqrt\Sigma\big)_i$$
with weights $w_0=\tfrac\kappa{n+\kappa}$ and $w_{\pm i}=\tfrac1{2(n+\kappa)}$. These reproduce $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ exactly. Pass each through $g$ and set $\bar y=\sum w_ig(x_i)$ and $\Sigma_y=\sum w_i(g(x_i)-\bar y)^2$. For $n=1$, $\kappa=2$ is the Gaussian rule of thumb ($n+\kappa=3$). The transform is **exact for any quadratic** $g$ (the mean and variance of a quadratic depend only on the first four moments, which the points match). For the UKF update add the cross-covariance:
$$P_{xz}=\sum w_i(x_i-\mu)(h(x_i)-\bar z),\quad S=\sum w_i(h(x_i)-\bar z)^2+R,\quad K=P_{xz}/S,\quad\hat x=\mu+K(z-\bar z),\quad P=P^--KSK^\top.$$

### What neither can do

If $z=x^2$ and the prior mean is 0, the true posterior is *bimodal* (the sign of $x$ is lost). Both filters have $H=0$ (EKF) or $P_{xz}=0$ (UKF) at that mean, so they conclude the measurement carries no information. A particle filter or a mixture of Gaussians is needed. Also remember: the filters are *approximate*: they can be inconsistent (too small $P$) and must be checked with NIS (C07).

## Worked example

$g(x)=x^2$, $x\sim\mathcal N(1,\,0.25)$ ($\mu=1$, $\sigma=0.5$).

**Exact.** $\mathbb E[x^2]=\mu^2+\sigma^2=1.25$; $\operatorname{Var}=4\mu^2\sigma^2+2\sigma^4=1+0.125=1.125$.

**EKF.** $g'(1)=2$: mean $g(1)=1$, variance $4\cdot0.25=1.0$. Mean error $-0.25$ (the curvature bias $\tfrac12g''\sigma^2=\sigma^2$), variance error $-0.125$.

**UT ($\kappa=2$).**
1. $\sqrt{(1+2)\cdot0.25}=0.866$; sigma points $1,\ 1.866,\ 0.134$; weights $\tfrac23,\tfrac16,\tfrac16$.
2. Outputs: $1,\ 3.482,\ 0.0179$.
3. Mean: $\tfrac23\cdot1+\tfrac16(3.482+0.0179)=0.6667+0.5833=1.25$.
4. Variance: deviations $-0.25,\ 2.232,\ -1.232$; $\tfrac23(0.0625)+\tfrac16(4.982+1.518)=0.0417+1.0833=1.125$.
Exact, as promised.

**Measurement update.** Same prior, sensor $z=x^2+v$ with $R=0.04$ and reading $z=1.5$.
- EKF: $H=2$, $S=4\cdot0.25+0.04=1.04$, $K=0.25\cdot2/1.04=0.4808$; $\hat x=1+0.4808\cdot(1.5-1)=1.2404$; $P=(1-0.9615)^2\cdot0.25+0.4808^2\cdot0.04=0.0096$.
- UKF: $\bar z=1.25$, $S=1.125+0.04=1.165$, $P_{xz}=\tfrac16\cdot0.866\,[(3.482-1.25)+(1.25-0.0179)]=\tfrac16\cdot0.866\cdot3.464=0.5$; $K=0.5/1.165=0.4292$; $\hat x=1+0.4292\cdot0.25=1.1073$; $P=0.25-0.4292^2\cdot1.165=0.0354$.
- Exact (numerical) posterior mean and variance: $1.2105$ and $0.0072$. Here the EKF is closer than the UKF: a strong sensor shrinks the posterior into a small region where linearisation is good, while the UKF's sigma points are spread over the broad prior. No method dominates.

## Reference implementation

The worked example's propagation: exact moments, linearisation and three sigma points for $g(x)=x^2$.

```python
# Pushing N(mu, var) through x^2: exact moments vs linearisation vs three sigma points.
import math
mu, var = 1.0, 0.25
exact_mean, exact_var = mu * mu + var, 4 * mu * mu * var + 2 * var * var
ekf_mean, ekf_var = mu * mu, (2 * mu) ** 2 * var

kappa, n = 2.0, 1
s = math.sqrt((n + kappa) * var)
pts = [mu, mu + s, mu - s]
w = [kappa / (n + kappa), 0.5 / (n + kappa), 0.5 / (n + kappa)]
ys = [p * p for p in pts]
ut_mean = sum(a * b for a, b in zip(w, ys))
ut_var = sum(a * (b - ut_mean) ** 2 for a, b in zip(w, ys))

print(f"exact  mean {exact_mean:.4f} var {exact_var:.4f}")
print(f"EKF    mean {ekf_mean:.4f} var {ekf_var:.4f}")
print(f"UT     mean {ut_mean:.4f} var {ut_var:.4f}")
assert abs(ut_mean - exact_mean) < 1e-12 and abs(ut_var - exact_var) < 1e-12
assert abs(exact_mean - ekf_mean - var) < 1e-12            # the EKF mean bias is exactly var * g''/2 = var
assert ekf_var < exact_var
```

### Starter code

```python
import math, random

def linearized_moments(g, dg, mu, var):
    """First-order (EKF-style) propagation of N(mu, var) through g with derivative dg:
    mean = g(mu), variance = dg(mu)^2 * var."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def sigma_points(mu, var, kappa=2.0):
    """Scalar (n = 1) sigma points and weights: points mu, mu +/- sqrt((1+kappa)*var);
    weights kappa/(1+kappa) for the centre and 1/(2*(1+kappa)) for each of the other two.
    Return (points, weights) as lists in the order [centre, plus, minus]."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def unscented_moments(g, mu, var, kappa=2.0):
    """Unscented transform: mean = sum w_i g(x_i); variance = sum w_i (g(x_i) - mean)^2."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def monte_carlo_moments(g, mu, var, n, seed):
    """Sample mean and (population) variance of g(x), x ~ N(mu, var), using
    random.Random(seed).gauss(mu, sqrt(var)) n times."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def ekf_update(mu, var, z, h, dh, R):
    """Scalar EKF measurement update. H = dh(mu); S = H^2 var + R; K = var*H/S;
    mu' = mu + K (z - h(mu)); var' = (1 - K H)^2 var + K^2 R. Return (mu', var')."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def ukf_update(mu, var, z, h, R, kappa=2.0):
    """Scalar UKF update: sigma points x_i through h -> y_i; zbar = sum w y_i;
    Szz = sum w (y_i - zbar)^2 + R; Pxz = sum w (x_i - mu)(y_i - zbar);
    K = Pxz/Szz; mu' = mu + K (z - zbar); var' = var - K^2 Szz. Return (mu', var')."""
    raise NotImplementedError
```

# 1. quadratic map, mu = 1, var = 0.25: exact mean mu^2 + var = 1.25, exact var 4 mu^2 var + 2 var^2 = 1.125

```python
# ---- library track (needs: pip install numpy scipy) ----
import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import quad
from scipy.stats import norm

# 1. exact moments by numerical quadrature vs your unscented / linearised values
def exact_moments(g, mu, var):
    s = np.sqrt(var)
    m = quad(lambda x: g(x) * norm.pdf(x, mu, s), mu - 10 * s, mu + 10 * s)[0]
    v = quad(lambda x: (g(x) - m) ** 2 * norm.pdf(x, mu, s), mu - 10 * s, mu + 10 * s)[0]
    return m, v
for name, g, dg, mu, var in [("x^2", lambda x: x * x, lambda x: 2 * x, 1.0, 0.25),
                             ("exp", np.exp, np.exp, 0.0, 1.0),
                             ("sin", np.sin, np.cos, 1.0, 0.36)]:
    ex = exact_moments(g, mu, var)
    ut = unscented_moments(g, mu, var); ln = linearized_moments(g, dg, mu, var)
    print("%-4s exact (%.4f, %.4f)  UT (%.4f, %.4f)  EKF (%.4f, %.4f)" % ((name,) + ex + ut + ln))
assert np.allclose(exact_moments(lambda x: x * x, 1.0, 0.25), (1.25, 1.125), atol=1e-8)

# 2. exact scalar posterior on a grid for the range update, vs your EKF / UKF updates
x = np.linspace(-6, 10, 400001)
def grid_posterior(mu, var, z, h, R):
    w = norm.pdf(x, mu, np.sqrt(var)) * norm.pdf(z, h(x), np.sqrt(R))
    w /= w.sum(); m = (w * x).sum()
    return m, (w * (x - m) ** 2).sum()
h2 = lambda x: x * x
m, v = grid_posterior(1.0, 0.25, 1.5, h2, 0.04)
me, ve = ekf_update(1.0, 0.25, 1.5, h2, lambda t: 2 * t, 0.04)
mu_, vu_ = ukf_update(1.0, 0.25, 1.5, h2, 0.04)
print("grid posterior (%.4f, %.4f); EKF (%.4f, %.4f); UKF (%.4f, %.4f)" % (m, v, me, ve, mu_, vu_))
assert abs(m - 1.2105) < 1e-3 and abs(v - 0.0072) < 1e-3

# 3. the mu = 0, z = x^2 case: the posterior is bimodal with mean 0 and variance ~0.88
m, v = grid_posterior(0.0, 1.0, 1.0, h2, 0.1)
print("bimodal posterior: mean %.3f variance %.3f" % (m, v))
assert abs(m) < 1e-9 and abs(v - 0.882) < 0.01
```

## Verification

Verify propagation against quantities you can compute exactly (quadratic, exponential, sine have closed forms), then against a Monte Carlo with a fixed seed. Verify an update against a grid posterior for a scalar problem. For filters in operation, check consistency (NIS near 1) and look at innovations for sign and periodicity: unwrapped angles, wrong Jacobians and wrong sensor positions show up as structured innovations.

## Failure modes

- **Linearising at a poor estimate:** a bad $\hat x$ gives a bad $H$ and a worse update; initial errors can make the EKF diverge.
- **Overconfidence:** EKF covariance is too small near strong curvature (variance error $-2\sigma^4$ for $x^2$).
- **Angle wrap** (exercise 5): residuals of $2\pi$ produce huge corrections; normalise $y$ to $(-\pi,\pi]$.
- **Zero Jacobian** (exercise 6): the measurement carries no first-order information at that state.
- **UKF with a large $\kappa$ or a negative weight** can give negative variances; use the scaled transform for higher $n$.
- **Assuming Gaussian:** multimodal beliefs (kidnapped robot, symmetric maps) need particle or multi-hypothesis filters.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

EKF: cheapest, needs Jacobians (analytic or automatic differentiation). UKF: no Jacobians, better mean propagation for moderate nonlinearity, about $2n+1$ function evaluations. Iterated EKF re-linearises at the updated estimate and helps strong measurements. Particle filters handle multimodality at a cost exponential in dimension for naive versions. Smoothers and factor graphs (batch) relinearise whole trajectories and are standard in SLAM. Invariant EKF exploits geometry for attitude and pose.

1. With small $\sigma$, $g$ is nearly linear over the belief, so the tangent line describes the output Gaussian well. The mean error is $\tfrac12g''(\mu)\sigma^2$, so for convex $g$ ($g''>0$) the EKF mean is too low (biased below the true mean) and the variance is too small.
2. Exact: mean $4+0.04=4.04$; variance $4\cdot4\cdot0.04+2\cdot0.0016=0.6432$. EKF: mean 4, variance $(2\cdot2)^2\cdot0.04=0.64$.
3. $\hat z=\sqrt{16+9}=5$; $H=4/5=0.8$; $S=0.64+0.04=0.68$; $K=0.8/0.68=1.1765$; $\hat x=4+1.1765\cdot0.2=4.2353$; $P=(1-0.9412)^2\cdot1+1.1765^2\cdot0.04=0.0035+0.0554=0.0588$. Plausible: a 0.2 m excess range moves $x$ by about 0.2/0.8, shrunk by the prior; the numerical posterior is $(4.2269,\ 0.0571)$.
4. Sigma points $0,\pm\sqrt3$ with weights $\tfrac23,\tfrac16,\tfrac16$: $\tfrac23+\tfrac16(e^{1.732}+e^{-1.732})=0.6667+\tfrac16(5.652+0.1769)=1.638$. Exact $e^{0.5}=1.6487$; EKF $e^0=1$. The UT error is 0.011; the EKF error is 0.65.
5. The residual is not wrapped: when the true and predicted bearings straddle $\pm\pi$, $y$ can be about $2\pi$ instead of a small number. Fix: `y = (y + math.pi) % (2*math.pi) - math.pi` (or `atan2(sin y, cos y)`) before using it.
6. EKF: $H=2\mu=0$, so $K=0$ and the result is $(0,1)$. UKF: $P_{xz}=0$ by symmetry (the sigma points $\pm\sqrt3$ give equal $h$), so also $K=0$: $(0,1)$. The true posterior has two peaks near $x=\pm1$ (mean 0, variance about 0.88): a single Gaussian cannot represent it, and the filters report no information. A mixture or particle filter is required.
7. Points $\mu,\ \mu\pm\sqrt3\sigma$ with weights $\tfrac23,\tfrac16,\tfrac16$. Mean: $\tfrac23\mu^2+\tfrac16\cdot2(\mu^2+3\sigma^2)=\mu^2+\sigma^2$. Deviations from the mean: $-\sigma^2$, $2\sigma^2\pm2\sqrt3\mu\sigma$. Variance: $\tfrac23\sigma^4+\tfrac16[(2\sigma^2)^2\cdot2+(2\sqrt3\mu\sigma)^2\cdot2]=\tfrac23\sigma^4+\tfrac43\sigma^4+4\mu^2\sigma^2=2\sigma^4+4\mu^2\sigma^2$. Both exact.
8. EKF mean $\mu^2$ versus exact $\mu^2+\sigma^2$: bias $-\sigma^2$. EKF variance $4\mu^2\sigma^2$ versus exact $4\mu^2\sigma^2+2\sigma^4$: error $-2\sigma^4$. For $\mu=0.3,\sigma=0.5$: exact $4\cdot0.09\cdot0.25+2\cdot0.0625=0.09+0.125=0.215$, EKF $0.09$, relative error $-58\%$. Near the extremum ($\mu\to0$) the EKF variance goes to 0 although the true variance is $2\sigma^4$: strongly overconfident.
