## Why this matters

Kinematics and dynamics tell you what torque and speed a joint needs. Actuator selection tells you whether any real motor can deliver it. Many robot designs fail here, not in the software: a motor that is strong enough for a second but overheats in a minute, a gearbox ratio that gives plenty of torque but cannot reach the required speed, a leg joint so stiff in its gearbox that every impact is transmitted to the frame.

This lesson gives you the compact model that engineers use for first-pass sizing: a DC motor with a linear torque-speed line, a gearbox with a ratio and efficiency, and three limits (peak torque, continuous thermal torque, and the voltage limit set by back-EMF). With it you can decide in minutes whether a motor-gearbox pair works, and why a *range* of ratios works rather than one number.

## Intuition

A motor is a fast, weak machine; a robot joint usually needs slow and strong. A gearbox trades speed for torque: it makes the output turn $N$ times slower and push about $N$ times harder, like a bicycle's low gear. Power does not change except for friction losses. Two penalties come with that trade. A spinning motor generates a voltage that opposes the supply (back-EMF) and grows with speed, so a high ratio at a given joint speed runs the motor near its voltage limit. And the motor's rotor inertia seen from the joint grows as $N^2$, which makes the joint sluggish and impact-prone.

## Theory and derivation

### The DC motor

Electrical: $V=RI+k_e\omega$, where $k_e\omega$ is the back-EMF. Mechanical: $\tau=k_tI$. In SI units $k_t=k_e$ numerically ($\text{N m/A}=\text{V s/rad}$). Eliminating $I$:
$$\tau(\omega)=\frac{k_t}{R}\,(V-k_e\omega).$$
This is a line. At $\omega=0$ the **stall torque** is $\tau_s=k_tV/R$ (and the stall current is $V/R$, large). At $\tau=0$ the **no-load speed** is $\omega_0=V/k_e$. Mechanical power $P=\tau\omega$ is a parabola whose maximum is at $\omega_0/2$:
$$P_{\max}=\frac{\tau_s\,\omega_0}{4}.$$
Real systems also have a **peak current** $I_{\text{peak}}$ (driver or magnet limit), so $\tau\le k_tI_{\text{peak}}$, and a **continuous current** $I_{\text{cont}}$ set by heating: copper loss is $RI^2$, so temperature rise is roughly proportional to $I^2$.

### The gearbox

For a ratio $N>1$ and efficiency $\eta\in(0,1]$ the output (joint) side is
$$\tau_{\text{out}}=\eta N\tau_m,\qquad \omega_{\text{out}}=\omega_m/N,\qquad P_{\text{out}}=\eta P_m.$$
Efficiency drops with ratio and type: a spur stage is often 0.95-0.98, a harmonic drive roughly 0.6-0.85. Efficiency is also lower when backdriving.

### Inertia and the best ratio

A load with inertia $J_l$ about its joint axis, driven through $N$, makes the motor see
$$J_{\text{refl}}=J_m+\frac{J_l}{N^2}.$$
At the joint, the load accelerates as $\alpha=\dfrac{\eta N\tau_m}{J_l+N^2J_m}$ (neglecting gravity). Maximise over $N$: $d\alpha/dN=0$ gives $J_l+N^2J_m-2N^2J_m=0$, so
$$N^*=\sqrt{J_l/J_m},\qquad \alpha^*=\frac{\eta\tau_m}{2\sqrt{J_lJ_m}}.$$
At $N^*$ the reflected load inertia equals the rotor inertia ("inertia matching"). Higher ratios than needed only add motor-side inertia.

### The feasible ratio window

To hold a load torque $\tau_L$ at joint speed $\omega_L$ continuously:

1. **Thermal:** $I=\dfrac{\tau_L}{N\eta k_t}\le I_{\text{cont}}$ gives $N\ge N_{\text{th}}=\dfrac{\tau_L}{\eta k_tI_{\text{cont}}}$.
2. **Voltage:** $V_{\text{req}}=RI+k_e\omega_LN\le V$, i.e. $\dfrac{c}{N}+aN\le V$ with $a=k_e\omega_L$, $c=R\tau_L/(\eta k_t)$. Multiply by $N$: $aN^2-VN+c\le0$, which holds between the roots $N=\dfrac{V\pm\sqrt{V^2-4ac}}{2a}$ (real only if $V^2\ge4ac$).
3. **Peak** (for short bursts): $N\ge\tau_{\text{peak}}/(\eta k_tI_{\text{peak}})$.

The feasible window is the intersection. Too small a ratio fails the *current* limits; too large fails the *voltage* limit. If $V^2<4ac$ no ratio works and you need a stronger motor or higher voltage.

## Worked example

Motor: $k_t=k_e=0.05$, $R=1.0\ \Omega$, $V=24$ V, $I_{\text{cont}}=3$ A, $I_{\text{peak}}=10$ A. Gearbox efficiency $\eta=0.8$. Load: a 2 kg mass held 0.5 m from the joint, to be moved at 2 rad/s.

1. **Motor data.** Stall torque (voltage-limited) $0.05\cdot24/1=1.2$ N m, but the current limit gives $0.05\cdot10=0.5$ N m. No-load speed $24/0.05=480$ rad/s (4584 rpm).
2. **Load.** $\tau_L=2\cdot9.81\cdot0.5=9.81$ N m. Load inertia (point mass) $J_l=2\cdot0.5^2=0.5$ kg m$^2$.
3. **Try $N=50$.** $\tau_m=9.81/(50\cdot0.8)=0.24525$ N m, $I=0.24525/0.05=4.905$ A $>3$ A: **thermal limit violated** (copper loss $4.905^2=24.1$ W versus $9$ W at the limit). Voltage: $V_{\text{req}}=4.905+0.05\cdot(2\cdot50)=9.905$ V, fine. Peak ($4.9<10$ A) is fine.
4. **Try $N=100$.** $\tau_m=0.12263$ N m, $I=2.4525$ A $<3$ A. Motor speed $200$ rad/s, $V_{\text{req}}=2.4525+0.05\cdot200=12.45$ V $<24$ V. All limits hold; copper loss $6.0$ W.
5. **Window.** $N_{\text{th}}=9.81/(0.8\cdot0.05\cdot3)=81.75$. Voltage: $a=0.05\cdot2=0.1$, $c=1\cdot9.81/(0.8\cdot0.05)=245.25$, $V^2-4ac=576-98.1=477.9$, root $21.86$; so $N\in[10.7,\;229.3]$. Combined window: $81.75\le N\le229.3$.
6. **Inertia matching.** $N^*=\sqrt{0.5/10^{-5}}=223.6$ (inside the window), with rotor inertia $J_m=10^{-5}$ kg m$^2$. But $N=100$ is a good compromise: higher speed margin and lower reflected rotor inertia at the joint: $N^2J_m=0.1$ versus $0.5$ for the load, giving total joint inertia 0.6.

## Reference implementation

`check_joint` evaluates one motor-gearbox pair against a load; the self-test reproduces the worked-example rows for $N=50$ and $N=100$.

```python
M = dict(kt=0.05, ke=0.05, R=1.0, V=24.0, i_cont=3.0, i_peak=10.0)   # N*m/A, V*s/rad, ohm, V, A, A

def check_joint(m, N, eta, tau_load, w_load):
    """Can motor `m` behind ratio N and efficiency eta hold tau_load (N*m) at w_load (rad/s) continuously?"""
    tau_m = tau_load / (N * eta)            # motor-shaft torque
    i = tau_m / m["kt"]                     # current needed
    w_m = w_load * N                        # motor speed
    v = m["R"] * i + m["ke"] * w_m          # voltage needed (back-EMF + resistive drop)
    return {"tau_m": tau_m, "current": i, "w_m": w_m, "volts": v,
            "thermal_ok": i <= m["i_cont"], "voltage_ok": v <= m["V"], "peak_ok": i <= m["i_peak"]}

# --- self-test: hold 2 kg at 0.5 m (9.81 N*m) while moving at 2 rad/s ---
r50 = check_joint(M, 50, 0.8, 9.81, 2.0)
assert abs(r50["tau_m"] - 0.245250) < 1e-6 and abs(r50["current"] - 4.9050) < 1e-9
assert abs(r50["volts"] - 9.9050) < 1e-9 and r50["peak_ok"] and r50["voltage_ok"] and not r50["thermal_ok"]
r100 = check_joint(M, 100, 0.8, 9.81, 2.0)
assert abs(r100["current"] - 2.45250) < 1e-9 and abs(r100["volts"] - 12.4525) < 1e-9
assert r100["thermal_ok"] and r100["voltage_ok"]
assert not check_joint(M, 300, 0.8, 9.81, 2.0)["voltage_ok"]       # too much gearing: back-EMF exceeds supply
print("R09 reference OK: N=50 draws %.2f A (limit 3), N=100 draws %.2f A" % (r50["current"], r100["current"]))
```

```python
import math
MOTOR = dict(kt=0.05, ke=0.05, R=1.0, V=24.0, i_cont=3.0, i_peak=10.0)  # N*m/A, V*s/rad, ohm, V, A, A

def stall_torque(m):
    """Torque at zero speed: kt*V/R, but never above kt*i_peak (current limit)."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def no_load_speed(m):
    """Speed where back-EMF equals the supply: V/ke (rad/s)."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def motor_torque(m, omega):
    """Available torque at motor speed omega: kt*(V - ke*omega)/R clipped to [0, kt*i_peak]."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def output_torque(tau_m, N, eta):
    """Gearbox output torque: tau_m * N * eta."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def output_speed(omega_m, N):
    """Gearbox output speed: omega_m / N."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def reflected_inertia(Jm, Jl, N):
    """Inertia seen at the motor shaft: Jm + Jl/N**2."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def optimal_ratio(Jm, Jl):
    """Ratio that makes Jl/N**2 equal Jm: sqrt(Jl/Jm)."""
    raise NotImplementedError

def ratio_window(m, tau_load, w_load, eta):
    """(N_lo, N_hi) of gear ratios that hold tau_load at w_load continuously, or None.
    Torque: tau_load/(N*eta) <= kt*i_cont.  Voltage: R*I + ke*w_load*N <= V, I = tau_load/(N*eta*kt).
    The voltage condition is a quadratic in N: solve it; N_lo is the larger of its smaller root
    and the torque bound."""
    raise NotImplementedError
```

### Library track

Check the window and the inertia optimum with NumPy and SciPy (`pip install numpy scipy` in a virtual environment).

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import brentq, minimize_scalar
from actuator_lab import MOTOR as m, ratio_window, optimal_ratio

tau, w, eta = 9.81, 2.0, 0.8
lo, hi = ratio_window(m, tau, w, eta)

# 1. window edges from the roots of a*N^2 - V*N + c = 0 (NumPy) and from a root finder (SciPy)
a, c = m["ke"] * w, m["R"] * tau / (eta * m["kt"])
roots = np.sort(np.roots([a, -m["V"], c]))
assert np.isclose(roots[1], hi)
volts = lambda N: m["R"] * tau / (N * eta * m["kt"]) + m["ke"] * w * N - m["V"]
assert np.isclose(brentq(volts, roots[0] + 1, 400), hi)           # upper edge: voltage limit
assert np.isclose(tau / (eta * m["kt"] * m["i_cont"]), lo)        # lower edge: thermal limit

# 2. vectorised brute-force scan N = 1..400 agrees with the window
N = np.arange(1, 401)
i = tau / (N * eta * m["kt"])
ok = (i <= m["i_cont"]) & (m["R"] * i + m["ke"] * w * N <= m["V"])
assert N[ok].min() == np.ceil(lo) and N[ok].max() == np.floor(hi)

# 3. inertia matching: maximise the joint acceleration over N numerically
Jm, Jl, tau_m = 1e-5, 0.5, 0.5
alpha = lambda N: eta * N * tau_m / (Jl + N**2 * Jm)
best = minimize_scalar(lambda N: -alpha(N), bounds=(1, 1000), method="bounded", options={"xatol": 1e-9})
assert np.isclose(best.x, optimal_ratio(Jm, Jl), rtol=1e-4)
print("window %.2f..%.2f, N* = %.1f, alpha* = %.1f rad/s^2" % (lo, hi, best.x, alpha(best.x)))
```

The asserts confirm that the quadratic edge equals a numerical root, that a vectorised scan over $N=1\ldots400$ finds the same integer window, and that a numerical maximiser of the joint acceleration lands on $N^*=\sqrt{J_l/J_m}$. Extension: add `scipy.optimize.linprog`-style margins by scanning $\eta$ from 0.6 to 0.9 and plotting how the window shrinks.

## Verification

Check a sizing calculation in three independent ways. (1) Units: torque in N m, speed in rad/s (convert rpm), $k_t$ in N m/A. (2) Limits: compute current, voltage and power at the operating point and compare each with its limit, not only the torque. (3) Conservation: output power times 1/$\eta$ equals motor mechanical power. Test the extremes: $N=1$ (is the current absurd?) and a very large $N$ (does back-EMF exceed the supply?). A check that could reject the design: if the required voltage exceeds $V$ at the *highest* speed in the duty cycle, the design fails even if its average power is low.

## Failure modes

- **Torque-only sizing.** Choosing the ratio from stall torque and never checking back-EMF. At $N=300$ in the example, $V_{\text{req}}\ge0.05\cdot600=30$ V $>24$ V.
- **Peak instead of continuous.** The $N=50$ design passes the peak limit (4.9 A $<10$ A) but overheats (4.9 A $>3$ A).
- **Wrong direction of conversion.** Multiplying output speed by $1/N$ instead of by $N$ to get motor speed; using $\tau/N$ without $\eta$.
- **rpm in SI formulas.** A factor $2\pi/60$ error gives 9.5 times too much back-EMF.
- **Ignoring efficiency.** At $\eta=0.8$ the real motor torque is 25% higher than the ideal calculation.
- **Backdrivability forgotten.** Large-ratio gearboxes with $\eta<0.5$ in reverse cannot be pushed by hand and transmit impacts to the structure.
- **Constant $k_t$ assumption.** Saturation and temperature change $k_t$ by 10-20%; leave margin.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Choice | Torque | Speed | Reflected inertia | Backdrivable | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct drive ($N=1$) | low | high | tiny | yes | high-speed, force control |
| Quasi-direct ($N\approx6$-10) | medium | medium | small | mostly | legged robots |
| Planetary (3-100) | medium-high | medium | moderate | partly | general joints |
| Harmonic drive (50-160) | high | low | large ($N^2J_m$) | poorly | precise arms, zero backlash |

Select the lowest ratio that satisfies the thermal limit with margin; it keeps speed headroom and keeps the joint light and compliant.

High ratios give strength but a stiff, heavy-feeling joint: in a foot impact the reflected rotor inertia and poor backdrivability transmit large forces to the gearbox. Quasi-direct drive gives speed and compliance with less torque.
10. Voltage: $V^2\ge4ac$ with $a=0.1$ and $c=R\tau/(0.04)=25\tau$: $576\ge10\tau$, so $\tau\le57.6$ N m. Thermal: $N_{\text{th}}=\tau/(0.12)$. The upper root at $\tau=57.6$ is $N=24/(0.2)=120$, while $N_{\text{th}}=480$: thermal binds first. Solve $N_{\text{th}}(\tau)\le N_{\text{hi}}(\tau)$ numerically: $\tau\approx 20$ N m ($N_{\text{th}}=166.7$, $N_{\text{hi}}=(24+\sqrt{576-200})/0.2=216.9$) is feasible; $\tau\approx27$ N m ($N_{\text{th}}=225$, $N_{\text{hi}}=(24+\sqrt{576-270})/0.2=207.4$) is not. The limit is about 25.2 N m (bisection), where the thermal lower bound meets the back-EMF upper bound.
11. A good answer sums $I^2t$ over segments for RMS, takes the maximum current and voltage across segments, compares to $I_{\text{cont}}$, $I_{\text{peak}}$, $V$, and validates against $N=50$ (fails thermal) and $N=100$ (passes).
