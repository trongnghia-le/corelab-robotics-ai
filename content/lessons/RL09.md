## Why this matters

REINFORCE weights each action by the full return that followed it. That is unbiased but noisy, and it needs complete episodes. The previous lesson showed that subtracting a state-dependent baseline removes noise without bias. The natural baseline is the value $V(s)$, and since we do not know it, we learn it. The learned part is the **critic**; the policy it advises is the **actor**. Almost every deep RL algorithm for robots (A2C, PPO, SAC, TD3) has this two-network structure.

The critic buys lower variance and the ability to learn online from single transitions, and it costs bias: if the critic is wrong, the advice given to the actor is wrong. Knowing which of the two components failed is the main debugging skill in this lesson.

## Intuition

The actor asks "which action should I take?"; the critic answers "how good is the situation I am in?". After each step the robot compares what happened (reward plus the critic's value of the next state) with what the critic expected (its value of the current state). The difference, the TD error $\delta$, is a surprise. A positive surprise says that the action taken turned out better than expected, so make it more likely. A negative surprise says the reverse. The same $\delta$ also corrects the critic's expectation.

![Block diagram of one-step actor-critic: the environment supplies s, r, s' to the critic; the TD error updates both actor and critic](/lesson-figures/RL09.svg)

*A single TD error serves two consumers. If the critic's values are wrong, the surprise is wrong and the actor is trained on a bad signal.*

A useful extreme: if the critic says every state is worth $0$ and every step costs $-1$, then every action produces $\delta=-1$. The actor sees no difference between actions, and worse, its update rule pushes down whatever it just did.

## Theory and derivation

### Advantage

For a policy $\pi$ define $Q^\pi(s,a)$ and $V^\pi(s)=\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)Q^\pi(s,a)$. The **advantage** is

$$A^\pi(s,a)=Q^\pi(s,a)-V^\pi(s),\qquad \mathbb E_{a\sim\pi}[A^\pi(s,a)]=0.$$

The policy gradient with a state baseline is $\nabla J=\mathbb E\bigl[\nabla\log\pi(a\mid s)\,(Q^\pi(s,a)-V^\pi(s))\bigr]$, which is the advantage-weighted form of the previous lesson.

### The TD error as an advantage estimate

With $Q^\pi(s,a)=\mathbb E[r+\gamma V^\pi(s')\mid s,a]$,

$$\mathbb E[\delta\mid s,a]=\mathbb E[r+\gamma V^\pi(s')\mid s,a]-V^\pi(s)=A^\pi(s,a)\quad\text{when the critic equals }V^\pi.$$

So $\delta=r+\gamma V(s')-V(s)$ (with $V(s')=0$ at a terminal state) is an unbiased advantage estimate if $V=V^\pi$. If the critic is $V=V^\pi+e$, then

$$\mathbb E[\delta\mid s,a]=A^\pi(s,a)+\gamma\,\mathbb E[e(s')\mid s,a]-e(s).$$

The term $-e(s)$ is the same for all actions in a state and acts like an extra baseline error; harmless in expectation, noisy in practice. The term $\gamma\,\mathbb E[e(s')]$ depends on the action through the next state, and can change which action looks better.

### One-step actor-critic updates

With a softmax actor over logits $\theta_s$ and a tabular critic:

$$\delta=r+\gamma V(s')-V(s),\quad V(s)\leftarrow V(s)+\beta\delta,\quad \theta_{s,i}\leftarrow\theta_{s,i}+\alpha\,\delta\,\bigl(\mathbf 1[i=a]-\pi_i(s)\bigr).$$

The step sizes play different roles. The actor should move slowly enough that the critic can track the changing policy, so in practice $\beta$ is at least as large as $\alpha$, unless the problem is easy.

### Bias-variance of advantage estimates

The $n$-step advantage uses $n$ real rewards and then bootstraps. Writing $\delta_t=r_t+\gamma V(s_{t+1})-V(s_t)$, **generalised advantage estimation** is

$$\hat A_t^{\gamma,\lambda}=\sum_{k\ge0}(\gamma\lambda)^k\delta_{t+k}.$$

At $\lambda=0$ it is the one-step $\delta_t$ (low variance, biased if $V$ is wrong). At $\lambda=1$ it equals the Monte Carlo return minus $V(s_t)$ (unbiased, higher variance). Intermediate $\lambda$ trades one for the other.

## Worked example

A corridor with states $0,1,2$ and a goal after state 2. Actions: left (stay at 0 or move one left) and right. Every step costs $-1$; $\gamma=0.9$. The uniform random policy has exact values, found by solving $(I-\gamma P_\pi)V=r$:

$$V^\pi=(-6.141,\;-5.283,\;-3.377).$$

Check state 2: $\tfrac12(-1)+\tfrac12(-1+0.9\cdot(-5.283))=-0.5-2.878=-3.377$.

**Advantages at state 2.** $Q(2,\text{right})=-1$ (goal), $Q(2,\text{left})=-1+0.9(-5.283)=-5.755$. So $A(2,\text{right})=-1+3.377=+2.377$ and $A(2,\text{left})=-5.755+3.377=-2.377$. Their policy average is zero, as it must be.

**With the exact critic**, $\delta$ equals the advantage: $+2.377$ after right, $-2.377$ after left. An update with $\alpha=0.1$ after "right" moves the logits by $0.1\cdot2.377\cdot(-0.5,+0.5)=(-0.119,+0.119)$, raising P(right).

**With a zero critic** $V\equiv0$: $\delta(2,\text{right})=-1$ and $\delta(2,\text{left})=-1$. After "right" the update is $0.1\cdot(-1)\cdot(-0.5,+0.5)=(+0.05,-0.05)$, which lowers P(right), the wrong direction, although "right" is the better action.

**With a rough critic** $\hat V=(-6,-5,-3)$: $\delta(2,\text{right})=-1+3=+2.0$, $\delta(2,\text{left})=-1+0.9(-5)+3=-2.5$. The mean over the policy is $-0.25$, not 0, but the ranking is right, so learning proceeds.

**Separate errors.** Critic error $\max|\hat V-V^\pi|=\max(0.141,0.283,0.377)=0.377$. Actor gap $V^*(0)-V^\pi(0)=-2.71-(-6.141)=3.43$. The first says how reliable the advice is; the second says how far the behaviour is from optimal.

## Reference implementation

```python
"""One-step actor-critic on a 3-state corridor (stdlib only).
States 0,1,2 then the goal (terminal). Actions: 0 = left, 1 = right. Each step costs 1."""
import math, random

N, GAMMA = 3, 0.9

def step(s, a):
    s2 = max(0, s - 1) if a == 0 else s + 1
    return (None, -1.0, True) if s2 == N else (s2, -1.0, False)

def softmax(z):
    m = max(z); e = [math.exp(x - m) for x in z]; t = sum(e)
    return [x / t for x in e]

def solve(A, b):
    n = len(A); M = [row[:] + [b[i]] for i, row in enumerate(A)]
    for c in range(n):
        p = max(range(c, n), key=lambda r: abs(M[r][c])); M[c], M[p] = M[p], M[c]
        for r in range(n):
            if r != c:
                f = M[r][c] / M[c][c]; M[r] = [x - f * y for x, y in zip(M[r], M[c])]
    return [M[i][n] / M[i][i] for i in range(n)]

def v_pi(theta):
    """Exact value of the softmax policy: solve (I - gamma P) V = r."""
    A = [[1.0 if i == j else 0.0 for j in range(N)] for i in range(N)]
    for s in range(N):
        for a, p in enumerate(softmax(theta[s])):
            s2, _, done = step(s, a)
            if not done: A[s][s2] -= GAMMA * p
    return solve(A, [-1.0] * N)

def v_star():
    V = [0.0] * N
    for _ in range(500):
        V = [max(step(s, a)[1] + (0 if step(s, a)[2] else GAMMA * V[step(s, a)[0]]) for a in (0, 1)) for s in range(N)]
    return V

def td_error(V, s, r, s2, done):
    return r + (0.0 if done else GAMMA * V[s2]) - V[s]

def run(episodes, alpha, beta, seed, critic_first=0):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    theta = [[0.0, 0.0] for _ in range(N)]; V = [0.0] * N
    hist = []
    for ep in range(episodes):
        s = 0
        for _ in range(200):
            p = softmax(theta[s]); a = rng.choices((0, 1), weights=p)[0]
            s2, r, done = step(s, a)
            d = td_error(V, s, r, s2, done)
            V[s] += beta * d                                  # critic: TD(0)
            if ep >= critic_first:                            # actor: policy gradient with advantage ~ delta
                for i in range(2):
                    theta[s][i] += alpha * d * ((1.0 if i == a else 0.0) - p[i])
            if done: break
            s = s2
        if ep % 100 == 99:
            vp = v_pi(theta)
            hist.append((ep + 1, max(abs(x - y) for x, y in zip(V, vp)), v_star()[0] - vp[0]))
    return theta, V, hist

if __name__ == "__main__":
    vs = v_star()
    print("V* =", [round(x, 3) for x in vs])
    print("uniform-policy V =", [round(x, 3) for x in v_pi([[0.0, 0.0]] * N)])
    for cf in (0, 100):
        theta, V, hist = run(600, 0.1, 0.1, 0, critic_first=cf)
        print(f"actor starts at episode {cf}")
        for ep, ce, ae in hist:
            print(f"  episode {ep}: critic error {ce:.3f}  actor gap V*(0)-Vpi(0) {ae:.3f}")
    # With V = 0 the TD error is just r = -1 for every action: no information to separate actions.
    assert all(abs(td_error([0.0] * N, s, -1.0, min(s + 1, N - 1), False) + 1) < 1e-12 for s in range(N))
    # Exact-critic check: delta = Q(s,a) - V(s) for this deterministic MDP.
    # With the exact critic, E[delta | s, a] equals the advantage; for a deterministic MDP delta = Q - V.
    theta = [[0.3, -0.2] for _ in range(N)]; V = v_pi(theta)
    for s in range(N):
        p = softmax(theta[s])
        mean_delta = 0.0
        for a in (0, 1):
            s2, r, done = step(s, a)
            mean_delta += p[a] * td_error(V, s, r, s2, done)
        assert abs(mean_delta) < 1e-9          # E_a[advantage] = 0 under the policy
    print("self-test passed")
```

    return len(a) == len(b) and all(abs(x - y) < tol for x, y in zip(a, b))

### Extension challenge

Combine your functions into a training loop on the corridor of the reference implementation using GAE with $\lambda=0.8$, and compare its actor gap after 300 episodes with the one-step version over 5 seeds.

## Verification

- With the exact critic (from solving the linear system) the mean TD error under the policy must be 0 at every state; the reference asserts this.
- With a zero critic every TD error equals the reward; if not, the bootstrap term is wrong.
- GAE must reduce to $\delta_t$ at $\lambda=0$ and to return minus value at $\lambda=1$.
- Log critic error and the actor gap separately. If the critic error is large and falling while the actor gap is not, wait. If the critic error is small and the actor gap is large, the actor (step size, exploration) is the problem.
- Check terminal handling: no bootstrap after a true terminal, but bootstrap after a time-limit truncation.

## Failure modes

- **Updating the critic before computing $\delta$ for the actor.** The reused $\delta$ is $(1-\beta)$ times the true one; in Exercise 5 it is $0.1$ instead of $0.2$.
- **Actor learns before the critic.** With the frozen zero critic the actor gap went from 3.4 to about 7.3: worse than the uniform policy.
- **Bootstrapping through terminals.** Value leaks from outside the episode and the critic never converges.
- **Treating a time limit as a terminal.** The critic learns that states near the limit are worthless.
- **One shared learning rate for both.** An actor that moves faster than the critic can track amplifies critic errors.
- **Reporting only return.** A rising return can hide a critic that has diverged.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Advantage estimate | Bias | Variance | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monte Carlo $G_t-V$ | none (if $V$ only a baseline) | high | full episodes |
| One-step TD $\delta_t$ | critic bias | low | critic |
| GAE($\lambda$) | between | between | critic, episodes or rollouts |
| No critic, mean-return baseline | none | medium | batches |

Use one-step TD for fast online learning with a good critic, GAE for batch methods like PPO, and Monte Carlo when the critic cannot be trusted (early training, hard-to-model reward).

## Transfer and retention

For a manipulation policy, the critic estimates the expected remaining reward from the current joint and object state; a collapse in critic accuracy after a change of camera or object is a typical cause of sudden policy degradation. The same decomposition (log the critic loss, the advantage statistics and the policy entropy separately) applies to PPO and SAC. Retain: advantage $=Q-V$ has zero mean under the policy; $\delta$ equals the advantage only for the exact critic; actor and critic errors have different symptoms and need separate logs.
