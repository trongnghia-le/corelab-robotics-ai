## Why this matters

Planners and users describe goals in task space: "put the gripper at (0.7, 0.9) m". Motors act in joint space. Inverse kinematics (IK) is the translation, and it is harder than forward kinematics because the map from joints to tip is many-to-one: some targets have no joint solution, some have two, and some have a whole family. Production failures live here: an arm that flips its elbow in the middle of a motion, a solver that "converges" to a pose 5 cm from the target, a division by zero at a singular pose.

This lesson builds on forward kinematics (R04) and on the multivariable calculus of M06, in particular the Jacobian as the local linear model of a map. The optional V2 bridge "Planar 2R inverse kinematics" gives a compact hand derivation of the position-only 2R case; here we use that result as a tool and spend the time on what the bridge only touches: comparing branches, numerical solvers, constraints and honest failure.

## Intuition

Forward kinematics is a function $x=f(q)$. Inverse kinematics asks for the preimage $f^{-1}(x^*)$ of a target, a set that can be empty, finite or a continuum. For a 2R arm the picture is simple. The elbow can bend either way; both bends put the tip at the same point, like two mirror-image triangles standing on the same base-to-target line. When the arm is fully stretched or folded the two triangles merge into one. Beyond the stretched length, or inside the folded "hole", no triangle exists.

![Planar 2R arm reaching one target with two elbow solutions drawn as mirror triangles, and the annular reachable workspace between |l1-l2| and l1+l2](/lesson-figures/R05.svg)

*Left: two elbow solutions for one target are mirror images across the base-to-target line. Right: the reachable set is a ring; targets outside it have no solution.*

A numerical solver does the opposite of this global picture: it walks downhill from a start pose using only the local Jacobian. It finds one preimage, usually the one whose basin of attraction contains the start.

## Theory and derivation

### Analytic solution for the planar 2R arm

With relative joint angles, the tip is $x=l_1\cos q_1+l_2\cos(q_1+q_2)$, $y=l_1\sin q_1+l_2\sin(q_1+q_2)$. Squaring and adding gives the law of cosines for the elbow,
$$x^2+y^2=l_1^2+l_2^2+2l_1l_2\cos q_2\;\Rightarrow\;\cos q_2=\frac{x^2+y^2-l_1^2-l_2^2}{2l_1l_2}.$$
A real $q_2$ exists iff $|\cos q_2|\le1$, which is the **ring condition** $|l_1-l_2|\le r\le l_1+l_2$ with $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. Then $q_2=\pm\arccos(\cdot)$ and, for each sign,
$$q_1=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)-\operatorname{atan2}(l_2\sin q_2,\;l_1+l_2\cos q_2).$$
Solution count: 2 strictly inside the ring, 1 on either edge, 0 outside (and infinitely many if $l_1=l_2$ and the target is the base). The two solutions are reflections of each other across the line from the base to the target.

### Numerical IK: Newton, Jacobian and damping

For a target $x^*$ and current $q$, let the error be $e=x^*-f(q)$. Linearising, $f(q+\Delta q)\approx f(q)+J(q)\Delta q$, so Newton's step solves $J\Delta q=e$. For the 2R arm
$$J=\begin{pmatrix}-l_1\sin q_1-l_2\sin(q_1+q_2)&-l_2\sin(q_1+q_2)\\ l_1\cos q_1+l_2\cos(q_1+q_2)&l_2\cos(q_1+q_2)\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det J=l_1l_2\sin q_2.$$
At $q_2=0$ or $\pi$ the determinant is zero (a **singularity**): the arm cannot move its tip along the arm's own axis, and $J^{-1}$ does not exist. Near a singularity $J^{-1}$ is huge, so a small task error demands enormous joint steps.

**Damped least squares** (Levenberg-Marquardt style) replaces the inverse by
$$\Delta q=J^{T}\left(JJ^{T}+\lambda^2I\right)^{-1}e,$$
which stays finite at singularities, trading exactness of each step for stability. A **trust region** (limit $\lVert\Delta q\rVert\le\Delta_{\max}$) stops the linearisation being used far from where it is valid. Newton-type methods converge to the nearest solution in the sense of their basin of attraction; they need a good start, and they cannot tell "unreachable" from "slow".

### Redundancy

If the number of joints $n$ exceeds the task dimension $m$ (a 3R arm reaching a point: $n=3$, $m=2$), the preimage is an $(n-m)$-dimensional family. Moving along the **null space** $\{\dot q: J\dot q=0\}$ changes the pose but not the tip, which can be used to avoid limits or obstacles.

### Constraints and choosing a solution

Joint limits $[q_{\min},q_{\max}]$ filter the solutions; since $q$ and $q+2\pi k$ are the same pose, test whether *some* representative lies inside and report that one. When several solutions survive, pick by an explicit rule, for example the smallest joint motion from the current pose, to avoid flipping between branches in successive control cycles. Report "reachable but no admissible solution" separately from "unreachable".

### Verification by residual

Every candidate $q$ must be checked by $\lVert f(q)-x^*\rVert$. The residual is the only test that is independent of conventions, branches and angle wrapping.

## Worked example

Arm: $l_1=1.0$ m, $l_2=0.6$ m. Target $(0.7,\,0.9)$ m. (This is a different arm from the bridge's.)

1. **Ring test.** $r^2=0.49+0.81=1.30$, $r=1.140$. The ring is $0.4\le r\le1.6$: reachable, strictly inside, so two solutions.
2. **Elbow.** $\cos q_2=(1.30-1.00-0.36)/(2\cdot1.0\cdot0.6)=-0.06/1.2=-0.05$, so $q_2=\pm\arccos(-0.05)=\pm1.620817$ rad.
3. **Branch A ($q_2=+1.620817$).** $\sin q_2=0.998749$, so $l_2\sin q_2=0.599250$ and $l_1+l_2\cos q_2=1.0+0.6(-0.05)=0.970$. Then $\operatorname{atan2}(0.599250,0.970)=0.553393$ and $\operatorname{atan2}(0.9,0.7)=0.909753$. So $q_1=0.909753-0.553393=0.356360$ rad.
4. **Branch B ($q_2=-1.620817$).** The second term changes sign: $q_1=0.909753+0.553393=1.463146$ rad.
5. **Check A by FK.** $x=\cos(0.356360)+0.6\cos(1.977177)=0.93717-0.23717=0.7000$ and $y=\sin(0.356360)+0.6\sin(1.977177)=0.34887+0.55113=0.9000$. Residual below $10^{-6}$.
6. **One Newton step from $q=(0.3,\,0.9)$.** FK gives $(1.17275,\,0.85474)$, so $e=(-0.47275,\,0.04526)$. The Jacobian is $\begin{pmatrix}-0.85474&-0.55922\\1.17275&0.21741\end{pmatrix}$ with $\det J=l_1l_2\sin0.9=0.46999$. Solving $J\Delta q=e$: $\Delta q_1=(0.21741\cdot(-0.47275)-(-0.55922)(0.04526))/0.46999=-0.16484$, $\Delta q_2=(-1.17275\cdot(-0.47275)+(-0.85474)(0.04526))/0.46999=1.09732$. The new pose is $(0.13516,\,1.99732)$; its residual is 0.259 m, down from 0.475 m, but the step was far from a solution: $\Delta q_2=1.10$ rad is large and $q_2$ overshoots the true 1.6208 by 0.38 rad. Newton's linear model is only local; a trust region of 0.3 rad would have shortened the step and avoided the overshoot.

## Reference implementation

`pick_ik` returns every elbow solution that satisfies joint limits (with angle wrapping), sorted by distance from the current pose, and says why the list is empty if it is. Run the self-test; it verifies each answer by FK residual.

```python
import math

def fk(q1, q2, l1, l2):
    return (l1 * math.cos(q1) + l2 * math.cos(q1 + q2),
            l1 * math.sin(q1) + l2 * math.sin(q1 + q2))

def wrap_into(q, lo, hi):
    """Return the representative q + 2*pi*k inside [lo, hi], or None."""
    k = math.ceil((lo - q) / (2 * math.pi) - 1e-12)
    r = q + 2 * math.pi * k
    return r if r <= hi + 1e-12 else None

def pick_ik(x, y, l1, l2, limits, q_now):
    """Both elbow solutions, filtered by joint limits, nearest to q_now first.
    Returns (list_of_poses, reason). The list is empty with a reason if nothing works."""
    r = math.hypot(x, y)
    if not (abs(l1 - l2) <= r <= l1 + l2):
        return [], "unreachable: outside the ring"
    c = max(-1.0, min(1.0, (r * r - l1 * l1 - l2 * l2) / (2 * l1 * l2)))
    out = []
    for q2 in {math.acos(c), -math.acos(c)}:
        q1 = math.atan2(y, x) - math.atan2(l2 * math.sin(q2), l1 + l2 * math.cos(q2))
        w1, w2 = wrap_into(q1, *limits[0]), wrap_into(q2, *limits[1])
        if w1 is not None and w2 is not None:
            out.append((w1, w2))
    if not out:
        return [], "reachable, but both elbows violate joint limits"
    out.sort(key=lambda p: (p[0] - q_now[0]) ** 2 + (p[1] - q_now[1]) ** 2)
    return out, "ok"

# --- self-test (arm of the worked example) ---
l1, l2 = 1.0, 0.6
poses, why = pick_ik(0.7, 0.9, l1, l2, [(-math.pi, math.pi), (-math.pi, math.pi)], (0.3, 0.9))
assert why == "ok" and len(poses) == 2
for p in poses:
    fx, fy = fk(*p, l1, l2)
    assert math.hypot(fx - 0.7, fy - 0.9) < 1e-9          # residual check by forward kinematics
assert poses[0][1] > 0                                      # nearest to q_now = elbow with q2 > 0
assert pick_ik(0.7, 0.9, l1, l2, [(-3.14, 3.14), (-3.14, 0.0)], (0, 0))[0][0][1] < 0   # limits keep only q2<0
assert pick_ik(2.0, 0, l1, l2, [(-4, 4), (-4, 4)], (0, 0))[0] == []                    # r=2.0 > 1.6
print("R05 reference OK:", [tuple(round(v, 4) for v in p) for p in poses])
```

## Verification

Verify an IK implementation in four ways. (1) FK of every returned pose must equal the target within tolerance (the test samples 200 random reachable targets). (2) Counts: 2 inside the ring, 1 on each edge, 0 outside. (3) Hand-computed values, such as the bridge's $q_2=\pm1.213225$. (4) Break it on purpose: replace `atan2` by `atan`, or relative angles by absolute ones, and confirm that the residual test fails. A check that could reject a numerical solver: after convergence the residual must be below tolerance; a stall at 0.2 m for a target 0.2 m outside the ring is the *correct* behaviour and must be reported as unreachable, not as success.

## Failure modes

- **`atan(y/x)` instead of `atan2`.** For targets with $x<0$ the answer is a half-turn off; the pose is the mirror target (residual about 1.41 m in exercise 4). $x=0$ divides by zero.
- **Clamping before the ring test.** `acos(max(-1,min(1,c)))` turns an unreachable target into a "solution" with a large residual.
- **Degrees versus radians.** `math.acos` returns radians; mixing them gives plausible-looking angles that fail the residual check.
- **Singular start.** At $q_2=0$ the first row of $J$ is zero, so plain Newton divides by zero and DLS moves only along the direction it can.
- **No trust region.** One Newton step overshoots (worked example, step 6) and can jump to another basin.
- **Declaring "unreachable" because the iteration did not converge.** Decide reachability with the ring test; use the residual only to judge the solver.
- **Branch flipping.** Picking "the first" solution each cycle can switch elbow when the order changes; choose by distance to the current pose.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Method | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Analytic (closed form) | Exact, all branches, microseconds | Only for special geometries (2R, 3R, 6R with spherical wrist) |
| Newton / DLS | Any kinematic chain, handles redundancy | One solution per start, may stall, needs tuning |
| Optimisation with constraints | Limits, obstacles, preferences in one problem | Slower, local minima |
| Learned or lookup IK | Fast after training | Needs data, no guarantee of residual; verify with FK |

Use closed form when it exists, warm-started numerical IK for general arms, and always keep FK as the independent check.

7. The best the arm can do is stretch toward the target, so the tip ends 0.05 m short: residual about 0.05 m (the lab example with 0.2 m outside stalls at about 0.20). 8. $J=\begin{pmatrix}-0.5&-0.5&0\\1.0&0.5&0.5\end{pmatrix}$. The null vector from the $2\times3$ cross-product formula is $n=(-0.25,0.25,0.25)\propto(-1,1,1)$; $Jn=(0.125-0.125+0,\,-0.25+0.125+0.125)=(0,0)$. Physically, the base joint turns one way while the other two turn the opposite way and the wrist partly cancels it: the elbow swings while the tip stays put (self-motion).
9. Place the base at the origin and let the target direction be the $u$ axis. The triangle (base, elbow, tip) has fixed side lengths $l_1,l_2$ and base-to-tip distance $r$, so its shape is determined up to reflection. The two elbow positions are the two apexes of that triangle on either side of the line; reflecting across the line maps one to the other and flips the sign of the interior angle at the elbow, that is $q_2\to-q_2$.
10. A good answer: precheck the ring; solve both branches analytically; apply limits with wrapping; choose the admissible solution nearest to the previous pose (hysteresis); warm-start numerical IK from the previous pose; limit joint speed near $\det J\to0$; if the next point leaves the workspace, stop or slide along the boundary and report it; verify by FK residual each cycle.
11. A good project reports per-step residual (analytic about $10^{-16}$, DLS about $10^{-10}$), no elbow flip when nearest-branch selection is used, and notes that this line stays inside the ring (its minimum distance from the base is about 0.68 m and the maximum is 0.9 m), so to see the failure the line must be extended outward past $r=1.3$. It compares run times and states the rule for unreachability.
