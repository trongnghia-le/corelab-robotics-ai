## Why this matters

Every ROS tool that draws, plans for or simulates your robot reads one file: the robot description. `robot_state_publisher` turns it into the TF tree, RViz draws its meshes, MoveIt and the physics engines read its masses and joint limits. A wrong origin by 5 cm, a mesh in millimeters, an inertia that no real object can have, a revolute joint with no limits: each produces a failure that looks like an algorithm bug further downstream. Treat the description like code: write down what each line claims, check it against a hand calculation, and run validators before anything depends on it. (For a general spatial chain and `lookup` of transforms between links, see the V2 bridge on URDF chains; this lesson concentrates on reading a description, validating it, and its inertial data.)

## Intuition

A URDF is a **tree**: links (rigid bodies, each with its own frame) connected by joints (the allowed relative motion). Each joint says "my child frame sits at this offset and orientation relative to my parent frame when the joint value is zero, and then it moves about this axis". Forward kinematics is nothing but multiplying those offsets down the tree. So an arm description is a compact list of numbers you can check with a ruler: "the second joint is 0.4 m along the first link's x axis".

![Left: a URDF tree base, j1, l1, j2, l2, jt, tool with each joint's origin offset. Right: the planar arm at q1 = 30 degrees and q2 = 60 degrees, with the hand-computed tool position](/lesson-figures/S04.svg)

*The tree on the left and the geometry on the right say the same thing; the hand-computed tool position is the test of the file.*

Three kinds of data live in a link. **Visual** geometry is for display; **collision** geometry is a simpler shape used by planners and contact detection; **inertial** data (mass, center of mass, inertia tensor) is what dynamics simulation and controllers use. Display can be wrong without harming physics; inertial data cannot.

## Theory and derivation

### Frames and joints

- Each link has a frame. A joint's frame is its child link's frame.
- `<origin xyz="x y z" rpy="r p y"/>` of a joint gives the child frame pose in the parent frame at joint value 0. Units are meters and radians. Roll, pitch, yaw are rotations about fixed parent axes: $R=R_z(\text{yaw})R_y(\text{pitch})R_x(\text{roll})$.
- `<axis xyz>` is expressed in the joint (child) frame, default $(1,0,0)$. A `revolute` joint rotates about it within `<limit lower upper effort velocity>` (required for revolute and prismatic joints); `continuous` has no limits; `prismatic` slides; `fixed` does not move.
- Validity of the tree: link and joint names are unique; every joint names defined links; each link has at most one parent joint; there is one root (the link that is nobody's child); every link is reachable from the root.

### Planar forward kinematics from the file

For joints that rotate about $z$ with offsets $(o_x,o_y)$ and fixed yaw $\psi_i$ in the origin, the pose accumulates:
$$\theta_i=\theta_{i-1}+\psi_i+q_i,\qquad p_i=p_{i-1}+R_z(\theta_{i-1})\,o_i .$$
(The offset is rotated by the *parent's* accumulated orientation, then the joint's own rotation $\psi_i+q_i$ is applied.) For the arm of the figure, $p_{\text{tool}}=(l_1\cos q_1+l_2\cos(q_1+q_2),\ l_1\sin q_1+l_2\sin(q_1+q_2),\ z_0)$, the same two-link formula you derived in R04, plus the vertical offset.

### Inertia

The inertial block gives mass $m$ (kg), center of mass (origin of `<inertial>`), and the symmetric tensor $I$ (kg m$^2$) about the center of mass, in the inertial frame. Standard solids about their centers:
$$\text{box }(s_x,s_y,s_z):\ I_{xx}=\tfrac{m}{12}(s_y^2+s_z^2),\ \text{etc.};\qquad \text{cylinder (axis }z):\ I_{zz}=\tfrac12mr^2,\ I_{xx}=I_{yy}=\tfrac m{12}(3r^2+h^2).$$
The moment about a parallel axis at distance $d$ from the center of mass: $I=I_{\text{com}}+md^2$ (parallel-axis theorem). Physical validity: all principal moments positive and they satisfy the **triangle inequalities** $I_{xx}+I_{yy}\ge I_{zz}$ (and cyclic), because
$$I_{xx}+I_{yy}=\int (y^2+z^2)+(x^2+z^2)\,dm=I_{zz}+2\int z^2\,dm\ \ge I_{zz}.$$
An inertia that violates this cannot belong to any real mass distribution and many simulators will reject it or behave erratically.

### Tools

`check_urdf robot.urdf` parses and prints the tree; `urdf_to_graphviz robot.urdf` draws it; `xacro robot.xacro > robot.urdf` expands macros and parameters; `ros2 run tf2_ros tf2_echo base tool` prints a transform from the running TF tree (to compare with your hand value).

## Worked example


### Validating a three-link description, its forward kinematics and link inertia

**Problem.** The file in the tests below describes: root `base`; joint `j1` (revolute, axis $z$) at $(0,0,0.1)$; link `l1`; joint `j2` (revolute, axis $z$) at $(0.4,0,0)$; link `l2`; fixed joint `jt` at $(0.3,0,0)$; link `tool`. Link `l1` is a box $0.4\times0.05\times0.05$ m of mass $m=2$ kg centred at $(0.2,0,0)$. (a) Check that the description is a valid tree. (b) Find the position and heading of `tool` for $q_1=\pi/6$, $q_2=\pi/3$, and in the zero pose. (c) Find the inertia tensor of `l1` about its centre, check it is physical, and find its inertia about the $z$ axis of `j1`; then find what a 1 kg tool at the end adds.

**Solution.**
1. Count: links are base, l1, l2, tool, so 4 links; joints are j1, j2, jt, so 3 joints. A tree on $n$ links has $n-1$ joints: $3=4-1$ ✓.
2. Roots: the child links named by the joints are l1, l2, tool. The only link that is nobody's child is `base`, so it is the unique root.
3. Connectivity: following parent to child, base $\to$ l1 (via j1) $\to$ l2 (via j2) $\to$ tool (via jt) reaches all 4 links, so there is no cycle or detached piece. Both revolute joints have limits, so the description is valid.
4. Forward kinematics composes each joint's offset, rotated by the heading accumulated so far. After j1: the joint origin is $p=(0,0,0.1)$ and the heading is $\theta=q_1=\pi/6$.
5. After j2: the offset $(0.4,0)$ is rotated by $\pi/6$: $(0.4\cos30^\circ,0.4\sin30^\circ)=(0.4\cdot0.86603,0.4\cdot0.5)=(0.34641,0.2)$. Add to $p$: $p=(0.34641,0.2,0.1)$. Heading $\theta=\pi/6+\pi/3=\pi/2$.
6. After jt (fixed, adds no angle): the offset $(0.3,0)$ is rotated by $\pi/2$: $(0.3\cos90^\circ,0.3\sin90^\circ)=(0,0.3)$. So the tool is at $p=(0.34641,0.2+0.3,0.1)=(0.34641,0.5,0.1)$.
7. Zero pose ($q_1=q_2=0$, no rotation): $p=(0+0.4+0.3,0,0.1)=(0.7,0,0.1)$, the fully stretched arm.
8. Inertia of `l1` about its centre, box formulas $I_{xx}=\tfrac{m}{12}(b^2+c^2)$ etc. with sides $a=0.4$ (along $x$), $b=c=0.05$: $I_{xx}=\tfrac2{12}(0.0025+0.0025)=\tfrac2{12}\cdot0.005=8.33\times10^{-4}$ kg m$^2$; $I_{yy}=I_{zz}=\tfrac2{12}(0.4^2+0.05^2)=\tfrac2{12}\cdot0.1625=0.027083$ kg m$^2$.
9. Physical check (triangle inequality for principal moments): $I_{xx}+I_{yy}=0.000833+0.027083=0.02792\ge I_{zz}=0.02708$ ✓ (and the other two combinations hold by symmetry).
10. About the joint axis (the $z$ axis through j1), the centre is $d=0.2$ m away, so by the parallel-axis theorem $I=I_{zz}+md^2=0.027083+2\cdot0.2^2=0.027083+0.08=0.107083$ kg m$^2$.
11. A 1 kg tool at the end, $d=0.7$ m from joint 1 (as a point mass): $\Delta I=1\cdot0.7^2=0.49$ kg m$^2$, which is $0.49/0.107083=4.6$ times the link's own value.

**Check.** FK: the tool distance from the $j1$ axis is $\sqrt{0.34641^2+0.5^2}=0.6083$ m; the same value follows from the law of cosines with link lengths 0.4 and 0.3 and an elbow angle $\pi/3$ ($0.4^2+0.3^2+2\cdot0.4\cdot0.3\cos60^\circ=0.37$, $\sqrt{0.37}=0.6083$). The heading of the tool direction is $\arctan(0.5/0.34641)=55.3^\circ$, between the $30^\circ$ first link direction and $90^\circ$ final heading, as expected. Inertia: the inertia about the joint axis can be cross-checked with the thin-rod value $\tfrac13mL^2=\tfrac13\cdot2\cdot0.4^2=0.1067$ about one end, which is within 0.4 % of $0.1071$ (the rod formula ignores the 0.05 m width).

**Takeaway.** A link description is validated with simple counts and a walk from the root (joints $=$ links $-1$, one root, all links reachable), forward kinematics is the offset-rotate-add recursion, and the payload at the tip can dominate the joint inertia, so a controller tuned without it sees a very different plant.

## Reference implementation

```python
import math, xml.etree.ElementTree as ET

URDF = """<robot name="r"><link name="a"/><link name="b"/><link name="c"/>
<joint name="j1" type="revolute"><parent link="a"/><child link="b"/><origin xyz="0 0 0.1"/>
<axis xyz="0 0 1"/><limit lower="-3" upper="3" effort="5" velocity="2"/></joint>
<joint name="jt" type="fixed"><parent link="b"/><child link="c"/><origin xyz="0.4 0 0"/></joint></robot>"""

def tool_xy(urdf, q):
    root = ET.fromstring(urdf)
    x = y = th = 0.0
    for j in root.findall("joint"):                      # assumes the file lists the chain in order
        ox, oy, oz = (float(v) for v in j.find("origin").get("xyz", "0 0 0").split())
        x += math.cos(th) * ox - math.sin(th) * oy
        y += math.sin(th) * ox + math.cos(th) * oy
        if j.get("type") == "revolute":
            th += q[j.get("name")]
    return x, y

x, y = tool_xy(URDF, {"j1": math.pi / 2})               # link 0.4 m along x, rotated 90 degrees
assert abs(x) < 1e-12 and abs(y - 0.4) < 1e-12
assert tool_xy(URDF, {"j1": 0.0}) == (0.4, 0.0)
print("reference ok")
```

Real commands for the same check: `check_urdf arm.urdf`, then publish joint states and compare `ros2 run tf2_ros tf2_echo base tool` with the hand value. The reference trusts the order of the file; the lab replaces that assumption by finding the root and walking parent to child.

## Independent practice

### Goal
Write a small URDF parser and validator for planar arms and verify a two-link description against hand-derived transforms and inertial values.

### Setup
Python 3 standard library. Save starter and tests as `s04_lab.py`. Optional: with ROS installed, run `check_urdf` on the string in the tests (written to a file).

### Tasks
1. `parse_urdf` reads links (with optional mass) and joints (type, parent, child, origin xyz and yaw, axis); reject duplicate names, undefined links, revolute/prismatic joints without `<limit>`, and non-planar rpy.
2. `chain_order` finds the single root and returns joints from root to leaf; reject two roots, two parents, branching, unreachable joints.
3. `fk_planar` returns $(x,y,z,\text{yaw})$ for given joint values.
4. `box_inertia`, `cylinder_inertia`, `inertia_ok` (triangle inequalities and positivity), `pivot_inertia`.
5. Run the tests; then deliberately change one origin by 0.01 and observe which test fails.
6. In a comment write assumptions, baseline (zero pose), boundary case (all joints at a limit) and a rejecting observation.

  <link name="l1"><inertial><mass value="2.0"/></inertial></link>
  <link name="l2"><inertial><mass value="1.0"/></inertial></link>

### Library track

Check the same `arm2` description with the real ROS 2 tools and NumPy (tested on ROS 2 Humble with the system Python 3.10 that ROS was built for; Jazzy or later is the same). The script runs `check_urdf`, computes FK with $4\times4$ transforms, then starts a real `robot_state_publisher`, sends a `JointState` and compares the `base` to `tool` transform from TF with your hand value.

```python
# Needs a sourced ROS 2 install (tested on Humble, system Python 3.10) with numpy.
import math, subprocess, sys, tempfile, time
import numpy as np
import rclpy
from rclpy.node import Node
from sensor_msgs.msg import JointState
from tf2_ros import Buffer, TransformListener

URDF = """<robot name="arm2">
  <link name="base"/>
  <link name="l1"><inertial><origin xyz="0.2 0 0"/><mass value="2.0"/>
    <inertia ixx="8.333e-4" ixy="0" ixz="0" iyy="0.027083" iyz="0" izz="0.027083"/></inertial></link>
  <link name="l2"/>
  <link name="tool"/>
  <joint name="j1" type="revolute"><parent link="base"/><child link="l1"/>
    <origin xyz="0 0 0.1"/><axis xyz="0 0 1"/><limit lower="-3" upper="3" effort="10" velocity="2"/></joint>
  <joint name="j2" type="revolute"><parent link="l1"/><child link="l2"/>
    <origin xyz="0.4 0 0"/><axis xyz="0 0 1"/><limit lower="-3" upper="3" effort="10" velocity="2"/></joint>
  <joint name="jt" type="fixed"><parent link="l2"/><child link="tool"/><origin xyz="0.3 0 0"/></joint>
</robot>"""

# 1. Official validator.
with tempfile.NamedTemporaryFile("w", suffix=".urdf", delete=False) as f:
    f.write(URDF); path = f.name
out = subprocess.run(["check_urdf", path], capture_output=True, text=True).stdout
print(out.splitlines()[0]); assert "Successfully Parsed XML" in out and "root Link: base" in out

# 2. Homogeneous-transform FK with NumPy (what robot_state_publisher computes).
def T(xyz, yaw=0.0):
    c, s = math.cos(yaw), math.sin(yaw)
    M = np.eye(4); M[:2, :2] = [[c, -s], [s, c]]; M[:3, 3] = xyz
    return M
q1, q2 = math.pi / 6, math.pi / 3
M = T([0, 0, 0.1], q1) @ T([0.4, 0, 0], q2) @ T([0.3, 0, 0])
print("numpy tool position:", np.round(M[:3, 3], 5))
assert np.allclose(M[:3, 3], [0.34641016, 0.5, 0.1])

# 3. Real TF: robot_state_publisher + a JointState, then look the transform up.
rsp = subprocess.Popen(["ros2", "run", "robot_state_publisher", "robot_state_publisher", "--ros-args", "-p", f"robot_description:={URDF}"],
                       stdout=subprocess.DEVNULL, stderr=subprocess.DEVNULL)
rclpy.init(); node = Node("check")
pub = node.create_publisher(JointState, "joint_states", 10)
buf = Buffer(); TransformListener(buf, node)
tf = None; t_end = time.time() + 8
while time.time() < t_end and tf is None:
    m = JointState(); m.header.stamp = node.get_clock().now().to_msg()
    m.name = ["j1", "j2"]; m.position = [q1, q2]
    pub.publish(m); rclpy.spin_once(node, timeout_sec=0.1)
    if buf.can_transform("base", "tool", rclpy.time.Time()):
        tf = buf.lookup_transform("base", "tool", rclpy.time.Time()).transform.translation
print("tf2 tool position: (%.5f, %.5f, %.5f)" % (tf.x, tf.y, tf.z))
assert np.allclose([tf.x, tf.y, tf.z], M[:3, 3], atol=1e-6)
rsp.terminate(); node.destroy_node(); rclpy.shutdown()

# 4. Inertia check with NumPy: eigenvalues of the tensor must satisfy the triangle inequalities.
I = np.diag([8.333e-4, 0.027083, 0.027083]); w = np.linalg.eigvalsh(I)
assert w.min() > 0 and w[0] + w[1] >= w[2]
print("library track ok")
```

Expected: `robot name is: arm2`, `tool position (0.34641, 0.5, 0.1)` from both NumPy and TF, and `library track ok`. If it hangs waiting for the transform, `robot_state_publisher` could not parse the file: run `check_urdf` on it first. If you do not have ROS, run only the NumPy part.

## Verification

Hand-check three poses: zero pose $(0.7,0,0.1)$; $q=(\pi/2,0)$ gives $(0,0.7,0.1)$; the worked pose $(0.3464,0.5,0.1)$. Compare against the closed-form $l_1\cos q_1+l_2\cos(q_1+q_2)$ at random joint values. Verify the tree properties (one root, reachability) by code and by `check_urdf`. For inertia, check positivity and triangle inequalities, compare with a CAD tool or the solid formulas, and confirm the total mass equals the weighed robot. A boundary check: every joint at both limits still gives a pose inside the stated workspace $|r|\le l_1+l_2$ horizontally.

## Failure modes

- Treating `rpy` as intrinsic or in the wrong order: a $90^\circ$ roll then axis mapping $(0,0,1)\to(0,-1,0)$ appears as a wrong joint sign.
- Mesh unit errors: mm vs m gives a 1000-fold size error visible in RViz, and meshes also used for automatic inertia.
- Missing `<inertial>`: simulators treat the link as massless or fixed it to its parent; real controllers see zero inertia.
- Inertia that violates the triangle inequality, or a tensor given about the wrong point (joint instead of center of mass).
- Revolute joints without `<limit>`: parse error in many tools.
- Two roots (a forgotten fixed joint) giving two disconnected TF trees.
- A detached cycle that still satisfies "joints = links - 1": only the reachability walk finds it.
- Using collision meshes of the same complexity as visuals: slow planning with no accuracy benefit.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

URDF is simple, ROS-native and tree-only (no closed loops, limited physics options). SDF (Gazebo) adds worlds, sensors and closed loops; MJCF is richer for physics and contacts but not read by ROS tools directly. `xacro` keeps one parametrized source and generates URDF, at the price of a build step. A common workflow: author in `xacro`, expand to URDF for ROS, and convert (with checks) for the simulator, comparing masses and FK after conversion. Choose one source of truth and test the conversions rather than editing generated files.

## Transfer and retention

Pick a published robot model (for example a manipulator from a manufacturer repository). List its root, joint types, axes and limits; compute the end-effector position at the zero pose by hand from the origins; compare with `tf2_echo` or a viewer. After a week, redo the tree validity rules and the inertia triangle inequality from memory and explain why a roll of $\pi/2$ in an origin changes the meaning of a $z$ axis.

## Exercise answers

1. The origin is the pose of the child frame in the parent frame when the joint value is zero (meters, radians, parent frame). The joint frame is by definition the child frame, so the axis and the joint motion are expressed there. `rpy="0 0 1.5708"` rotates the child's axes by $90^\circ$ about the parent $z$: the child's $x$ points along the parent's $+y$.
2. $I_{zz}=\frac12(0.5)(0.03)^2=2.25\times10^{-4}$; $I_{xx}=I_{yy}=\frac{0.5}{12}(3\cdot0.0009+0.04)=1.779\times10^{-3}$ kg m$^2$.
3. Add `scale="0.001 0.001 0.001"` to the `<mesh>` element. The `<inertial>` block is independent of the mesh, so it does not change (it may need recomputation if it was derived from the mesh).
4. Link 1 points along $+y$: $p=(0,0.4)$, $\theta=\pi/2$; joint 2 subtracts $\pi/2$, so $\theta=0$ and the last link points along $+x$: tool $=(0.3,0.4,0.1)$.
5. $I_{xx}+I_{yy}=0.02<I_{zz}=0.05$: violates the triangle inequality, so no real body has it. Valid: $I_{zz}=\frac12(1)(0.0025)=1.25\times10^{-3}$, $I_{xx}=I_{yy}=\frac1{12}(3\cdot0.0025+0.01)=1.458\times10^{-3}$.
6. Rule: the axis is expressed in the child frame, so rotate it by the origin rotation: $R_x(\pi/2)(0,0,1)=(0,-1,0)$ (the rotation maps $(a,b,c)$ to $(a,-c,b)$). The positive joint direction then points along parent $-y$, which flips the apparent sign if you assumed $+z$.
7. Extra inertia $=1\cdot0.7^2=0.49$ on top of $0.107$ (link 1) and further link-2 inertia: a $4.6\times$ increase relative to link 1 alone. With $K_p/I$ scaling, the natural frequency drops by up to $\sqrt{4.6}\approx2.1$ (less once link 2 and the other inertia about the joint are counted) and the loop responds more slowly; a gain tuned empty would feel sluggish, while gains tuned for the loaded arm would be too aggressive when empty.
8. Parallel axes: with $r=d+r'$ in the plane perpendicular to the axis and $\int r'\,dm=0$ about the center of mass, $\int|r|^2dm=\int|r'|^2dm+2d\cdot\int r'dm+md^2=I_{\text{com}}+md^2$. Triangle: shown in the text by splitting $I_{xx}+I_{yy}=I_{zz}+2\int z^2dm$. For a flat plate $z=0$ so $\int z^2dm=0$ and equality holds.
9. Good answers: xacro gives parameters and reuse, one source for ROS; raw URDF is simple but repetitive; MJCF gives physics control not seen by ROS tools; conversion loses features (closed loops, actuator models, material). Rule: single source of truth with automatic conversions plus numeric checks (mass, FK, limits) on every generated file.
10. Expect the parser to reject the three variants; FK agrees with closed form to $10^{-15}$; zero pose $(0.7,0,0.1)$. A rejecting result is any disagreement above $10^{-9}$ or a root count $\ne1$.
