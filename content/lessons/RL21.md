## Why this matters

A learning robot arm explores by trying things. If "things" include hitting the table, driving a joint past its limit or entering a human's workspace, then maximum return is the wrong only objective. Folding safety into the reward as a penalty hides a design decision (how many units of reward is a collision worth?) and gives no guarantee that violations fall below any particular rate. Safe RL makes the cost a separate signal, states the allowed budget, and evaluates violations separately from the average return.

This lesson builds on RL13 (policy-gradient methods that can be extended with constraints) and on S07 (safety and constraint concepts). Everything is demonstrated on a problem small enough to solve exactly.

## Intuition

Imagine three ways to cross a workspace: a slow safe path (reward 1, never causes an incident), a medium path (reward 2, 10% chance of an incident per use) and a fast path (reward 3, 40% chance). If your incident budget is 15% per step, neither the medium nor the fast path alone is optimal: you *mix* them, using the fast path one time in six. The best constrained policy may therefore be stochastic even when the best unconstrained one is deterministic.

## Theory and derivation

### The constrained MDP

A constrained MDP (CMDP) adds a cost function $c(s,a)$ to the MDP of RL02. For a policy $\pi$ define the expected discounted (or per-episode) return and cost
$$J_R(\pi)=\mathbb E_\pi\Big[\sum_t\gamma^tr_t\Big],\qquad J_C(\pi)=\mathbb E_\pi\Big[\sum_t\gamma^tc_t\Big].$$
The safe RL problem is
$$\max_\pi J_R(\pi)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad J_C(\pi)\le d .$$
Because the objective and the constraint are both linear in the (discounted) state-action occupancy measure, the problem is a linear program, and a feasible optimum generally needs a *stochastic* policy: with one constraint, an optimal solution randomises between at most two actions in a state.

### Lagrangian relaxation and duality

Introduce a multiplier $\lambda\ge0$ and form
$$\mathcal L(\pi,\lambda)=J_R(\pi)-\lambda\,(J_C(\pi)-d).$$
For any fixed $\lambda$, maximising $\mathcal L$ over $\pi$ is an ordinary RL problem with reward $r-\lambda c$. The dual function $g(\lambda)=\max_\pi\mathcal L(\pi,\lambda)$ satisfies **weak duality**: for any feasible $\pi$ the term $\lambda(J_C-d)\le0$, so $\mathcal L(\pi,\lambda)\ge J_R(\pi)$ and therefore $g(\lambda)\ge J_R(\pi^*)$. Every $\lambda\ge0$ gives an upper bound on the constrained optimum; the best (smallest) bound is $\min_\lambda g(\lambda)$, which for this linear problem equals the optimum (strong duality). In practice algorithms alternate a policy update on $r-\lambda c$ with a multiplier update $\lambda\leftarrow\max\{0,\lambda+\eta\,(\hat J_C-d)\}$: the multiplier rises while the constraint is violated. This pair of updates can oscillate, which is why CPO and related methods add trust-region constraints.

### Expected cost is not a violation rate

The constraint is on an expectation. If incidents occur independently with probability $q$ per step, then over $H$ steps the number of incidents is Binomial$(H,q)$. Satisfying $\mathbb E[\text{incidents}]=Hq\le d_{\text{ep}}$ says nothing about $P(\text{incidents}>d_{\text{ep}})$. Stronger formulations exist: a chance constraint $P(\text{cost}>0)\le\delta$ and a risk measure such as CVaR (the mean of the worst $\alpha$-fraction of episodes). They are harder to optimise but match the requirement "rarely violate" better.

### Safe exploration

Even a correct CMDP solution is reached by exploring, and early exploration can be unsafe. Practical layers: a conservative initial policy (demonstrations or a verified controller); hard limits and a *shield* that overrides any action violating a known constraint (joint limits, speed caps); a cost critic that predicts cost before the action is executed; a safe backup policy to which the system reverts when the cost estimate is high; and training in simulation first with a separate real-world check.

## Worked example

Actions $(r,p)$ with $p$ the per-step incident probability: A $(1,0)$, B $(2,0.1)$, C $(3,0.4)$. Budget $d=0.15$ per step; episode length $H=10$.

**Step 1, single actions.** A and B are feasible ($0\le0.15$, $0.1\le0.15$); C is not ($0.4>0.15$). The best feasible single action is B with reward 2.

**Step 2, mixtures.** Mix B and C with weight $w$ on C: cost $0.1+0.3w\le0.15\Rightarrow w\le1/6$. Reward $=2+w=2+1/6=13/6=2.1667$. Mixing A with C needs $0.4w\le0.15$, $w=0.375$, reward $1+2(0.375)=1.75$, worse. So the optimum is $5/6$ B and $1/6$ C with value $13/6$.

**Step 3, multiplier.** The edge from B to C has slope $(3-2)/(0.4-0.1)=10/3$, so $\lambda^*=10/3$. Check the Lagrangian rewards $r-\lambda p$: A $=1$, B $=2-0.333=1.667$, C $=3-1.333=1.667$. B and C tie, and A is worse, consistent with mixing B and C.

**Step 4, dual bound.** $g(\lambda^*)=\max(1,1.667,1.667)+\tfrac{10}{3}(0.15)=1.667+0.5=2.1667$. Equal to the primal optimum. At $\lambda=1$: $\max(1,1.9,2.6)+0.15=2.75\ge2.1667$, as weak duality demands.

**Step 5, violations.** Expected incidents per episode $=10(0.15)=1.5$, exactly on budget. But $P(\text{incidents}>1.5)=P(\ge2)=1-0.85^{10}-10(0.15)(0.85^9)=1-0.1969-0.3474=0.4557$. About 46% of episodes exceed 1.5 incidents (two or more), although the expected-cost constraint is satisfied. A simulation with 20 000 episodes gives 0.451.

## Reference implementation

Exact solution of the constrained problem by trying singles and pairs, the Lagrangian dual bound, the exact violation probability and a Monte Carlo check.

```python
import math, random

# (name, reward, probability that taking it causes a unit of cost)
ACTIONS = [("A", 1.0, 0.0), ("B", 2.0, 0.1), ("C", 3.0, 0.4)]

def best_response(actions, lam):
    """Action maximising the Lagrangian reward r - lam * p (first one wins ties)."""
    return max(actions, key=lambda a: a[1] - lam * a[2])[0]

def dual_bound(actions, d, lam):
    """g(lam) = max_a (r - lam*p) + lam*d : an upper bound on the constrained optimum."""
    return max(r - lam * p for _, r, p in actions) + lam * d

def solve_cmdp(actions, d):
    """Best stochastic mixture with expected cost sum_a x_a p_a <= d (one constraint =>
    an optimal mixture uses at most two actions). Return (mixture dict, value)."""
    best = (None, -math.inf)
    for n1, r1, p1 in actions:
        if p1 <= d + 1e-12 and r1 > best[1]:
            best = ({n1: 1.0}, r1)
        for n2, r2, p2 in actions:
            if p1 <= d < p2:
                w = (d - p1) / (p2 - p1)
                v = (1 - w) * r1 + w * r2
                if v > best[1]:
                    best = ({n1: 1 - w, n2: w}, v)
    return best

def violation_prob(q, H, limit):
    """P(Binomial(H, q) > limit): chance that an H-step episode exceeds the cost limit."""
    return sum(math.comb(H, k) * q**k * (1 - q)**(H - k) for k in range(H + 1) if k > limit)

def simulate(mixture, actions, H=10, episodes=20000, seed=0):
    rng = random.Random(seed); info = {n: (r, p) for n, r, p in actions}
    names = list(mixture); w = [mixture[n] for n in names]; rets, costs = [], []
    for _ in range(episodes):
        R = C = 0.0
        for _ in range(H):
            r, p = info[rng.choices(names, w)[0]]
            R += r; C += rng.random() < p
        rets.append(R); costs.append(C)
    return sum(rets) / episodes, sum(costs) / episodes, sum(c > 1.5 for c in costs) / episodes

if __name__ == "__main__":
    mix, v = solve_cmdp(ACTIONS, 0.15)
    print("mixture", {k: round(x, 4) for k, x in mix.items()}, "value per step %.4f" % v)
    for lam in (0.0, 1.0, 10 / 3, 10.0):
        print("lambda=%.3f best response %s dual bound %.4f" % (lam, best_response(ACTIONS, lam), dual_bound(ACTIONS, 0.15, lam)))
    print("P(episode cost > 1.5) exact: %.4f" % violation_prob(0.15, 10, 1.5))
    ret, cost, viol = simulate(mix, ACTIONS)
    print("simulated: return %.2f, mean cost %.3f, violation rate %.3f" % (ret, cost, viol))
    assert abs(v - 13 / 6) < 1e-9 and all(dual_bound(ACTIONS, 0.15, l) >= v - 1e-9 for l in (0, 1, 2, 3.5, 10))
    assert abs(dual_bound(ACTIONS, 0.15, 10 / 3) - v) < 1e-9
    print("self-test passed")
```

Expected: mixture B 0.8333 / C 0.1667, value 2.1667; the dual bound equals 2.1667 at $\lambda=10/3$; exact violation probability 0.4557 against about 0.451 simulated.

### Tests

```python
# 1. hand-computed optimum for d = 0.15: B and C mixed, 5/6 and 1/6, value 13/6
mix, v = solve_cmdp(ACTIONS, 0.15)
assert set(mix) == {"B", "C"} and abs(mix["C"] - 1 / 6) < 1e-9 and abs(v - 13 / 6) < 1e-9
# 2. Lagrangian: at lambda = 10/3, B and C tie; dual bound equals the optimum there
assert best_response(ACTIONS, 0.0) == "C" and best_response(ACTIONS, 10.0) == "A"
assert abs(dual_bound(ACTIONS, 0.15, 10 / 3) - 13 / 6) < 1e-9
# 3. weak duality (property): every lambda >= 0 gives an upper bound
assert all(dual_bound(ACTIONS, 0.15, l / 4) >= v - 1e-9 for l in range(0, 60))
# 4. edge budgets: zero budget -> safe action only; generous budget -> best action
assert solve_cmdp(ACTIONS, 0.0) == ({"A": 1.0}, 1.0)
assert solve_cmdp(ACTIONS, 0.4)[1] == 3.0
# 5. monotone: relaxing the budget never lowers the optimum
vals = [solve_cmdp(ACTIONS, d / 20)[1] for d in range(0, 9)]
assert all(b >= a - 1e-12 for a, b in zip(vals, vals[1:]))
# 6. expected cost within budget does not bound the violation rate of an episode
assert abs(violation_prob(0.15, 10, 1.5) - 0.455700) < 1e-6
assert violation_prob(0.0, 10, 0) == 0.0 and abs(violation_prob(0.5, 1, 0) - 0.5) < 1e-12
print("all tests passed")
```

## Verification

- Report return, mean cost and the fraction of episodes above the budget, each with seed spread.
- Certify the optimum: primal value equals the dual bound at the multiplier.
- Evaluate the final policy and also the policies from early training; safety during learning is a separate claim from safety at the end.
- Test the edge case $d=0$: the result should be the pure safe policy.

## Failure modes

- **Penalty instead of constraint**: the penalty weight silently sets the incident rate; changing the reward scale changes safety.
- **Reporting only the mean**: 1.5 incidents per episode on average hid a 45.6% rate of two or more.
- **Multiplier oscillation**: with a large $\eta$, $\lambda$ overshoots, the policy swings between unsafe and over-conservative.
- **Sign error in the multiplier update** (exercise 5): the multiplier falls when the cost is too high, so the constraint is never enforced.
- **Safe at the end only**: a policy that violates the constraint heavily during exploration can still end safe.
- **Cost estimates from too little data**: an optimistic cost critic is a silent route to violations.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Approach | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reward penalty | Simplest; any RL algorithm | No guarantee; tuning decides safety |
| Lagrangian (primal-dual) | Meets expected-cost limits; simple | Oscillation; violations during training |
| CPO / trust-region constrained | Better near-constraint behaviour | More complex; approximation errors |

Use a shield for hard physical limits and a CMDP for soft, statistical limits; never use only the reward penalty where a failure is costly.

## Exercise answers

1. A penalty fixes an exchange rate between collisions and reward, so the achieved collision rate depends on the unknown trade-off and on reward scale; a constraint states the required collision rate. Difficulty: tuning the weight takes many training runs and changes with reward shaping; too small gives unsafe policies, too large gives timid ones.
2. Cost $0.5(0.1)+0.5(0.4)=0.25>0.15$: infeasible. Reward $0.5(2)+0.5(3)=2.5$, higher than the optimum 2.1667 because it breaks the budget.
3. $P(\ge2)=0.4557$, about 46%. Also report the violation rate, the cost spread over seeds, cost during training and a tail measure such as CVaR.
4. Step 1: $1+2(0.30-0.15)=1.30$, the constraint is violated so the multiplier rises and the next policy is more cautious. Step 2: $1.30+2(0.05-0.15)=1.10$, the constraint is satisfied with slack so the multiplier decays and the policy can become more aggressive. Large $\eta$ makes this oscillate.
5. The sign is reversed: `d - J_c` is positive when the policy is safe, so lam increases when safe and decreases when unsafe. It must be `lam + lr*(J_c - d)`.
6. Lagrangian rewards: A $=1$, B $=2-0.2=1.8$, C $=3-0.8=2.2$; best response is C. $g(2)=2.2+2(0.15)=2.5$, above the optimum 2.1667, consistent with weak duality.
7. Good answers include: a hard limit or shield (force/velocity caps, workspace keep-out around the glass), a conservative start (a verified controller or demonstrations, low speeds), a learned cost model or sensor trigger to stop; each fails when the model of the hazard is wrong, a sensor is blind, or the glass moves outside the modelled region, so human supervision and an emergency stop remain necessary.
8. For feasible $\pi$, $J_C(\pi)\le d$ and $\lambda\ge0$, so $\lambda(J_C-d)\le0$ and $\mathcal L(\pi,\lambda)\ge J_R(\pi)$. Taking the max over $\pi$ yields $g(\lambda)\ge J_R(\pi)$, in particular for the optimum. For the second part: the problem is an LP over weights $x_a\ge0$ with the constraints $\sum x_a=1$ and $\sum x_ap_a\le d$; a basic optimal solution has as many nonzero variables as there are binding constraints, at most two.
9. $1-(1-q)^{10}\le0.05\Rightarrow q\le1-0.95^{0.1}=0.005116$. Mix A and B: $w_B=0.005116/0.1=0.05116$, reward $1+0.05116=1.0512$ per step. Expected-cost budget 0.5 per episode means $q=0.05$, $w_B=0.5$, reward 1.5. The chance constraint is far stricter (1.05 versus 1.5 reward).
10. A good project reports for each policy return, mean cost, violation rate and CVaR with at least 3 seeds. The conservative policy should have lower violation rate and CVaR at lower return; the claim "expected cost is enough" is rejected if the policy meeting the expected limit has a materially higher tail cost than the conservative policy at similar mean cost, or if the budget-0 case fails to give the pure safe policy.
