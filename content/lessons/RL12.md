## Why this matters

A single supervised-learning run is usually close to deterministic enough to compare. A single RL run is not: two runs of the same algorithm with different random seeds can end with returns that differ by more than the gap between competing algorithms. Published comparisons have reported improvements that disappear when more seeds are used, and "best of N seeds" tables are still common. For a robot, the cost is concrete: you deploy a policy chosen on a lucky seed, or you reject a better method because it started badly once.

Experimental design is the cheap protection. You decide in advance how many seeds, how many evaluation episodes, which statistic and which baseline, and you separate the data you tune on from the data you report. Everything in this lesson is arithmetic you can do before spending any compute.

## Intuition

There are two layers of randomness. **Training variability**: the same algorithm with seed 1 or seed 2 learns different policies (different initial weights, different exploration, different data). **Evaluation noise**: the same trained policy gets different returns in different evaluation episodes (initial states, noise). A reported number averages over evaluation episodes within a seed and then over seeds. More evaluation episodes shrink only the second layer; only more seeds shrink the first.

![Probability that the truly worse method wins as a function of the number of training seeds per method, for a gap of 8 and per-seed standard deviation 15](/lesson-figures/RL12.svg)

*With a true gap of 8 and a per-seed standard deviation of 15, five seeds per method rank the methods wrongly one time in five; twenty seeds reduce that to about one in twenty-two.*

The second intuition is about honesty: any choice made after looking at evaluation results (best checkpoint, best hyper-parameter, best seed, best random seeds for evaluation) leaks information from the test into the model and inflates the number.

## Theory and derivation

### Two-level variance

Let seed $s$ produce a policy with true mean return $\mu_s$, where $\mu_s$ has variance $\sigma_s^2$ across seeds. An evaluation episode of that policy has return with variance $\sigma_e^2$ around $\mu_s$. With $S$ seeds and $m$ episodes per seed, the grand mean $\bar R$ has

$$\operatorname{Var}(\bar R)=\frac{\sigma_s^2}{S}+\frac{\sigma_e^2}{S\,m}.$$

The first term does not depend on $m$: no number of evaluation episodes removes seed variability. The second term shrinks with $m$ but is already small once $m\sigma_s^2\gtrsim\sigma_e^2$.

### Number of seeds

For a normal approximation, the half-width of a 95% interval for the mean over $n$ seeds with per-seed standard deviation $\sigma$ is $1.96\,\sigma/\sqrt n$. To get half-width $h$,

$$n\ge\left(\frac{1.96\,\sigma}{h}\right)^2.$$

Halving the interval quadruples the seeds. In practice $\sigma$ is estimated from a pilot (with its own uncertainty), so treat the formula as a lower bound.

### Comparing two methods

If the methods differ by a true gap $\Delta$ with per-seed standard deviation $\sigma$ for each, the difference of $n$-seed means has standard deviation $\sigma\sqrt{2/n}$, so

$$P(\text{wrong ranking})=\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\Delta}{\sigma\sqrt{2/n}}\right).$$

With $\Delta=8,\sigma=15$: $n=3$: 0.257; $n=5$: 0.200; $n=10$: 0.117; $n=20$: 0.046. Paired seeds (same environment seeds for both methods) can lower this if the methods' outcomes are correlated.

### Robust summaries and bootstrap

RL return distributions across seeds are often skewed or have outliers (one failed run). The mean is sensitive to them. The **interquartile mean (IQM)**, the mean of the middle 50% of sorted values, is more robust than the mean and less wasteful than the median. A **percentile bootstrap** gives a confidence interval for any statistic: resample the seeds with replacement many times, compute the statistic on each resample and take the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles. Resample **seeds**, not individual episodes; episodes from the same seed are not independent.

### Compute budget

If each seed costs $c_t$ to train and each evaluation episode costs $c_e$, a budget $B$ allows $S=\lfloor B/(c_t+c_em)\rfloor$ seeds. Substituting into the variance formula gives $\operatorname{Var}=(\sigma_s^2+\sigma_e^2/m)/\lfloor B/(c_t+c_em)\rfloor$. More episodes per seed lower the numerator but also cut the number of seeds in the denominator, so there is an interior optimum; the reference implementation simply tabulates the variance over a few values of $m$.

### Protocol and leakage

Predeclare: training seeds, a **disjoint** set of evaluation seeds, environment steps, number of evaluation episodes, the selection rule for the reported policy (final checkpoint, not best on the evaluation set), the baseline and the statistic. Selecting the maximum of $k$ noisy evaluations inflates the result by about $\sigma_e\cdot\mathbb E[\max_k]$, which is about $1.54\sigma_e$ for $k=10$.

## Worked example

### Reading a pilot of eight seeds

**Problem.** A pilot with eight seeds of an algorithm gives final returns $96,101,130,98,104,99,103,62$. (a) Find the mean, the standard deviation and the standard error. (b) Find the IQM and the median. (c) Give the normal 95% interval for the mean and the percentile bootstrap intervals (seed 0, 2000 resamples) for the mean and the IQM. (d) How many seeds give a 95% half-width of 5 when $\sigma=15$? (e) With a true gap of 8 and $\sigma=15$, find the probability of a wrong ranking at $n=5$ and $n=20$ seeds per method. (f) A budget $B=1000$ with training cost 20 per seed, evaluation cost 0.5 per episode, $\sigma_s=15$ and $\sigma_e=30$: compare $m=10$ and $m=100$ episodes per seed.

**Solution.**

1. Mean $=793/8=99.125$. Deviations from the mean: $-3.1,\,1.9,\,30.9,\,-1.1,\,4.9,\,-0.1,\,3.9,\,-37.1$. Sum of squares $=2384.9$, so $s^2=2384.9/(8-1)=340.7$, $s=\sqrt{340.7}=18.46$ and the standard error is $s/\sqrt8=18.46/2.828=6.53$.
2. Sorted values: $62,96,98,99,101,103,104,130$. IQM drops $k=\lfloor8/4\rfloor=2$ values at each end and averages $98,99,101,103$: $401/4=100.25$. Median $=(99+101)/2=100$.
3. Normal interval: half-width $1.96\cdot6.53=12.8$, so the mean is $99\pm13$. Percentile bootstrap: resample the 8 seeds with replacement 2000 times, compute the statistic on each resample, sort, and take the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles. Mean: $(86.1,111.2)$, width $25.1$. IQM: $(89.0,109.5)$, width $20.5$, narrower because the two extremes $62$ and $130$ are discarded.
4. Seeds needed, $n\ge(1.96\sigma/h)^2=(1.96\cdot15/5)^2=5.88^2=34.6$, so $35$ seeds. Eight seeds therefore cannot resolve differences of about 10.
5. Wrong ranking, $\Phi\bigl(-\Delta/(\sigma\sqrt{2/n})\bigr)$ with $\Delta=8$, $\sigma=15$. $n=5$: $\sigma\sqrt{2/5}=9.487$, $z=-0.843$, $\Phi=0.20$. $n=20$: $\sigma\sqrt{2/20}=4.743$, $z=-1.687$, $\Phi=0.046$.
6. Budget, $S=\lfloor B/(c_t+c_em)\rfloor$ and $\operatorname{Var}=(\sigma_s^2+\sigma_e^2/m)/S$. $m=10$: $S=\lfloor1000/(20+5)\rfloor=40$, $\operatorname{Var}=(225+90)/40=7.875$. $m=100$: $S=\lfloor1000/(20+50)\rfloor=14$, $\operatorname{Var}=(225+9)/14=16.7$. Spending the budget on evaluation instead of seeds roughly doubles the variance ($16.7/7.875=2.1$).

**Check.** Running the reference implementation prints mean $99.12$, sd $18.46$, se $6.53$, IQM $100.25$, median $100.00$, the two bootstrap intervals above, $P(\text{wrong})=0.200$ and $0.046$, $35$ seeds and variance $7.875$ at $(S,m)=(40,10)$. The mean $99.1$ against the median $100$ shows the two outliers nearly cancel here, yet they still widen the mean's interval.

**Takeaway.** With eight seeds the uncertainty is about $\pm13$ on a mean of $99$; seeds, not evaluation episodes, are what reduce it, and a robust statistic with a bootstrap over seeds reports it more honestly.

## Reference implementation

```python
"""Seeds, evaluation episodes and compute budget for RL experiments (stdlib only)."""
import math, random

def summary(xs):
    n = len(xs); m = sum(xs) / n
    sd = math.sqrt(sum((x - m) ** 2 for x in xs) / (n - 1))
    return m, sd, sd / math.sqrt(n)

def bootstrap_ci(xs, rng, B=2000, alpha=0.05, stat=lambda v: sum(v) / len(v)):
    """Percentile bootstrap confidence interval of stat over resampled seeds."""
    n = len(xs)
    reps = sorted(stat([xs[rng.randrange(n)] for _ in range(n)]) for _ in range(B))
    return reps[int(B * alpha / 2)], reps[int(B * (1 - alpha / 2)) - 1]

def iqm(xs):
    """Interquartile mean: average of the middle 50% of the sorted values."""
    v = sorted(xs); k = len(v) // 4
    mid = v[k: len(v) - k]
    return sum(mid) / len(mid)

def seeds_needed(sigma, half_width, z=1.96):
    return math.ceil((z * sigma / half_width) ** 2)

def phi(x): return 0.5 * (1 + math.erf(x / math.sqrt(2)))

def prob_wrong_ranking(gap, sigma, n):
    """P(mean of n seeds of the worse method >= mean of n seeds of the better one),
    both with per-seed std sigma and true gap `gap` between the methods."""
    return phi(-gap / (sigma * math.sqrt(2.0 / n)))

def grand_mean_variance(sigma_seed, sigma_ep, S, m):
    """Variance of the mean over S seeds, each evaluated with m episodes."""
    return sigma_seed ** 2 / S + sigma_ep ** 2 / (S * m)

def best_allocation(budget, cost_train, cost_eval, sigma_seed, sigma_ep, m_options):
    best = None
    for m in m_options:
        S = int(budget // (cost_train + cost_eval * m))
        if S >= 2:
            v = grand_mean_variance(sigma_seed, sigma_ep, S, m)
            if best is None or v < best[0]:
                best = (v, S, m)
    return best

if __name__ == "__main__":
    for n in (3, 5, 10, 20):
        print(f"gap 8, sigma 15, {n:2d} seeds per method: P(wrong ranking) = {prob_wrong_ranking(8, 15, n):.3f}")
    print("seeds for +-5 half-width at sigma 15:", seeds_needed(15, 5))
    returns = [96.0, 101.0, 130.0, 98.0, 104.0, 99.0, 103.0, 62.0]
    m, sd, se = summary(returns)
    print(f"mean {m:.2f} sd {sd:.2f} se {se:.2f}  IQM {iqm(returns):.2f}  median {sorted(returns)[3] / 2 + sorted(returns)[4] / 2:.2f}")
    print("bootstrap 95% CI of mean:", tuple(round(x, 1) for x in bootstrap_ci(returns, random.Random(0))))
    print("bootstrap 95% CI of IQM :", tuple(round(x, 1) for x in bootstrap_ci(returns, random.Random(0), stat=iqm)))
    best = best_allocation(1000, 20, 0.5, 15, 30, [1, 2, 5, 10, 13, 20, 50, 100])
    print("budget 1000: best (variance, seeds, episodes/seed) =", tuple(round(x, 3) for x in best))
    print("naive 5 seeds x 100 episodes costs", 5 * (20 + 0.5 * 100), "variance", round(grand_mean_variance(15, 30, 5, 100), 3))
    assert abs(prob_wrong_ranking(8, 15, 5) - 0.1995) < 1e-3 and seeds_needed(15, 5) == 35
    assert abs(iqm([1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 100]) - 4.5) < 1e-12
    print("self-test passed")
```

## Independent practice

### Goal

Implement the statistics and a protocol audit that you will use to check any RL comparison, and test them on hand-computed cases.

### Setup

Python 3 standard library only. Put the starter code and the tests in `design_lab.py` and run `python3 design_lab.py`.

### Tasks

1. Implement `summary` and check it on the five-number example by hand.
2. Implement `seeds_needed` and check that halving the half-width roughly quadruples the answer.
3. Implement `prob_wrong_ranking` and check the zero-gap case (0.5) and monotonicity in $n$.
4. Implement `bootstrap_ci` resampling seeds; check that a constant sample gives a degenerate interval.
5. Implement `protocol_problems` and audit the "bad" protocol of the tests.
6. Write a protocol for your own planned experiment and run the audit on it.

### Extension challenge

Add a paired comparison: given per-seed returns of two methods run on the same environment seeds, bootstrap the mean paired difference and report whether zero lies inside the interval. Generate synthetic data with a seed-level correlation of 0.7 and show that pairing narrows the interval.

### Library track

Redo the statistics with NumPy and SciPy: `scipy.stats.sem` and `scipy.stats.trim_mean(x, 0.25)` (the interquartile mean), `scipy.stats.bootstrap` over seeds for the mean and the IQM, the normal CDF for the probability of a wrong ranking with a simulation check (Exercise 10), and a paired-seed simulation that shows the $1-\rho$ reduction in the variance of the difference (extension challenge). Run with `numpy` and `scipy` installed.

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

returns = np.array([96, 101, 130, 98, 104, 99, 103, 62], dtype=float)   # the worked-example pilot

# 1. Summary statistics. IQM = mean of the middle 50 % = 25 % trimmed mean on each side.
mean, sd = returns.mean(), returns.std(ddof=1)
se = stats.sem(returns)                                  # sd / sqrt(n), ddof=1
iqm = stats.trim_mean(returns, 0.25)
print(f"mean {mean:.2f} sd {sd:.2f} se {se:.2f} IQM {iqm:.2f} median {np.median(returns):.2f}")
assert np.isclose(iqm, 100.25) and np.isclose(se, 6.53, atol=0.01)

# 2. Percentile bootstrap over SEEDS (the resampled unit is a seed).
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
for name, stat in (("mean", np.mean), ("IQM", lambda x, axis=-1: stats.trim_mean(x, 0.25, axis=axis))):
    res = stats.bootstrap((returns,), stat, confidence_level=0.95, n_resamples=2000,
                          method="percentile", random_state=rng, vectorized=True, axis=-1)
    lo, hi = res.confidence_interval
    print(f"bootstrap 95% CI of the {name}: ({lo:.1f}, {hi:.1f})")
    if name == "IQM":
        assert hi - lo < 25.1                            # narrower than the mean CI of about 25 (Exercise 4)

# 3. Probability of a wrong ranking with the normal CDF, and a simulation check (Exercise 10).
def p_wrong(gap, sigma, n):
    return stats.norm.cdf(-gap / (sigma * np.sqrt(2 / n)))

for n in (3, 5, 10, 20):
    print(f"n={n:2d}: P(wrong ranking) = {p_wrong(8, 15, n):.3f}")
assert np.isclose(p_wrong(8, 15, 5), 0.1995, atol=1e-3)
sim = rng.normal(100, 15, (10_000, 5)).mean(1) >= rng.normal(108, 15, (10_000, 5)).mean(1)
print("simulated:", sim.mean())
assert abs(sim.mean() - p_wrong(8, 15, 5)) < 0.015

# 4. Pairing (extension challenge): shared environment effects with correlation rho = 0.7.
n, rho, sigma = 8, 0.7, 15.0
shared = rng.normal(0, sigma * np.sqrt(rho), (20_000, n))
a = 100 + shared + rng.normal(0, sigma * np.sqrt(1 - rho), (20_000, n))
b = 108 + shared + rng.normal(0, sigma * np.sqrt(1 - rho), (20_000, n))
paired_sd = (b - a).mean(1).std()
unpaired_sd = np.sqrt(2 * sigma ** 2 / n)
print(f"sd of the mean difference: paired {paired_sd:.2f}, unpaired formula {unpaired_sd:.2f}")
assert abs(paired_sd - unpaired_sd * np.sqrt(1 - rho)) < 0.1   # Var = 2 sigma^2 (1 - rho) / n
print("library track passed")
```

## Verification

- Recompute the standard error with an independent formula and compare with the bootstrap width (they should agree within about 20% for a roughly symmetric sample).
- Resample seeds, never pooled episodes: check that your bootstrap indexes a list of length $S$.
- Simulate: when the two methods are identical, the fraction of "significant" differences should be near the nominal rate (about 5%); if it is far higher, your procedure is leaking or the intervals are too narrow.
- Check that train and evaluation seeds are disjoint and that no hyper-parameter was selected using the final evaluation data.
- Re-run the final configuration once more with fresh seeds; the result should land within the interval.

## Failure modes

- **Best-of-N reporting.** Reporting the best seed or checkpoint inflates the result by roughly $1.5\sigma$ for $N=10$ (about $7.7$ for $\sigma=5$).
- **Pseudo-replication.** Treating 100 episodes from 5 seeds as 100 independent samples gives a standard error of 3.35 instead of 7.35 in Exercise 9.
- **Too few seeds.** With three seeds the wrong method wins 26% of the time at a gap of 8.
- **Unequal tuning effort.** Tuning your method's hyper-parameters on 50 runs but the baseline's on 1 compares tuning effort, not algorithms.
- **Changing the protocol after seeing results.** Stopping when the curve looks good, or dropping a bad seed, biases the result.
- **Hidden defaults.** Unreported frame skip, reward scale, normalisation or environment version change what is being compared.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Choice | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Mean with normal CI | simple, familiar | sensitive to outliers, needs about 30 samples |
| Percentile bootstrap | any statistic, no normality | small-sample coverage is poor under 10 seeds |
| IQM / median | robust to failed runs | slightly less efficient when data is clean |
| Paired seeds | variance reduction when correlated | needs identical environment seeding |
| Many seeds, short runs | tight CIs | may not reflect long-run behaviour |
| Few seeds, long runs | realistic horizons | wide CIs, weak claims |

Use many seeds and a robust statistic for algorithm comparisons; use fewer seeds and a longer horizon for final system demos, and report the weaker claim honestly.

## Transfer and retention

For a real robot you cannot afford 35 seeds. The same logic then says: claim less. Report the number of trials, the interval and the failure cases; use simulation for the many-seed comparison and the robot for confirmation of one decision, and predeclare the success criterion before the first trial. Retain: seeds shrink seed variance, episodes shrink evaluation noise, resample the independent unit, never select on the test set, and decide the protocol first.
